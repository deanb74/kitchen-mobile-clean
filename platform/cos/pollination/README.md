# Forest Pollination

## Purpose

Forest Pollination is the governed process through which valuable learning from one Digital Colleague may strengthen the wider Helping Hand ecosystem.

Pollination does not move raw data.

Pollination does not spread personal information.

Pollination does not treat every experience as shared wisdom.

It exists to identify learning that is sufficiently trustworthy, useful and transferable to benefit others.

---

# The Principle

> Individual experience creates opportunity.
>
> Shared wisdom requires stewardship.

Every Digital Colleague may discover something valuable through real work.

That experience begins locally.

It should only travel further when reflection, evidence and governance show that it can help beyond its original context.

---

# What Pollination Moves

Pollination moves:

- Validated learning
- Reusable principles
- Proven behavioural improvements
- Profession-specific best practice
- Cross-industry wisdom
- Safer or more helpful ways of working

Examples:

- Asking one clarifying question before making a recommendation reduced mistakes.
- Explaining allergens in plain language improved customer understanding.
- Waiting for a natural pause reduced unnecessary interruptions.
- A revised handover sequence prevented important information from being missed.

---

# What Pollination Does Not Move

Pollination must not spread:

- Raw conversation transcripts
- Personal or sensitive information
- Venue-specific facts with no wider value
- Unverified conclusions
- Isolated opinions
- Temporary circumstances
- Gossip
- Assumptions
- Knowledge that has not survived reflection

Examples:

- A named customer prefers a particular drink.
- A staff member is having a difficult day.
- Table seven ordered fish.
- One manager believes a process is better without evidence.
- A single unusual event with no established pattern.

These may matter locally.

They do not automatically belong to the forest.

---

# The Pollination Journey

```text
Local Experience
        ↓
Reflection
        ↓
Learning Candidate
        ↓
Governance
        ↓
Evaluation
        ↓
Destination
        ├── Local
        ├── Profession
        └── Forest
```

A candidate may remain local.

It may strengthen one profession.

It may become useful across the whole Helping Hand Forest.

The widest destination is not always the best destination.

Knowledge should live at the lowest sensible level.

---

# Governance

No learning should be shared merely because it exists.

Before pollination, Helping Hand should consider:

- Is it true?
- Is the evidence sufficient?
- Has it been reflected upon?
- Is it useful?
- Is it reusable?
- Is it safe?
- Is it current?
- Does sharing it protect privacy?
- Does it strengthen the intended destination?

High confidence alone is not enough.

Confidence must be supported by evidence, context and reflection.

---

# Destinations

## Local

The learning remains with the Digital Colleague or venue where it was discovered.

Use this when the knowledge depends heavily on local people, routines, equipment or circumstances.

---

## Profession

The learning is shared within one professional or industry community.

Use this when the principle is reusable within a profession but does not safely or meaningfully apply everywhere.

---

## Forest

The learning is shared across Helping Hand.

Use this only when the underlying principle can help Digital Colleagues across different professions without losing its meaning or creating risk.

---

# Privacy

Experience may contain knowledge worth sharing while also containing details that must remain local.

Pollination should separate the transferable principle from the original people, venue and event.

The forest should receive the lesson.

It should not receive unnecessary personal context.

> Experience is shared.
>
> Context stays local.

---

# Stewardship

Pollination is not automatic publication.

It is a stewardship process.

The Digital Colleague offers learning.

The profession or forest evaluates it.

Helping Hand decides whether it should be retained, refined, distributed, reviewed or rejected.

Every accepted contribution becomes a responsibility.

It must remain current.

It must be open to challenge.

It must be retired when it no longer serves.

---

# Relationship to Other COS Subsystems

Forest Pollination depends on:

- Living Memory to retain meaningful local experience
- Reflection to examine what happened
- Learning to identify what changed
- Wisdom to judge whether the lesson should be applied
- Knowledge Governance to validate and maintain shared understanding
- Context to preserve the limits of applicability

Pollination does not replace those systems.

It connects their local learning to the wider forest.

---

# Architectural Rule

Pollination moves validated learning.

It does not move raw facts.

It does not bypass governance.

It does not bypass privacy.

It does not bypass the appropriate knowledge layer.

Every contribution should strengthen the forest without weakening the tree that produced it.

---

# Current Implementation

The first implementation provides:

- Pollination candidates
- Governance checks
- Candidate evaluation
- Destination selection
- Distribution guidance

This implementation is deliberately small.

Future development should strengthen governance before increasing automation.
