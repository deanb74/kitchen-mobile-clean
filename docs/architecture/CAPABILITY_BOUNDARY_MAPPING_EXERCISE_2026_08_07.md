# Capability Boundary Mapping Exercise

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Status:** Candidate — Analysis
**Type:** Discovery only
**Purpose:** Map the existing specialist boundaries in Helping Hand so future Digital Colleagues can identify what they do not own, who they collaborate with, and where understanding should route next.

---

## The Question

> Can every future Digital Colleague identify its own boundary and the person or capability to consult?

If the answer is yes, that may be the actual foundation of Helping Hand.

Not a bigger brain.

A better team.

---

## Boundary Mapping Principle

Capability is useful only when its boundary is clear.

A Digital Colleague should be able to say:

- what it does not own;
- what it is responsible for;
- what it should defer to;
- and who or what it should consult when understanding is incomplete.

This is not a new architecture.
It is a mapping exercise over the architecture that already exists.

## What This Exercise Is Looking For

The map is not asking, "What can this capability do?"

It is asking:

1. What is this capability not responsible for?
2. When should it stop and consult?
3. Which specialist should receive the question next?
4. What boundary protects meaning from being stretched too far?
5. What provenance must survive the handover?

---

## Expected Discovery

The likely result is that Helping Hand already assumes a distributed model of specialist boundaries:

- no DC should assume singular intelligence;
- no capability should be treated as universally sufficient;
- and consultation should follow understanding, not replace it.

If that is true, then the real architectural foundation is not a central intelligence source.

It is boundary literacy.

---

## Why This Matters

The same question appears in several forms across the architecture:

- the interdependence audit asks whether understanding emerges from specialised perspectives;
- the capability routing audit asks whether understanding selects the right specialist capability;
- the capability appropriateness principle asks whether capability is valuable only when applied appropriately;
- this exercise asks whether every future DC can locate its own boundary and consult correctly.

Together these suggest a single underlying architectural truth:

> Helping Hand is not a monolith of intelligence.
> It is a governed team of distinct perspectives.

---

## Open Questions

1. Should this map become a standard onboarding artifact for future Digital Colleagues?
2. Should each specialist boundary be documented as a maintained governed table rather than an open analysis?
3. Is there a common consult pattern across all specialists, or does each profession need its own boundary map?
4. Would naming the consult relationships more explicitly help preserve authority and provenance during handover?
5. Does every Digital Colleague require a boundary map as part of formation?

---

## Conclusion

This exercise does not propose a new capability-routing implementation.

It maps the existing specialist boundaries so Helping Hand can test whether future Digital Colleagues know where they end, where they defer, and who they consult.

If that map can be made explicit, then the architecture may already have the foundation it needs.
