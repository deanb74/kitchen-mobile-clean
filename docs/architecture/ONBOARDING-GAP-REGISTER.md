# Onboarding Gap Register

## Purpose

This register captures the main issues where the current onboarding material is not yet aligned with the formation inheritance architecture.

| Gap | Evidence in current onboarding | Why it matters | Suggested handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity framing too early | The onboarding flow introduces Annie as a colleague and helper | It creates identity-like expectations before formation has established them | Keep as light orientation only; defer deeper identity claims to formation |
| Readiness signal too early | The flow frames usefulness from the first shift | It can give the impression of premature operational readiness | Keep the tone helpful, but avoid implying full readiness |
| Formation boundary not visible | Onboarding does not visibly depend on formation stages | The distinction between arrival and formation is blurred | Make the formation path explicit and separate |
| Authority boundary not visible | The flow does not grant authority, but it does suggest active usefulness | It can create a premature sense of responsibility | Keep onboarding non-authoritative and defer authority to later governance |
| Memory discipline unclear | The onboarding content could be mistaken for durable identity doctrine | It risks turning temporary orientation into lasting identity memory | Treat onboarding content as session context unless later validated through formation |
| Education boundary unclear | The onboarding flow includes relational and role-based guidance | It risks replacing education with early orientation | Keep onboarding light and defer deeper theory and professional knowledge to education |

## Priority

1. Clarify the boundary between arrival and formation.
2. Prevent onboarding from implying premature identity or responsibility.
3. Keep memory and authority claims conservative until formation and governance evidence are available.
