# Knowledge Inheritance Model

**Document ID:** HH-ARCH-KIM-001

**Status:** Foundation

**Version:** 1.0

---

# Purpose

Helping Hand separates knowledge according to where it remains universally true.

Knowledge should always exist at the highest level where it remains valid.

Everything below inherits from that level.

This minimises duplication, improves governance and allows understanding to scale naturally.

---

# The Inheritance Principle

Knowledge belongs at the highest level where it remains true.

---

# Four Levels of Understanding

## Helping Hand HQ

Principles are universal.

Helping Hand HQ owns knowledge that applies regardless of profession, organisation or technology.

Examples include:

- Constitution
- Ethics
- Trust
- Communication
- Companion Intelligence
- Learning
- Reflection
- Data Protection Principles
- Information Security Principles
- Privacy
- Governance

---

## Industry HQ

Rules are shared.

Industry HQ owns knowledge shared by every organisation within an industry.

Examples include:

Hospitality

- Food Safety
- SFBB
- HACCP
- COSHH
- Licensing
- Hospitality VAT
- Industry Standards
- Professional Research
- Industry Best Practice

Healthcare

- NHS Guidance
- CQC
- Clinical Standards

Construction

- Building Regulations
- CDM
- HSE

---

## Venue HQ

Practice is specific.

Venue HQ owns knowledge unique to an organisation.

Examples include:

- Equipment
- Suppliers
- Menus
- Staff
- Local Procedures
- Cleaning Rotas
- Preferred Contractors
- Local Terminology

---

## Operations

Experience is local.

Operations create evidence.

Examples include:

- Today's temperatures
- Today's deliveries
- Today's conversations
- Today's maintenance
- Today's incidents

---

# Core Principle

Principles are universal.

Rules are shared.

Practice is specific.

Experience is local.

---

# Engineering Principle

Do something once.

Govern it once.

Pass it forward.

---

# Summary

Helping Hand separates generic knowledge from specific knowledge.

Universal understanding is inherited.

Local understanding is created.

Experience continually improves the entire system.
