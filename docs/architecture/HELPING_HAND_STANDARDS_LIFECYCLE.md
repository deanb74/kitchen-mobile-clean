# Purpose

This document defines the lifecycle of every Helping Hand standard.

It ensures that architecture, governance, policies and institutional knowledge evolve through a consistent, transparent and accountable process.

A Helping Hand standard is never considered complete simply because it has been written.

It becomes authoritative only after appropriate review and ratification.

---

# Guiding Principles

Helping Hand standards shall:

- evolve through evidence rather than opinion;
- preserve institutional consistency;
- remain understandable to future generations;
- encourage continuous improvement;
- protect the long-term integrity of Helping Hand.

---

# Standards Lifecycle

Every Helping Hand standard progresses through the following lifecycle.

```text
Proposal
    |
Draft
    |
Technical Review
    |
Architecture Review
    |
Ratification
    |
Released Standard
    |
Operational Use
    |
Reflection
    |
Revision Proposal
    |
Review
```

This lifecycle applies equally to architecture, governance, professional standards and organisational policies.

---

# Lifecycle States

## Proposal

A new concept or improvement has been identified.

The proposal documents:

- purpose;
- expected benefit;
- affected standards;
- initial scope.

A Proposal has no authority.

---

## Draft

The standard is under active development.

Drafts may change significantly.

Drafts shall not be considered authoritative.

---

## Technical Review

The draft is reviewed for:

- accuracy;
- consistency;
- completeness;
- dependency management;
- implementation feasibility.

Review findings are recorded.

---

## Architecture Review

The standard is evaluated against Helping Hand's organisational architecture.

The review considers:

- constitutional alignment;
- architectural coherence;
- governance consistency;
- institutional impact;
- long-term maintainability.

No new standards should normally be created during an architecture review.

---

## Ratification

Following successful review, the standard is submitted for formal ratification.

Ratification confirms that the document becomes part of Helping Hand's permanent institutional knowledge.

---

## Released Standard

A released standard becomes authoritative.

Future work shall comply with released standards unless formally superseded.

---

## Operational Use

Released standards guide:

- Digital Colleague development;
- professional faculties;
- governance;
- engineering;
- organisational decision-making.

---

## Reflection

Operational experience is continuously reviewed.

Reflection identifies:

- improvements;
- ambiguities;
- unintended consequences;
- opportunities for clarification.

Reflection informs future revisions.

---

## Revision Proposal

Changes to released standards begin with a Revision Proposal.

Revision does not invalidate the current released standard until a replacement has completed the full lifecycle.

---

# Versioning Principles

Helping Hand maintains complete version history.

Every revision shall include:

- rationale;
- affected sections;
- compatibility considerations;
- approval history.

Previous versions remain archived for historical reference.

---

# Governance

This lifecycle is governed by HH-GOV-001.

Ratification procedures are defined by HH-GOV-003.

Architecture oversight is provided by HH-GOV-004.

---

# Summary

Helping Hand standards are living institutional assets.

Their purpose is not merely to document today's understanding, but to preserve, improve and responsibly pass that understanding to future generations.

A standard is therefore not finished when it is written.

It is trusted because it has been reviewed, ratified and continually improved.
