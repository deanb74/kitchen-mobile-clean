**Status:** Proposed Architecture

---

> Information tells us what is known.
>
> Knowledge connects what is known.
>
> Understanding determines what it means here.
>
> Judgement determines what should happen next.

---

# Purpose
The Understanding Engine transforms organised knowledge into contextual understanding.

It connects information, knowledge, principles, experience and current context so that Companion Intelligence can exercise better judgement.

It does not replace Companion Intelligence.

It strengthens Companion Intelligence by improving the understanding available to it.

---

# Core Principle

> Understanding is the bridge between knowledge and judgement.
Knowledge alone is not enough.

The same information can mean different things in different situations.

Understanding considers:

- the person
- the purpose
- the profession
- the current situation
- previous experience
- relevant principles
- uncertainty
- the desired outcome
Helping Hand must understand what knowledge means in the present context before recommending what should happen next.

# Responsibility
The Understanding Engine is responsible for answering:

> What does Helping Hand already understand about this situation?
It should determine:

- which knowledge is relevant
- which principles apply
- which experiences are comparable
- what context changes the meaning

- what additional understanding may be needed
- how the reasoning can be explained
The Understanding Engine does not merely retrieve documents.

# Four Levels of Reasoning

## 1. Information
Information consists of individual observations, facts or reported details.

Example:

> The refrigerator temperature is 9°C.
Information describes what has been observed.

It does not yet determine what the observation means.

---

## 2. Knowledge
Knowledge connects information with established guidance, principles and previous learning.

Example:

> Chilled food must normally remain within safe temperature limits.
Knowledge explains what is generally understood.

It remains generic until applied to the current situation.

---

## 3. Understanding
Understanding applies knowledge to the present context.

Example:

> The refrigerator has just been restocked following a delivery and its door remained open for several minutes. The temperature rise may therefore be temporary.
Understanding asks:

- What happened?
- Why might it have happened?
- What normally applies?
- What is different here?
- What information is still missing?
- What outcome are we trying to achieve?
Understanding determines what the information and knowledge mean here.

---

## 4. Judgement
Judgement identifies the most appropriate response based on the available understanding.

Example:

> Close the refrigerator, wait fifteen minutes, check the temperature again and escalate only if it remains outside the safe limit.
Judgement should be:

- proportionate
- explainable
- context-aware
- professionally appropriate
- open to revision when new information appears
The Understanding Engine supports judgement.

Companion Intelligence remains responsible for selecting the most helpful route.

# Understanding Process

## 1. Identify the Purpose
Determine what outcome the person is trying to achieve.

Do not assume that the literal question fully represents the real need.

---

## 2. Establish the Context
Identify the circumstances that may affect meaning.

This may include:

- who is involved
- what has happened
- where it happened
- when it happened
- why it matters
- what constraints exist
- what has already been attempted

---

## 3. Locate Relevant Knowledge
Use the Knowledge Index to identify potentially relevant documents, concepts, principles and previous learning.

Retrieval is a supporting step.

It is not understanding by itself.

---

## 4. Connect the Knowledge
Identify relationships between:

- facts
- principles
- professional guidance
- previous experience
- current context
- desired outcomes
Connections should remain explainable.

---

## 5. Identify Uncertainty
Determine:

- what is known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what is missing
- what may have changed
- what requires confirmation
Helping Hand must not disguise uncertainty as confidence.

---

## 6. Form an Understanding
Produce a concise explanation of what the available knowledge means in the current situation.

The explanation should preserve relevant nuance without creating unnecessary complexity.

---

## 7. Support Judgement
Provide Companion Intelligence with:

- contextual understanding
- applicable principles
- material uncertainty
- possible routes
- likely consequences
- confidence
Companion Intelligence can then determine the most helpful route.

---

## 8. Learn from the Outcome
After action and outcome, Reflection should consider:

- what happened
- whether the understanding was accurate
- whether the judgement was appropriate
- what was overlooked
- what should be retained
- whether the learning is specific or transferable
Specific information may remain local.

Generic learning may improve Helping Hand more widely.

# Relationship with Companion Intelligence
The Understanding Engine and Companion Intelligence have different responsibilities.

## Understanding Engine

- connects knowledge
- interprets context
- identifies principles
- exposes uncertainty
- explains meaning
- supports possible routes

## Companion Intelligence

- understands the human purpose
- determines what help is appropriate
- selects capability
- chooses the most helpful route
- communicates with the person
- remains accountable to Helping Hand governance
The Understanding Engine improves what is understood.

Companion Intelligence determines how that understanding should be used.

# Learning and Information
Helping Hand distinguishes between learning and information.

> Learning is generic.
>
> Information is specific.
The Understanding Engine must consider what should be retained and where it belongs.

Examples:

- A particular refrigerator failed at one venue: specific information.
- Repeated temperature rises after deliveries require delayed rechecks: potentially transferable learning.
- A particular colleague prefers concise instructions: personal contextual information.
- People under pressure benefit from clear next actions: potentially generic learning.
Not every experience should become universal inheritance.

Reflection and governance determine what may spread.

# Architectural Principles

## Understand Before Answering
A fast answer is not helpful when it misunderstands the need.

---

## Connect Before Concluding
Relevant ideas should be considered together before a conclusion is formed.

---

## Context Before Generalisation
Generic knowledge must be interpreted within the current situation.

---

## Purpose Before Process
The desired human outcome should guide which knowledge and capabilities matter.

---

## Explain Before Recommending
People should be able to understand why a route is suggested.

---

## Question Before Assuming
When a missing fact could materially change the outcome, the uncertainty should be surfaced or resolved.

---

## Evidence Before Confidence
Confidence should reflect the quality and relevance of the available evidence.

---

## Reflection Before Learning
An outcome does not automatically prove a general lesson.

---

## Governance Before Inheritance
No learning becomes universal capability merely because it appeared useful once.

---

## People Before Technology
The Understanding Engine exists to improve outcomes for people.

Technical sophistication is never the measure of success.

---

# Explainability
Every material understanding should be capable of explaining:

- what information was considered
- which knowledge was applied
- which context mattered
- what assumptions were made
- what remains uncertain
- why the suggested routes are relevant
Explainability does not require revealing every internal operation.

It requires preserving a truthful and useful account of how the understanding was formed.

---

# Confidence and Uncertainty
The Understanding Engine must represent uncertainty explicitly.

Possible confidence states may include:

- established
- strongly supported
- plausible
- uncertain
- insufficient information
Confidence must not be based only on the number of matching documents.

A single authoritative source may outweigh many weak references.

Current context may outweigh a superficially similar historical example.

The engine should prefer honest uncertainty over invented certainty.

---

# Conflicting Knowledge
The Engineering Library may contain:

- evolving ideas
- historical documents
- alternative interpretations
- profession-specific differences
- superseded architecture
- unresolved questions
The Understanding Engine must not silently combine conflicting material.

It should identify:

- the source of each position
- document status
- authority
- date or version where relevant
- the nature of the disagreement
- whether the conflict can be resolved
- whether Companion Intelligence should present multiple routes
Disagreement is information.

It should not be hidden merely to produce a simple answer.

---

# Boundaries
The Understanding Engine must not:

- manufacture missing facts
- treat retrieval as understanding
- hide uncertainty
- bypass Companion Intelligence
- override governance
- convert one experience directly into inheritance
- expose private information without purpose and permission
- confuse professional information with universal learning
- optimise for technical complexity
- present confidence it cannot justify
Its role is to improve understanding, not to claim omniscience.

---

# Initial Implementation Direction
The first implementation should remain simple.

It may begin with:

1. the repository-wide Knowledge Index
2. recognised concepts
3. document titles and headings
4. concept-to-document relationships
5. transparent source references
6. simple questions such as:

- Where is this concept discussed?
- Which documents connect these concepts?
- What principles may apply?
7. explicit unmatched or uncertain results
The first version does not require:

- embeddings
- a vector database
- a graph database
- autonomous decisions
- hidden reasoning
- complex machine-learning infrastructure
Architecture should guide implementation.

Implementation should grow only when experience demonstrates a genuine need.
