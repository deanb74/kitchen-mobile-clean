# Helping Hand Evolution Principles

These are architectural laws for how Helping Hand should evolve.

They are not technical implementation rules.

They exist to answer one question:

What should happen next?

---

## Law 1: Purpose Before Change

No change should proceed unless it serves human purpose.

If a change improves systems but does not improve human outcomes, it should not be promoted.

## Law 2: Local First

Every new capability begins as a local response to a real need.

Helping Hand should not design universal capability in abstraction from lived context.

## Law 3: Evidence Before Promotion

Capability Promotion is an event, not an assumption.

A capability is promoted only when operational evidence suggests it may benefit others.

## Law 4: Governance Before Inheritance

Inheritance Review is required before promoted capability becomes shared.

Governance determines whether boundaries, ownership, and constitutional alignment are sufficient.

## Law 5: Shared by Merit, Not Novelty

Nothing becomes shared because it is new.

It becomes shared because it has earned the right through evidence.

## Law 6: Universal Mechanism, Local Expression

Shared inheritance should preserve universal capability while protecting local professional expression.

Helping Hand should centralise what is universal and preserve what is local.

## Law 7: Correct Ownership

Approved capability must be assigned to the correct owner.

Shared inheritance may belong to Academy, Operating System, HQ, Governance, or Constitution depending on its nature.

## Law 8: Character Constrains Capability

No capability may evolve in conflict with constitutional character.

Helping Hand should prefer trustworthy outcomes over faster autonomy.

## Law 9: Shared Capability Must Be Explainable

Every inherited capability should preserve provenance.

Helping Hand should always be able to explain where capability came from, why it was approved, and who governs it.

## Law 10: Evolution Is Continuous

Shared capability remains under observation after inheritance.

Future evidence may refine, extend, or retire inherited capability.

---

## Operating Axiom

Nothing becomes shared because it is new.

It becomes shared because it has earned the right through evidence.

---

## Practical Use

When deciding what should happen next, apply these laws in order:

1. Does this serve human purpose?
2. Is the need real and evidenced locally?
3. Is there enough evidence for Capability Promotion?
4. Has Inheritance Review confirmed boundaries, ownership, and alignment?
5. If inherited, is the owner correct and the provenance clear?
6. How will the capability remain observable and improvable over time?
7. If this proves valuable, who should inherit it next?
