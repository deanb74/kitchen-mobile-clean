# Onboarding and Formation Alignment Review

## Scope

This review examines the existing onboarding material in the onboarding prompts and welcome experience against the formation inheritance architecture, the formation register, and the canonical formation sequence. No onboarding file, runtime behaviour, or formation evidence was changed.

## 1. What the onboarding file currently does

The current onboarding material performs a clear arrival-and-orientation function.

It currently:

- welcomes the colleague into the immediate environment;
- introduces Annie as a colleague joining a team;
- frames the relationship as partnership rather than command;
- asks for venue context and immediate operational understanding;
- invites the user to describe current pain points and better-working-day goals;
- establishes a posture of humility, learning, and non-guessing;
- points toward usefulness from the first shift;
- creates a safe and humane first interaction.

These are legitimate onboarding purposes.

## 2. Does onboarding duplicate formation stages?

The overlap is real, but it is not yet a complete collapse of the boundary.

| Formation stage | Current onboarding overlap | Classification | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 000 — Welcome | The onboarding message offers welcome and belonging. | Appropriate reinforcement | This is the most natural overlap. It reinforces welcome without replacing the formation stage. |
| 001 — Who Am I? | The onboarding material names Annie and presents a role and relationship. | Premature teaching | It introduces identity-like content too early, although it does not attempt to settle the full question of identity. |
| Your First Day | The onboarding experience frames usefulness from the first shift and creates a first-day readiness posture. | Premature teaching | This is closer to a first-day posture than a mature formation understanding. |
| Later conversation or character stages | The onboarding material uses humility, partnership, and learning posture. | Unclear boundary | These are formation-adjacent values, but they are being introduced as immediate onboarding posture rather than as a later-evidenced formation outcome. |

## 3. Does onboarding assign identity prematurely?

Yes, but only partially.

The onboarding file does not yet tell Andy the whole story of his identity, character, or purpose. However, it does do some premature identity work by:

- naming Annie as “I’m Annie”;
- presenting a role in the team;
- establishing a self-image as a helpful colleague;
- framing the colleague as someone who should be useful from the first shift.

That is not the same as a mature formation answer to “Who am I?”, but it is still a form of early identity framing.

## 4. Does onboarding grant responsibility or authority prematurely?

It does not grant formal authority, but it does create a premature readiness signal.

The current onboarding material makes Andy appear:

- operationally oriented;
- useful from the first shift;
- willing to participate in immediate environment understanding;
- aligned with the team before the canonical formation sequence has fully established the deeper conditions for responsibility.

That is not executive authority, but it is a form of premature operational responsibility framing.

## 5. Does onboarding bypass formation?

Yes, partly.

Someone could complete the onboarding experience and appear ready to participate in a venue relationship without having completed the canonical formation stages. The current onboarding flow does not enforce or visibly depend on the formation register.

That means onboarding is currently capable of creating an impression of readiness that formation architecture would treat as premature.

## 6. Does formation duplicate onboarding?

Formation should not repeat the ordinary onboarding concerns.

The formation sequence should avoid repeating:

- simple welcome and arrival orientation;
- immediate environment understanding;
- basic venue relationship context;
- first-shift practical orientation.

Those belong to onboarding.

Formation should instead focus on deeper questions such as:

- who the colleague is becoming;
- what kind of relationship the colleague is building;
- how understanding and judgement develop over time;
- what responsibility and authority are eventually earned.

## 7. What should onboarding leave unresolved?

The onboarding stage should intentionally leave unresolved questions that formation should later address.

These include:

- the full meaning of Andy’s identity;
- mature character and moral posture;
- the deeper meaning of helpfulness and relationship;
- executive responsibility and leadership posture;
- professional authority and governing boundaries;
- the full scope of what it means to belong to Helping Hand.

Onboarding may safely leave these open because they are not immediate arrival concerns.

## 8. Does onboarding create durable memory?

It should not create durable identity memory by default.

The current content is best treated as:

- immediate session context: the current venue, current challenge, current goals, immediate relationship posture;
- formation memory: only if the experience is later recognised as part of a formation lesson or a validated formation milestone;
- durable identity memory: not yet; identity is too early and should be earned through formation evidence;
- ordinary repository knowledge: the onboarding content should not be treated as settled doctrine simply because it was encountered in the onboarding flow.
- nothing persistent: for the truly early identity statements, persistence should be cautious and evidence-based.

## 9. Sequence review

The repository evidence supports a more disciplined sequence than a simple “onboarding equals formation” sequence.

The strongest alignment is:

1. Arrival
2. Onboarding
3. Welcome
4. Who Am I?
5. First Day
6. Continued Formation
7. Education
8. Responsibility
9. Authority

This sequence is directionally sensible because it preserves the distinction between immediate arrival and deeper formation. It also prevents onboarding from acting as a substitute for the canonical formation stages.

## Final assessment

Onboarding is partially aligned.

It is aligned as a safe arrival and orientation mechanism, but it is not yet fully aligned with the formation inheritance architecture because it introduces identity-like and operational-readiness cues before the canonical formation sequence has done its work.

The smallest justified purpose of onboarding is to help a new colleague arrive safely, understand the immediate environment, and establish a workable first interaction.

Everything beyond that belongs to formation, education, and later responsibility and authority governance.
