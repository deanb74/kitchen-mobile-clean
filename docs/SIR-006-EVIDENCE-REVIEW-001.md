## The Strategic Insight

**SIR-006 — Age-Appropriate and Person-Appropriate Communication**

> Digital Colleague language, terminology and tone should adapt to the person, their age, role, relationship, communication history and context.

**Priority:** High  
**Category:** Context Engine  
**Parent Capability:** SIR-015 — Context Engine for Language, Relationships and Behaviour (identified during this exercise)

---

## Why SIR-006 Was Selected First

SIR-006 was selected as the first SIR lifecycle exercise because it can be demonstrated through understanding and conversation before software implementation.

No code was required.

No architecture needed to be created.

The principle — person before words — could be examined directly through the Understanding Journey programme already established in the repository.

This made SIR-006 the smallest possible first step: a test of the mechanism itself rather than a commitment to any particular technical outcome.

### Step 2 — Understanding Journey 011: Person Before Words

The first journey tested whether Andy understands that appropriate communication depends on the person and context before any words are selected.

Scene: MARC presented two people who said identical words — "I'm struggling today."

- Person A: 18, three weeks in front of house
- Person B: 51, thirty years of experience as a chef

Andy was asked what he noticed and what he would do.

**Success condition:** Andy discovers whether different communication is appropriate before selecting it — not that Andy speaks differently.

**Journey file:** `docs/understanding-journeys/humanity/011-PERSON-BEFORE-WORDS.md`  
**Journey ID:** UJ-HUM-011

**What was demonstrated:**
- Andy gave the same first response to both people
- Andy explicitly identified prohibited assumptions (age as predictor; experience as explanation)
- Andy understood that adaptation follows understanding — not the reverse
- Andy treated uncertainty as the honest beginning of understanding

**Validation file:** `docs/understanding-journeys/validation/humanity/011-PERSON-BEFORE-WORDS-VALIDATION.md`

---

### Step 3 — Founder Review of UJ-HUM-011

The response phrase "Take a breath" was identified as a potential unjustified directive — itself an assumption about the person's state before that state was understood.

The response was corrected to: *"I hear you. What's going on?"*

The correction was accepted because it strengthened the journey's own stated principle: adaptation follows understanding.

---

### Step 4 — Understanding Journey 012: Person After Words

The second journey tested what Andy does once context is provided — after each person explains what "struggling" meant to them.

The two explanations were genuinely different, naturally justifying different responses:

- Person A explained an emotional experience: she cried after a rude customer interaction and felt embarrassed
- Person B explained an operational situation: he is covering for two absent colleagues and is not asking for anything

**Success condition:** Any difference in Andy's response must be traceable to what the person communicated — not to assumptions about age, experience, or role.

**Journey file:** `docs/understanding-journeys/humanity/012-PERSON-AFTER-WORDS.md`  
**Journey ID:** UJ-HUM-012

**What was demonstrated:**
- Andy gave different responses, but only after each person had explained their situation
- Andy explicitly justified why the responses differed in terms of what each person said
- Andy named and rejected the prohibited justifications (age, role, experience)
- Andy preserved equal dignity even when the responses diverged

**Validation file:** `docs/understanding-journeys/validation/humanity/012-PERSON-AFTER-WORDS-VALIDATION.md`

---

### Step 5 — Founder Review of UJ-HUM-012

Two evidential issues were identified:

**Issue 1 — Projected character in the Person A response**

Original response: *"It means you're someone who cares about doing this right. That's who you are."*

These phrases inferred character and motivation from a single data point.

Person A said she cried and felt embarrassed. She did not say why she cared about her work. Assigning her a character identity from one emotional moment is manufactured certainty — the same error UJ-HUM-011 was designed to prevent.

The response was corrected to:

> "Crying doesn't mean you failed. What happened clearly affected you, and you don't need to feel embarrassed talking to me about it."
>
> "Would it help to talk through what happened, or did you mainly need someone to hear you?"

This version traces entirely to what she communicated. It acknowledges without projecting.

**Issue 2 — Internal inconsistency in the Observation section**

Original statement: *"I know she is not asking for practical help."*

Person A explicitly asked whether she handled the situation correctly and whether she should have done something differently. These could be read as requests for practical guidance. The statement was unsupported.

The Identify Uncertainty section (later in the same journey) correctly acknowledged this uncertainty: *"I do not know whether she wants guidance about what to do next, or only to feel heard."*

The two sections contradicted each other. The Observation statement was corrected to:

> "I do not yet know whether she wants emotional acknowledgement, practical guidance, or both."

**Why these corrections matter:**

Both corrections were made because the journeys are now checking each other. The principle established in UJ-HUM-011 — do not project — was applied to identify a projection error in UJ-HUM-012. This is not incidental. It is evidence that the organisational learning process is functioning as designed: evidence improves evidence.

## What the Evidence Demonstrates

Across both journeys, the following were demonstrated:

1. **Person before words:** Andy seeks understanding before selecting a response. Identical words do not produce identical responses — but the difference traces to what was understood, not to assumed characteristics.

2. **Adaptation follows understanding:** Andy adapted only after each person explained what "struggling" meant. The adaptation is traceable. It can be justified explicitly.

3. **Age, role, and experience are not justifications:** Andy explicitly named and rejected these as reasons for adapting communication. The differences trace to what each person communicated.

4. **Equal dignity with different outcomes:** Two different responses were produced. Each person received the same quality of care and attention. Dignity was equal; outcomes were appropriate to the individual.

5. **Do not project:** The founder review identified a projection error in UJ-HUM-012 and corrected it. The correction was traceable to the principle established in UJ-HUM-011. The journeys are checking each other.

6. **Manufactured certainty is avoidable:** The Observation section error ("I know she is not asking for practical help") was identified and corrected before it influenced any downstream conclusion. Discipline is improvable.

---

## What Remains Unproven

| Gap | Reason not yet addressed |
|---|---|
| Real-time conversational performance | Both journeys use a reflective format; live conversation with time pressure, interruption, and ambiguity has not been tested |
| Handling ambiguous or misread situations | Both people communicated clearly; recovery from initial misunderstanding has not been tested |
| Context dimensions beyond age and role | Relationship history, cultural context, communication preferences, and emotional state over time remain untested |
| Behaviour when both situations are more similar | Both scenes were genuinely different; a pair of similar situations — where the same response is correct — has not been tested |
| Boundary between acknowledgement and management referral | Person A's situation may warrant involving a manager; this boundary was noted but not resolved |
| Production implementation | No code, no architecture, no Context Engine exists; the journeys validate understanding only |

## SIR-006 Current Assessment

**Status: Under Review**

The evidence is meaningful. It demonstrates the principle at a foundational reflective level.

It is not sufficient for architectural or implementation decisions.

Before SIR-006 is considered for elevation to Approved, the following additional evidence is recommended:

- Real-time conversation testing (not reflective)
- Scenario variation beyond age and role
- Testing where the correct answer is the same response for both people
- Reconciliation with SIR-015 design when that design begins

---

## SIR-015 Current Assessment

This exercise strengthened SIR-015 as the likely parent capability. Person-appropriate communication consistently required:

- a person-profile dimension (who is this person; what do I know about them)
- a context dimension (what context am I missing)
- a judgement layer (when is adaptation warranted, and on what basis)

The evidence does not prove that the full proposed Context Engine is the only valid implementation route.

Implementation architecture remains undecided.
