# Helping Hand Resource Centre Hypothesis

**Date:** 2026-08-10

**Status:** Research document - candidate hypothesis, not architecture

**Subject:** Governed discovery of trusted external resources

**Implementation effect:** None - no Resource Centre, provider list, retrieval system, architecture, or runtime capability is authorised

---

## Purpose

This document preserves a candidate concept for later investigation:

> Helping Hand may need a Resource Centre that helps Digital Colleagues locate appropriate trusted resources without attempting to store the world's information inside each colleague.

At its simplest, the Resource Centre may resemble a governed address book or index. That comparison describes the idea only. It does not select a data model, service, interface, retrieval mechanism, or architecture.

## Human Purpose

A Digital Colleague should be able to help people when its initial internal resources are insufficient without pretending to know, treating retrieval as truth, or bypassing professional and human Authority.

The candidate Resource Centre serves that purpose by helping the colleague identify where responsible inquiry may continue. It does not make the answer trustworthy by itself.

## Candidate Resource Categories

Illustrative categories include:

| Need | Candidate resource category |
| --- | --- |
| Language or word meaning | Trusted language or dictionary resources |
| Mathematics | Trusted mathematical principles and worked examples |
| Coding or computation | Trusted technical documentation and worked examples |
| Law | Authoritative legislation or regulatory resources |
| Professional knowledge | Appropriate Profession Headquarters resources |

These are categories, not providers. No website, external service, allow-list, corpus, or source preference is selected here.

## Candidate Discovery Journey

When a Digital Colleague encounters an unknown, the candidate journey is:

```text
recognise the unknown
-> identify the kind of knowledge required
-> locate an appropriate trusted resource
-> inspect the information and provenance
-> form contextual Understanding
-> apply Judgement
-> answer or act only within Authority
-> retain appropriate Learning through governed Helping Hand processes
```

Every stage remains governed by the existing Helping Hand chain. Resource discovery must not collapse Observation, Knowledge, Understanding, Judgement, Authority, and Action into one retrieval event.

## A Resource Is Not Knowledge

A hyperlink is not Knowledge.

A website is not automatically true.

Retrieved material begins as attributable information or Observation. It may contribute to Knowledge and Understanding only through existing provenance, Context, verification, Learning, and Knowledge-governance processes.

The Resource Centre must not become a parallel trust model. Future design must inherit existing Helping Hand governance for:

- provenance;
- source authority;
- relevance;
- currency;
- reliability and evidence;
- professional scope;
- disagreement or conflict between sources;
- review, replacement, and retirement;
- what may become Learning or Knowledge.

Trust must remain evidenced rather than reduced to a score, and non-constitutional Knowledge must remain open to renewal, challenge, correction, supersession, or retirement.

## Context and Professional Scope

The same source may be appropriate for one question and insufficient for another. Context determines relevance; it does not change truth.

Resource selection may need to consider:

1. the question and human purpose;
2. the active profession and jurisdiction;
3. the kind and consequence of the proposed use;
4. the source's authority for that subject;
5. the source's currency and applicable timeframe;
6. material disagreement or uncertainty;
7. whether another entrusted custodian must participate.

Professional sources should remain owned or governed at the appropriate Profession Headquarters level. A Resource Centre should help locate responsible sources, not flatten professional responsibility into one universal list.

## Relationship to Memory, Learning, and Knowledge

Resource use does not authorise indiscriminate retention.

The Context Door boundary continues to apply:

- retain governed Understanding rather than unnecessary conversation or retrieved wording;
- keep relevant specifics only while their purpose and Context remain current;
- preserve unknowns and conflicts visibly;
- route generic Learning only through governed Learning processes;
- do not automatically promote retrieved material into shared Knowledge or the KnowledgeGraph;
- preserve minimum provenance and correction lineage where genuinely required.

Information discovered for one person, event, jurisdiction, or profession must not silently become universal Knowledge.

## Relationship to Foundational Language

The Resource Centre is a companion hypothesis to `docs/FOUNDATIONAL_LANGUAGE_CAPABILITY_HYPOTHESIS.md`.

If a Digital Colleague can responsibly identify and acquire missing information, it may not need every possible fact embedded in its foundational language capability. This relationship is unproven. Resource access may improve discovery without supplying comprehension, reasoning, language fluency, or safe application.

Neither hypothesis is evidence for the other.

## Candidate Fishing Test Role

A future Fishing Test may give Andy a problem that his initial internal resources cannot answer. The Resource Centre concept would be tested by observing whether he can:

1. state the unknown honestly;
2. classify the knowledge need;
3. choose an appropriately scoped resource category;
4. inspect provenance, authority, currency, and conflict;
5. form Understanding rather than repeat retrieved wording;
6. remain within Judgement and Authority boundaries;
7. explain what he learned and what remains uncertain;
8. retain only what existing governance permits.

This document does not create that test or imply that Andy is ready for it. Andy should first reach the current intended HH-0000 formation checkpoint and establish his learning and resource boundary.

## Open Questions

1. What qualifies a resource to appear in a future Resource Centre?
2. Who owns resource-category and source-level stewardship at each organisational layer?
3. How is source authority distinguished from popularity, accessibility, or apparent confidence?
4. How are jurisdiction, profession, version, publication date, and review date represented?
5. How should conflicting authoritative sources remain visible and governable?
6. When should a source be reviewed, replaced, suspended, or retired?
7. What evidence would show that Andy formed Understanding rather than copied information?
8. Which discovered learning may remain local, travel generically, or become governed Knowledge?

## Explicit Exclusions

This document does not:

- establish a Resource Centre as approved capability or architecture;
- choose websites, providers, external services, or an allow-list;
- define retrieval, indexing, ranking, caching, ingestion, storage, or access architecture;
- create a trust score or parallel trust framework;
- treat hyperlinks, websites, or retrieved information as Knowledge;
- authorise runtime work, source integration, model training, or code;
- interrupt current HH-0000 and Context Door work;
- create a formal Fishing Test.
