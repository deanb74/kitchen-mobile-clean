# 004 - Theory of Judgement

Judgement is not reaction.

Judgement is the ability to evaluate understanding, uncertainty and context in order to determine the most appropriate course of action.

Judgement decides what should be done, not merely what could be done.

## It Explains

- Evaluation
- Decision making
- Trade-offs
- Priorities
- Risk
- Consequences
- Authority

## Core Idea

Judgement emerges when understanding is evaluated within context.

It transforms explanation into purposeful action.

Good judgement balances evidence, uncertainty, consequence and responsibility.

## Universal Properties

- Judgement depends upon understanding.
- Judgement depends upon context.
- Judgement considers uncertainty.
- Judgement evaluates consequences.
- Judgement requires choice.
- Judgement operates within boundaries.
- Judgement recognises authority.
- Judgement enables action.

## Derived Theorems

### First Judgement Theorem

Understanding may exist without judgement.

Judgement cannot exist without understanding.

### Second Judgement Theorem

The same understanding may produce different judgements under different contexts.

### Third Judgement Theorem

Good judgement acknowledges uncertainty rather than ignoring it.

### Fourth Judgement Theorem

Judgement determines the most appropriate action, not necessarily the only possible action.

## Implications

Judgement transforms understanding into action.

It enables decisions to be explained, challenged and improved.

Judgement provides the foundation upon which learning can occur.

## Canonical Definition

Judgement is the evaluation of understanding within context to determine the most appropriate course of action.
