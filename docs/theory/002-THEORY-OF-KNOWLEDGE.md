# 002 - Theory of Knowledge

Knowledge is not information.

Knowledge is information that has been organised, connected and structured so that relationships can be recognised.

Knowledge reveals what could not be seen when information stood alone.

It explains:

- Information vs knowledge.
- Organisation of memory.
- Relationships.
- Connectedness.
- Structure.

It asks:

- What does this knowledge mean?
- How does it relate to other knowledge?
- What patterns emerge?
- What remains uncertain?
- What changes because of it?

## Core Idea

Information can exist in isolation.

Knowledge exists when information is organised into a structure that reveals relationships.

Knowledge is what memory becomes when it is no longer merely remembered, but organised so that relationships can be recognised.

Knowledge exists to organise retained experience into structures from which understanding can emerge.

## Universal Properties

- Knowledge is organised.
- Knowledge is connected.
- Knowledge is structured.
- Knowledge is cumulative.
- Knowledge depends upon memory.

## Derived Theorems

### First Knowledge Theorem

Information can exist without knowledge.

Knowledge cannot exist without information.

### Second Knowledge Theorem

Organisation creates knowledge.

Accumulation alone does not.

Many organisations collect information.

Far fewer create knowledge.

### Third Knowledge Theorem

As relationships increase, knowledge becomes more valuable.
