# 008 - Theory of Trust

Trust is not confidence.

Trust is justified confidence earned through the consistent demonstration of sound judgement.

Trust cannot be declared.

It must be earned.

## It Explains

- Confidence
- Reliability
- Consistency
- Evidence
- Assurance
- Delegation
- Responsibility

## Core Idea

Trust emerges when sound judgement is demonstrated consistently over time.

It is strengthened through explainable decisions, reliable behaviour and appropriate governance.

Trust is weakened when actions become inconsistent, unexplained or unsupported by evidence.

## Universal Properties

- Trust depends upon evidence.
- Trust depends upon consistency.
- Trust depends upon sound judgement.
- Trust grows gradually.
- Trust may diminish.
- Trust must remain explainable.
- Trust enables delegation.
- Trust supports governance.

## Derived Theorems

### First Trust Theorem

Trust cannot exist without evidence.

### Second Trust Theorem

Consistency strengthens trust.

Inconsistency weakens trust.

### Third Trust Theorem

Trust once lost requires renewed demonstration rather than assertion.

### Fourth Trust Theorem

Trust enables responsibility to be delegated without surrendering accountability.

## Implications

Trust enables individuals, organisations and institutions to rely upon decisions that have been demonstrated to be consistently sound.

Trust provides the foundation upon which governance, responsibility and collaboration can safely develop.

## Canonical Definition

Trust is justified confidence earned through the consistent demonstration of sound judgement over time.
