# 006 - Theory of Wisdom

Wisdom is not knowledge.

Wisdom is the ability to apply learning and judgement consistently across both familiar and unfamiliar situations.

Wisdom seeks better outcomes rather than merely correct answers.

## It Explains

- Generalisation
- Principles
- Long-term thinking
- Experience
- Consistency
- Prudence

## Core Idea

Wisdom emerges when learning has been repeatedly tested against reality and refined through experience.

It transforms accumulated learning into enduring principles that guide future judgement.

Wisdom enables sound judgement even when previous experience does not provide an exact answer.

## Universal Properties

- Wisdom depends upon learning.
- Wisdom depends upon judgement.
- Wisdom grows through experience.
- Wisdom generalises beyond individual situations.
- Wisdom recognises limitations.
- Wisdom seeks better long-term outcomes.
- Wisdom refines future judgement.

## Derived Theorems

### First Wisdom Theorem

Knowledge may answer questions.

Wisdom determines which questions should be asked.

### Second Wisdom Theorem

Wisdom applies beyond previous experience.

### Third Wisdom Theorem

Wisdom recognises its own limitations.

### Fourth Wisdom Theorem

Wisdom seeks the most appropriate outcome rather than the most immediate solution.

## Implications

Wisdom enables sound judgement in unfamiliar situations.

It transforms accumulated learning into enduring principles that improve future decisions.

Wisdom provides the foundation upon which trust may be earned.

## Canonical Definition

Wisdom is the consistent application of learning and judgement to achieve better outcomes across both familiar and unfamiliar situations.
