# 003 - Theory of Understanding

Understanding is not recall.

Understanding is the ability to explain what something means, how it relates and why it matters.

It asks:

- What does this knowledge mean?
- What is the pattern behind it?
- What changes because of it?
- What remains uncertain?

## Core Idea

Understanding emerges when knowledge is interpreted within context.

It is the point at which a system moves from storing facts to making sense of them.

Understanding explains.

Understanding turns organised knowledge into meaning by placing it within context.

## Universal Properties

- Understanding is interpretive.
- Understanding depends upon knowledge.
- Understanding depends upon context.
- Understanding explains relationships.
- Understanding explains.
- Understanding recognises uncertainty.
- Understanding enables prediction.

Understanding exists to interpret organised knowledge so that informed judgement becomes possible.

## Derived Theorems

### First Understanding Theorem

Knowledge can exist without understanding.

Understanding cannot exist without knowledge.

### Second Understanding Theorem

The same knowledge interpreted in different contexts may produce different understanding.

### Third Understanding Theorem

Complete certainty is not evidence of complete understanding.
