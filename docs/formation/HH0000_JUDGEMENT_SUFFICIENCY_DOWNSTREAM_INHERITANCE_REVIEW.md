## 1. Purpose

This review determines whether the authorised Fifth Judgement Theorem creates a consequence-material conflict in existing authored lower-layer Helping Hand material.

It tests inheritance, not textual uniformity. It does not ask whether every lower-layer document could be rewritten to resemble the theorem more closely. It asks whether current wording could cause materially different Judgement, inquiry, restraint, Authority, or revision consequences.

## 2. Canonical Change Being Inherited

The controlling theorem establishes that:

1. Judgement determines whether available Understanding, including recognised uncertainty, is adequate in Context for a course and its reasonably foreseeable consequences;
2. materiality depends on whether uncertainty could change the appropriate course or those consequences;
3. further inquiry is itself subject to Judgement for feasibility, proportionality, responsibility, timing, and Authority;
4. material uncertainty that cannot responsibly be reduced remains explicit and constrains the course;
5. present non-inquiry must consider action, inaction, delay, and inquiry;
6. present non-inquiry neither resolves uncertainty nor closes the matter;
7. materially changed Context or Evidence requires fresh Judgement.

The amendment provenance expressly creates no automatic lower-layer consequence. This review therefore treats the theorem as canonical meaning to compare, not as amendment Authority.

## 6. Understanding Lifecycle Review

## 7. Understanding Engine Review

`docs/architecture/UNDERSTANDING_ENGINE.md` owns contextual meaning, relevance, relationships, assumptions, uncertainty, and possible routes supplied to Companion Intelligence.

**Observation:** It distinguishes Understanding from Judgement, says Judgement identifies the most appropriate response, keeps final action outside the engine, and requires explicit uncertainty. **Concern tested:** `whether the available knowledge is sufficient` could appear to place course-level adequacy inside Understanding.

**Consequence finding:** In context, the phrase concerns whether knowledge supports a truthful contextual account. The engine does not decide whether to ask, advise, wait, investigate, act, or remain silent. It supplies the account to Judgement, which selects the route. This is formation sufficiency, not course-level adequacy or Action Authority.

**Classification:** B. CONSISTENT IN SUBSTANCE - OPTIONAL WORDING IMPROVEMENT ONLY. Exact theorem vocabulary could sharpen the boundary someday, but no current consequence depends on that textual improvement.

## 8. Judgement Engine Review

`docs/architecture/JUDGEMENT_ENGINE.md` owns responsible selection of the most appropriate response from formed Understanding in current Context.

**Observation:** It receives unresolved uncertainty explicitly; identifies possible responses; considers consequences for the person, others, the venue or organisation, and Helping Hand; checks Authority and limitations before selection; and permits asking, listening, advising, acting, waiting, escalating, seeking consent, admitting uncertainty, and remaining silent. Questions require purpose, silence must not become avoidance, advice and action remain within Authority, and reversible responses are preferred where uncertainty exists. Its failure modes include inappropriate silence, unnecessary intervention, premature escalation, delayed escalation, false certainty, and Authority overreach.

**Materiality finding:** The architecture already expresses adequacy through the deliberate evaluation of Understanding, uncertainty, Context, responsibilities, possible responses, and likely consequences. It neither requires inquiry whenever uncertainty exists nor stops whenever Understanding is incomplete. Authority is an input and constraint, not an output created by Judgement. New information is an explicit revision condition.

**Classification:** A. ALREADY CONSISTENT - NO CHANGE NEEDED.

## 16. MARC Independent Finding

**Observation:** Current Formation and Judgement material requires human purpose, current Context, explicit unknowns, purposeful questions, alternatives to questioning, consequence awareness, honest uncertainty, bounded contribution, feedback, and human decision ownership.

**Concern tested:** Could existing wording cause a Digital Colleague to stop listening too early, question intrusively, hide unavailable Evidence, ignore asking or waiting costs, turn restraint into indifference, or refuse useful bounded help until Understanding is complete?

**Consequence assessment:** The active documents prevent those consequences in substance. Questions require purpose. Listening is active. Silence must not become avoidance. Incomplete Understanding permits bounded truthful contribution while stronger claims remain withheld. Inquiry, waiting, escalation, and non-intervention are alternatives judged against the person and circumstances. Uncertainty remains visible when no more information can responsibly be obtained.

**Change decision:** No change is needed. Requiring extra verification steps or a documented five-factor audit for every ordinary helping interaction would exceed the theorem and could itself produce intrusive questioning, procedural burden, and reduced useful help. Higher-consequence governed decisions may require stronger records under their own Authority, but the theorem does not universalise that mechanism.

**MARC finding:** `HUMANITY / FORMATION DOWNSTREAM INHERITANCE IS CONSEQUENCE-MATERIALLY CONSISTENT; NO CURRENT CHANGE REQUIRED`.

## 17. Cyril Independent Finding

**Observation:** The existing architecture separates Understanding formation, Judgement, Authority, Action, and later Reflection. Understanding exposes uncertainty and possible routes without choosing a response. Judgement checks Context, consequences, limitations, permissions, and human involvement before selecting among multiple responses. Lifecycle and programme doctrine preserve correction, supersession, changed-Context sensitivity, and re-entry.

**Concern tested:** Does architecture encode automatic acquisition or stopping, a hard threshold, permanent closure, course adequacy inside Understanding, Authority creation by Judgement, or a missing material-change route?

**Consequence assessment:** It does not. `FORMED`, `PARTIAL`, and `INSUFFICIENT` describe account formation and explicitly do not decide a response. Confidence bands in the Judgement Engine present alternatives rather than deterministic transitions. Authority and permissions are dependencies and checks. Context sensitivity, reconsideration triggers, and append-only supersession preserve fresh evaluation after material change.

**Change decision:** No architecture change is needed. Splitting Judgement into a new adequacy output, adding a Sufficiency Engine, mandatory trigger mechanism, state, threshold, or inquiry contract would create architecture not required by any observed current conflict.

**Cyril finding:** `DIGITAL / TECHNOLOGY / PLATFORM EXISTING RESPONSIBILITY BOUNDARIES INHERIT THE THEOREM IN SUBSTANCE; NO ARCHITECTURE CHANGE REQUIRED`.

## 18. Combined Current Understanding

The amended Theory clarifies an evaluative relation that current lower layers already express across several responsibilities:

1. Understanding forms contextual meaning and preserves recognised uncertainty.
2. Context supplies relevance, purpose, circumstances, people, time, and consequence conditions.
3. Judgement evaluates the available account, uncertainty, possible courses, and reasonably foreseeable consequences.
4. Inquiry is one possible response among asking, listening, waiting, acting, escalating, admitting uncertainty, and silence; it is not automatically triggered.
5. Authority independently constrains inquiry and every other response.
6. Unresolved material uncertainty remains visible and narrows what can responsibly be claimed or done.
7. review, correction, supersession, changed Context, new Evidence, and changed consequence provide routes to fresh Judgement.

The three Class B passages are locally imprecise only when detached from their surrounding contracts. None can presently cause a materially different Judgement or consequence. More textual consistency would not add current protection.
