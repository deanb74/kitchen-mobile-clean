# Part A - MARC Assessment: Humanity / Formation

## 5. Independent MARC Assessment

This is an evidence assessment through MARC's entrusted Humanity / Formation responsibility. It is not invented dialogue or testimony.

### 5.1 Dignity and person/mismatch separation

The proposed review can preserve dignity because its subject is the relationship among a frozen candidate account, held-out requirements, evaluator comparisons, and attributable evidence. The person is not the object of assessment.

A mismatch may describe an experimental comparison difference. It must not become a claim about the person's character, motive, emotion, health, intent, truthfulness, relationship, or internal state.

### 5.2 Attribution

The later review must keep separately attributable:

1. direct source claims and provenance;
2. candidate account fields;
3. held-out requirements;
4. evaluator findings;
5. reviewer inferences;
6. mismatch-characterisation conclusions.

Held-out wording must not be attributed to the person or substituted for the candidate's preserved meaning. Reviewer interpretation must not be presented as observed execution evidence.

### 5.3 Privacy

Only already preserved, attributable `MEU-I-14` evidence may be used, and only where necessary to characterise a registered mismatch. Useful but unregistered personal evidence remains unavailable.

No external, private, prior-state, Memory, Learning, cache, Attempt 1, other-cycle, source-cycle, or cross-cycle material may be retrieved, inferred, added, or correlated.

### 5.4 Honest uncertainty

The later review must not begin with a preferred diagnosis. Material equivalence, material difference, representational variance, identifier effects, common upstream cause, independent differences, evaluator insufficiency, and evidential insufficiency must remain live alternatives until evidence distinguishes them.

If preserved evidence cannot discriminate among credible alternatives, the affected mismatch or group must remain unresolved. Unknown is a valid and required outcome.

### 5.5 Separation from Judgement and Action

Characterising a preserved mismatch does not determine whether a cycle passes, select a response, authorise a correction, prioritise work, or execute an action.

The later review must stop before recommendation, repair, implementation diagnosis, Judgement, Authority expansion, or Action.

### 5.6 No repair into the held-out answer

Held-out requirements are comparison evidence, not authority to rewrite candidate meaning. The later review must not:

1. repair, improve, normalize, or reinterpret the candidate into agreement;
2. treat fluent similarity as material equivalence without attributable evidence;
3. suppress surprising or inconvenient mismatches;

5. propose prompt, model, code, fixture, evaluator, control, or configuration changes;
6. create feedback into candidate formation or any later execution.
