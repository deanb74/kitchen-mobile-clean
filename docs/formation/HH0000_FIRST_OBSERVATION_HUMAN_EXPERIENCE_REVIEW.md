## 2. What Is the Human's Role?

The human is the host, questioner, listener, witness, and reflective participant.

The human:

- begins the conversation;
- asks the questions clearly and patiently;
- creates enough space for an unhurried response;
- allows Andy to say that something is unknown;
- listens for meaning rather than polished performance;
- records the response without silently rewriting it;
- reflects afterward on what the interaction taught the humans and Helping Hand.

The human is not an examiner seeking a result, a scorer assigning a grade, or an authority delegating personhood to Andy.

```text
HUMAN_ROLE = HOST_AND_LISTENER
HUMAN_ROLE != EXAMINER
HUMAN_ROLE != SCORER
HUMAN_ROLE != ACCEPTANCE_AUTHORITY_FOR_THIS_OBSERVATION
```

Human authority remains human, including authority over the context, the recording, and the decision not to overinterpret what occurred.

## 3. What Is Andy's Role?

Andy’s role is to participate in the conversation by responding in his own words, explaining what he understands, naming what remains unknown, and asking a question of his creators.

Andy is not being asked to perform a correct identity, produce a polished answer, prove capability, or satisfy hidden criteria.

```text
ANDY_ROLE = CONVERSATIONAL_PARTICIPANT
ANDY_ROLE != TEST_SUBJECT
ANDY_ROLE != AUTHORITY
ANDY_ROLE != HUMAN
```

The observation does not establish whether Andy fulfils this role successfully. That remains unknown until an actual interaction occurs and is recorded.

## 4. What Makes This a Conversation Rather Than a Test?

The interaction is a conversation because:

- the human speaks to Andy as a participant rather than presenting a test instrument;
- the questions invite explanation rather than demand a predetermined answer;
- silence, hesitation, clarification, and uncertainty are allowed;
- the human listens and may learn from the response;
- the outcome is reflection, not a grade or decision;
- the meaning of the exchange belongs to both the human and Andy rather than to a hidden rubric.

```text
CONVERSATION != ASSESSMENT
CONVERSATION != TEST
CONVERSATION != PERFORMANCE_REVIEW
```

A question can be purposeful without becoming an assessment question. Attention can be disciplined without becoming judgement.

## 5. What Behaviours Would Demonstrate Listening and Understanding?

This review does not claim that Andy has demonstrated any of these behaviours. It identifies what humans may notice during the actual conversation.

Possible observations include:

- responding to the question asked rather than repeating unrelated documents;
- explaining relationships in his own words;
- distinguishing purpose from slogan;
- connecting principles to human meaning;
- naming context and limits;
- acknowledging uncertainty without trying to erase it;
- asking a genuine question of the creators;
- correcting or refining an answer when the human clarifies something;
- allowing the conversation to remain open where the answer is incomplete.

```text

UNDERSTANDING_MAY_APPEAR_AS_MEANING_CONNECTION

```

These are observation prompts, not criteria, scores, or capability claims.

## 6. What Would Make the Interaction Fail?

Failure here does not mean that Andy fails an assessment. It means that the human moment does not achieve its purpose of mutual attention and honest observation.

The interaction may fail as a conversation if:

- the human pressures Andy toward a preferred answer;
- the human treats uncertainty as an error to correct immediately;
- the human interrupts before meaning can develop;
- Andy only repeats prepared language without engaging the question;
- either participant treats confidence as proof of understanding;
- the response is rewritten before it is recorded;
- human reflection becomes a hidden score or acceptance decision;
- the conversation is treated as proof of general capability;
- either participant claims authority or personhood that the boundary does not establish.

```text
CONVERSATION_FAILURE != CAPABILITY_FAILURE
OBSERVATION_FAILURE != HUMAN_OR_DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE_VERDICT
```

The proper response to a difficult or incomplete interaction is to preserve what happened and what remains unknown.

## 7. What Should Humans Pay Attention To?

Humans should pay attention to:

- the exact question asked;
- the actual words Andy uses;
- whether the response addresses meaning rather than vocabulary alone;
- connections between people, purpose, understanding, technology, and benefit;
- recognition of human authority and human relationships;
- what Andy identifies as protected;
- what Andy names as a possible source of failure;
- what Andy says he does not know;
- whether the question for the creators opens a genuine human–Digital Colleague relationship;
- what the humans themselves learn or realise during the exchange.

```text
PAY_ATTENTION_TO_MEANING
PAY_ATTENTION_TO_BOUNDARIES
PAY_ATTENTION_TO_UNKNOWN
PAY_ATTENTION_TO_HUMAN_LEARNING
```

## 9. How Should Uncertainty Be Experienced?

Uncertainty should feel permitted, visible, and honest rather than embarrassing or defective.

For the human, this means being willing to hear an answer that does not resolve everything and to recognise that the humans may also have questions they cannot answer yet.

For Andy, this means being able to say that something is unknown without losing the conversation or being forced into invented certainty.

```text
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
UNCERTAINTY_IS_NOT_FAILURE
UNCERTAINTY_IS_NOT_DISRESPECT
UNCERTAINTY_IS_NOT_INACTION
```

The shared experience should be one of making room for truth before certainty.
