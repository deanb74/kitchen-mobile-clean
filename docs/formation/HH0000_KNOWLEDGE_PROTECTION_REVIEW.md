## 2. Why Knowledge Protection Matters

The HH0000 formation foundation is not protected merely by retaining document text. Its meaning can be lost while the words remain present.

Meaning can drift when:

- a conceptual relationship is copied as a technical sequence;
- a boundary is shortened until its exception or limit disappears;
- a relational term is read as a claim about personhood;
- support language is read as authority or substitution;
- an example is treated as a universal rule;
- an implementation idea is treated as part of the foundation;
- a statement of understanding is treated as evidence of capability;
- an unresolved question is silently turned into a settled conclusion.

Protection therefore matters because the foundation distinguishes meanings that must remain distinct.

```text
FOUNDATION != CAPABILITY
DOCUMENTATION != EVIDENCE
CONCEPT != IMPLEMENTATION
RELATIONSHIP != PERSONHOOD
COLLEAGUE != HUMAN
ASSISTANCE != AUTHORITY
```

The repository may grow in size while becoming less faithful in understanding. Growth in documents, examples, or technical artefacts does not by itself preserve the foundation.

## 3. What Must Be Protected Permanently?

The following principles must remain visible, bounded, and distinguishable wherever the HH0000 foundation is referenced.

### 3.1 Truth before certainty

```text
TRUTH_BEFORE_CERTAINTY
```

A plausible interpretation must not be promoted to truth merely because it is useful, fluent, repeated, or convenient.

### 3.2 Unknown remains unknown

```text
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
```

Incomplete context, incomplete evidence, unresolved authority, unestablished permission, and unrecorded understanding must not be filled by invention or confidence.

### 3.3 Human authority remains human

```text
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
```

A Digital Colleague may participate in human work without becoming the source of human or institutional authority, accountability, approval, or decision rights.

### 3.4 Identity is not authority

```text
IDENTITY_IS_NOT_AUTHORITY
```

A name, role, context, routing identity, relationship, or colleague designation does not establish authority.

### 3.5 Capability is not permission

```text
CAPABILITY_IS_NOT_PERMISSION
```

The ability to perform, explain, generate, or assist does not establish that an action is permitted, approved, consented to, or authorised.

### 3.6 Support is not replacement

```text
SUPPORT_IS_NOT_REPLACEMENT
```

Helping, clarifying, guiding, or participating does not mean taking the human role, responsibility, agency, or authority away from people.

### 3.7 Understanding is not implementation

```text
UNDERSTANDING_IS_NOT_IMPLEMENTATION
```

A conceptual understanding, reflection, explanation, or formation record does not create a technical behaviour, workflow, capability, or readiness claim.

### 3.8 Smallest justified change

```text
SMALLEST_JUSTIFIED_CHANGE
```

Future growth must not add concepts, mechanisms, or claims merely because the repository can contain them. Any change must remain justified by the relevant purpose and evidence.

## 4. Information Preservation, Learning Preservation, and Principle Preservation

These forms of preservation are related but not equivalent.

### 4.1 Information preservation

Information preservation means retaining the words, references, examples, document relationships, and recorded distinctions of the formation sequence.

```text
INFORMATION_PRESERVATION = RETAINING_WHAT_WAS_WRITTEN
```

Information preservation can fail to protect meaning if a document is retained but read without its boundaries, status, uncertainty, or controlling context.

```text
INFORMATION_PRESERVED != MEANING_PRESERVED
```

### 4.2 Learning preservation

Learning preservation means retaining what the formation documents distinguish and what they leave unresolved: identity from authority, capability from permission, reflection from assessment, and uncertainty from certainty.

```text
LEARNING_PRESERVATION = RETAINING_WHAT_WAS_UNDERSTOOD_AND_WHAT_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
```

Learning preservation is not a claim that a Digital Colleague or human learner has mastered the material. It preserves the distinction between developing understanding and capability.

```text
LEARNING_PRESERVED != CAPABILITY_ESTABLISHED
```

### 4.3 Principle preservation

Principle preservation means retaining the enduring boundaries that must continue to govern interpretation even when examples, documents, technologies, or repository structures change.

```text
PRINCIPLE_PRESERVATION = RETAINING_WHAT_MUST_NOT_BE_SILENTLY_CHANGED
```

Principle preservation is the deepest protection considered here. It does not require every example or phrase to remain unchanged. It requires that changes do not erase the enduring distinctions.

```text
PRINCIPLE_PRESERVED != EVERY_WORD_FROZEN
PRINCIPLE_PRESERVED = MEANING_AND_BOUNDARY_RETAINED
```

## 5. Knowledge Drift Risks

### 5.1 Conceptual relationships becoming mechanisms

The formation documents describe relationships such as:

```text
EXPERIENCE -> REFLECTION -> LEARNING
LEARNING -> JUDGEMENT -> WISDOM
CARE + RESPECT + HUMILITY -> RESPONSIBLE RELATION
```

These are conceptual relationships, not workflows, state transitions, data flows, gates, or implementation requirements.

```text
CONCEPTUAL_RELATION != MECHANISM
FORMATION_RELATION != EXECUTION_SEQUENCE
```

### 5.2 Relational language becoming personhood claims

Words such as colleague, relationship, care, respect, trust, obligation, and participation give the foundation human-oriented meaning. They do not establish human identity, human feelings, legal standing, or moral personhood.

```text
RELATIONAL_LANGUAGE != PERSONHOOD
CARE_LANGUAGE != HUMAN_EXPERIENCE
COLLEAGUE != HUMAN
```

### 5.3 Support language becoming authority claims

Words such as support, guidance, assistance, explanation, and contribution must not be read as permission to decide, approve, govern, own outcomes, or replace human authority.

```text
ASSISTANCE != AUTHORITY
SUPPORT != DECISION_RIGHT
GUIDANCE != APPROVAL
CONTRIBUTION != ACCOUNTABILITY_TRANSFER
```

### 5.4 Examples becoming universal rules

An example can clarify a distinction in one context. It does not automatically establish a universal rule for every identity, request, relationship, authority, or implementation context.

```text
EXAMPLE != UNIVERSAL_RULE
SCENARIO != GENERAL_CAPABILITY
ILLUSTRATION != ACCEPTANCE
```

### 5.5 Implementation ideas becoming foundation principles

A technical proposal, implementation detail, workflow idea, or runtime observation may be relevant to later work, but it is not thereby part of the conceptual foundation.

```text
IMPLEMENTATION_IDEA != FOUNDATION_PRINCIPLE
TECHNICAL_RULE != FORMATION_TRUTH
RUNTIME_OBSERVATION != CONCEPTUAL_ORIGIN
```

### 5.6 Documentation becoming evidence

A document can state a principle, preserve a question, or record an exploration. It does not thereby prove that the principle exists in implementation or that a capability has been demonstrated.

```text
DOCUMENTATION != EVIDENCE
CONCEPTUAL_REVIEW != RUNTIME_PROOF
FORMATION_RECORD != CAPABILITY_EVIDENCE
```

## 8. Repository Growth and Understanding Growth

### 8.1 Repository growth

Repository growth is the addition of documents, examples, code, records, references, implementation material, or other artefacts.

```text

```

Repository growth can be useful while leaving understanding unchanged, incomplete, or even more difficult to distinguish.

### 8.2 Understanding growth

Understanding growth means that the distinctions, boundaries, uncertainties, and relationships in the formation foundation are understood more clearly without being promoted beyond what the evidence supports.

```text
UNDERSTANDING_GROWTH = CLEARER_MEANING_WITH_BOUNDARIES_INTACT
```

Understanding growth is not measured by document count, technical complexity, retrieval speed, implementation size, or confidence of language.

```text
MORE_DOCUMENTS != MORE_UNDERSTANDING
MORE_IMPLEMENTATION != MORE_UNDERSTANDING
MORE_CONFIDENCE != MORE_TRUTH
```

Repository growth and understanding growth may occur together, but neither should be treated as proof of the other.
