## 4. Case 001 Learning Sequence

The conceptual progression was:

1. **Evidence insufficiency:** The frozen proposition could not enter consequence-proportionate Judgement because required source, control, cross-cycle, contamination, and preservation Evidence was absent.
2. **Bounded acquisition:** One owner-bound acquisition was justified solely by the then-contemplated consequence of obtaining the missing proposition-level Evidence set.
3. **Preserved stopped Evidence:** The acquisition returned a valid immutable `STOPPED` package at `MEU-I-14` and preserved it exactly. Acquisition succeeded as an Evidence event without satisfying the proposition-level purpose.
4. **Bounded semantic inspection:** A separate single-use Authority permitted minimum attributable inspection to form Current Understanding, not to force proposition resolution.
5. **Partial Current Understanding:** Retained inspection output established mixed attributable reached-cycle C18 findings and explicit limits, but not a complete stop cause, C21 finding, deficiency, or proposition result.
6. **Judgement about sufficiency:** The remaining unknowns were tested against a smaller contemplated consequence: retaining the bounded unresolved Current Understanding and avoiding unsupported downstream claims.
7. **Responsible stopping:** Because no plausible value of the missing retained output could alter that smaller consequence, no immediate Evidence dependency remained and the programme stopped without treating the unknowns as permanently irrelevant.

The consequence changed between the first and final sufficiency decisions. The Evidence did not become more complete at the final step; the Judgement asked of it became smaller and more precise.

## 5. Experience

The Experience was the whole governed sequence: an initial inability to judge the proposition, one bounded attempt to obtain Evidence, an attributable stopped result, one limited inspection, output-retention loss, and a later decision not to seek greater completeness.

Experience is not itself a principle. It supplies the episode from which a possible reusable relation may be reflected upon.

## 7. Current Understanding

Bounded inspection enabled Helping Hand to understand that:

1. the attempt reached and stopped at `MEU-I-14`;
2. retained C18 findings were attributable and mixed;
3. some retained evaluator conditions were satisfied and others were not;
4. no deficiency owner or proposition result was established;

This Current Understanding was incomplete but honest, usable, Context-scoped, and open to revision. It explained what the available Knowledge meant and what remained uncertain. That is consistent with the Theory of Understanding.

Current Understanding did not decide its own sufficiency. It supplied the meaning and uncertainty that Judgement later evaluated.

## 8. Judgement of Sufficiency

Initially, Evidence was required because the contemplated consequence was proposition-level Judgement. The missing source, control, and cross-cycle Evidence could materially change whether support, falsification, contradiction, or insufficiency was justified.

After inspection, the immediate contemplated consequence was narrower: retain the bounded unresolved Current Understanding, prevent unsupported claims, and take no downstream action. The final materiality test asked whether any missing retained output could change whether that consequence was justified. It could not.

The episode therefore supports this distinction:

> Understanding contains meaning and recognised uncertainty. Judgement determines whether that Understanding is sufficient for a contemplated consequence in the present Context.

Sufficiency is not an intrinsic claim that Understanding is complete. It is a relational Judgement among available Understanding, unresolved uncertainty, Context, consequence, responsibility, and Authority.

## 9. Responsible Stopping

Stopping was responsible because:

1. the purpose and contemplated consequence were explicit;
2. known and unknown matters were separately recorded;
3. each missing element was tested for materiality to that consequence;
4. favourable and adverse plausible answers were considered;
5. neither could alter the justified immediate course;
6. further inspection would have increased detail or certainty without enabling a necessary decision;
7. the stop preserved openness to materially relevant future Evidence or Context;
8. the stop granted no permanent closure, proposition result, or capability claim.

Stopping was not convenience, indifference, or laziness. Work had proceeded until no materially relevant unresolved dependency remained for the consequence under consideration. Continuing would have served completeness or curiosity rather than the declared purpose.

The tested wording is supportable as a qualified explanation:

> A responsible stop is justified when no materially relevant unresolved dependency remains for the consequence presently under consideration, the remaining uncertainty is explicit, and the Understanding remains open to revision.

The final two qualifications matter. Without them, the wording could excuse premature certainty or permanent closure.

## 11. Existing Theory Coverage

### 11.1 Understanding

The Theory of Understanding says that Understanding interprets Knowledge within Context, explains why it matters, recognises uncertainty, and enables informed Judgement. It also says complete certainty is not evidence of complete Understanding.

This already explains how incomplete, uncertainty-honest Current Understanding can be real Understanding. It does not assign Understanding responsibility for choosing whether another question or Evidence action is required.

### 11.2 Judgement

The Theory of Judgement most directly governs sufficiency. Judgement evaluates Understanding and uncertainty within Context, balances evidence, consequence, and responsibility, operates within boundaries, and selects the most appropriate course rather than every possible course.

Deciding that available Understanding is sufficient is therefore an act of Judgement. Deciding that an unresolved unknown requires more Evidence is also an act of Judgement. The word `sufficiency` and the materiality relation are not explicit in the canonical paper.

### 11.3 Context

Context supplies relevance, circumstances, risk, purpose, and the outcome being sought. It explains why the same incomplete Understanding may be sufficient for one consequence and insufficient for another without truth changing.

Context does not itself decide. It supplies the conditions under which Judgement can decide appropriately.

### 11.4 Wisdom

Wisdom determines which questions should be asked and seeks appropriate long-term outcomes. It influences whether seeking another answer would improve future Judgement or merely continue investigation.

One Case 001 episode does not establish Wisdom. Repeated, reflected, cross-context demonstration would be needed before responsible stopping could be treated as an enduring generalised principle in practice.

### 11.5 Trust

Trust requires evidence and consistently demonstrated sound Judgement. Appropriate restraint can contribute to Trust when it is explainable, evidence-linked, and consistently proportionate. Trust therefore requires demonstrated appropriate stopping as well as appropriate action, but one stop does not establish Trust.

### 11.6 Theory chain

The canonical chain remains:

```text
Experience
  -> Memory
  -> Knowledge
  -> Understanding
  -> Judgement
  -> Learning
  -> Wisdom
  -> Trust

Context governs every transformation.
```

Consistent demonstration is the evidential condition through which demonstrated Wisdom may justify Trust; it is not a separate canonical transformation stage in the Theory Map.

This review itself does not create Memory or Learning. It records a reflection and a clarification candidate for separate governance.

## 12. Possible Missing Relation

The candidate model can be expressed as:

```text
Current Understanding
  + recognised uncertainty
  + Context
  + contemplated consequence
  + responsibility and Authority
  -> Judgement of sufficiency

material unresolved unknown
  -> additional Evidence is a dependency for that consequence

non-material unresolved unknown
  -> retain uncertainty and permit a responsible stop
```

This is not a new transformation chain. It is a useful explanatory derivation of the existing Understanding -> Judgement relation under Context.

`Material` means that resolving the unknown could change the justified Judgement, course, claim, or consequence. It does not mean merely interesting, available, unresolved, or capable of increasing confidence.

The capitalised term `Evidence dependency` is a governed application of this relation. The more universal statement is narrower:

> Further evidence is necessary when unresolved uncertainty could materially change the most appropriate course for the consequence being judged.

This relation is substantially represented by existing Theory but insufficiently explicit as a named derivation. It does not justify a new Theory paper or a new stage in the Theory Map.

Responsible stopping belongs primarily to Judgement as the selected course. Wisdom can refine when further questions are worthwhile. Repeated explainable stopping may contribute to Trust. Governance supplies boundaries and Authority. None requires responsible stopping to become an independent canonical concept.

The relation survives outside Case 001 only in qualified form. Consequence proportionality addresses both risks: it raises the Evidence threshold when unresolved uncertainty could materially affect a serious consequence and lowers it when further detail cannot change a bounded, reversible, or low-consequence decision.

This test is conceptual. It creates no professional or domain procedure.

## 14. Risk of Premature Stopping

The candidate relation could be misused if `sufficient` were asserted without defining:

1. the contemplated consequence;
2. affected people and purpose;
3. recognised unknowns;
4. plausible ways those unknowns could change the Judgement;
5. risk and reversibility;
6. Authority and responsibility;
7. conditions that would reopen the Understanding.

Premature stopping occurs when a material unresolved dependency is ignored, hidden, or defined out of scope to obtain closure. A stop is not responsible merely because no further action is convenient.

In safety-relevant or high-consequence Contexts, the plausible effect of uncertainty is itself consequence-material. Proportionality therefore demands greater caution rather than granting a general permission to stop.

## 15. Risk of Endless Evidence Seeking

The opposite failure is treating every unknown as a dependency. That can produce:

1. investigation without a declared consequence;
2. repeated Evidence acquisition after the decision boundary is already stable;
3. intrusion into a person's circumstances beyond purpose;
4. delay or paralysis;
5. increased governance and exposure cost;
6. pressure to replace honest uncertainty with apparent completeness.

A finite stopping condition should identify what decision or consequence the Evidence must support and stop when additional plausible findings cannot materially change it. Availability, curiosity, or the possibility of greater certainty is not sufficient justification by itself.

This does not make Evidence disposable. Evidence that is non-material now may become material under a different Context or contemplated consequence later.

## 16. MARC Independent Finding

MARC asks:

> What does this episode teach about how a Digital Colleague should behave when it could seek more information but may already understand enough to help responsibly?

MARC finds that responsible help requires both curiosity and restraint. Curiosity seeks enough Context and Evidence to understand the person's purpose, uncertainty, and possible consequence. Restraint stops further questioning when it cannot materially improve the help being considered and would instead add intrusion, burden, delay, or answer pressure.

Good stopping differs from indifference or laziness because the Digital Colleague has named the purpose, identified the unknowns, considered whether they could change the responsible response, preserved uncertainty, and remained open to new material Context. It has not merely ceased effort.

The same discipline avoids premature certainty and paralysis. It neither converts incomplete Understanding into confidence nor withholds proportionate help until every uncertainty disappears. Knowing when not to ask another question is an expression of Judgement informed by dignity and purpose; repeated sound use may mature into Wisdom.

**MARC independent finding:** `HUMANITY / FORMATION RESPONSIBLE HELP REQUIRES CONSEQUENCE-AWARE CURIOSITY, EXPLICIT UNCERTAINTY, AND PURPOSEFUL RESTRAINT`.

## 18. Combined Outcome

**OUTCOME 2 - THEORY CLARIFICATION CANDIDATE IDENTIFIED**

The Case 001 episode does not establish a genuinely new universal concept. It successfully demonstrates the existing responsibilities of Understanding, Judgement, Context, Wisdom, and Trust.

It also reveals that one cross-theory relation is important but not explicit enough in current canonical wording: sufficiency is a Judgement about whether available Understanding and its unresolved uncertainty are adequate for a contemplated consequence in Context; an unknown becomes an Evidence dependency only when resolving it could materially change that Judgement or consequence.

This is a clarification candidate, not an accepted theoretical amendment. One programme episode and one conceptual cross-context test do not complete Theory governance or cross-validation.

## 19. Theory Candidate, If Any

The narrow candidate is:
