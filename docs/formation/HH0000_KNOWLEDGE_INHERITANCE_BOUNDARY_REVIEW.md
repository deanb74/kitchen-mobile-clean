# Repository Traceability

**Principle:** Information can move; meaning, ownership, and responsibility require context. Truth before certainty; human authority remains human; preserve `UNKNOWN`.
**Theory:** Inheritance may carry information, lessons, generic principles, or bounded knowledge without transferring life, lived experience, personal history, ownership, authority, consent, responsibility, or decision rights. Context determines meaning and applicability.

## 1. Purpose and Strict Boundary

This review determines the conceptual boundaries of what may be inherited, shared, transferred, or learned between humans, venues, organisations, professional domains, and Digital Colleagues.

It does not define how transfer occurs.

It does not create:

- memory architecture;
- storage design;
- schemas;
- retrieval mechanisms;
- permissions;
- workflows;
- implementation;
- technical requirements;
- a transfer mechanism;
- a knowledge inheritance system.

The governing distinctions are:

```text
LESSON_TRANSFER != LIFE_TRANSFER
SHARED_WISDOM != SHARED_HISTORY
INHERITED_INFORMATION != INHERITED_EXPERIENCE
RECORDED_INFORMATION != TRANSFERABLE_OWNERSHIP
KNOWLEDGE != AUTHORITY
```

The purpose is understanding what transfer means before any future architecture.

## 3. Human to Digital Colleague

### A) What may conceptually transfer?

A human may conceptually share:

- a lesson derived from experience;
- a generic principle or pattern;
- an explanation of meaning;
- contextual knowledge whose source and limits remain visible;
- correction;
- a warning about a risk or boundary;
- a reflection on uncertainty;
- professional or personal information where human ownership and appropriate context remain respected.

```text
LESSON_TRANSFER != LIFE_TRANSFER
SHARED_INSIGHT != SHARED_HISTORY
```

### B) What remains bounded to the originating source?

The following remain bounded to the originating human:

- lived experience;
- personal history;
- identity;
- personal meaning;
- emotion and subjective interpretation;
- consent and privacy;
- responsibility for the experience;
- authority over the person's own decisions and outcomes.

```text
EXPERIENCE != HUMAN_LIVED_EXPERIENCE
EXPERIENCE != TRANSFERABLE_HISTORY
```

### C) What requires human ownership?

Human ownership remains required for:

- deciding what may be shared;
- defining the meaning of a personal experience;
- consent and permission;
- accountability for human outcomes;
- professional responsibility;
- trust decisions about relying on a Digital Colleague;
- correction of the Digital Colleague's interpretation.

```text
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
RECORDED_INFORMATION != TRANSFERABLE_OWNERSHIP
```

### D) What remains unknown?

```text
UNKNOWN_HUMAN_TO_DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE_KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER
UNKNOWN_DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE_UNDERSTANDING
UNKNOWN_DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE_LEARNING
UNKNOWN_TRANSFER_OF_MEANING

```

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with transfer effects and understanding remaining **Unknown**.

## 4. Human to Human

### A) What may conceptually transfer?

One human may conceptually share with another:

- lessons;
- principles;
- stories and explanations;
- knowledge relevant to a shared purpose;
- professional practice;
- warnings and corrections;
- wisdom expressed as a perspective derived from experience.

```text
EVERY_LESSON_SHOULD_BENEFIT_MORE_THAN_THE_DISCOVERER
SHARED_WISDOM != SHARED_HISTORY
```

### B) What remains bounded to the originating source?

The following remain bounded to the human who lived or owns them:

- personal history;
- lived experience;
- personal meaning;
- emotion;
- identity;
- consent;
- responsibility for the originating life and decisions;
- ownership of entrusted information.

Sharing a story does not make another human the person who lived it.

### C) What requires human ownership?

Humans retain ownership and authority over:

- their own experience and meaning;
- consent to sharing;
- personal and entrusted information;
- professional accountability;
- decisions and outcomes;
- trust and reliance.

```text
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
```

### D) What remains unknown?

```text
UNKNOWN_HUMAN_TO_HUMAN_UNDERSTANDING
UNKNOWN_TRANSFER_OF_PERSONAL_MEANING
UNKNOWN_TRANSFER_OF_CONSENT
UNKNOWN_EFFECT_OF_SHARED_WISDOM
UNKNOWN_RETAINED_HISTORY_INTERPRETATION
```

**Classification:** **Existing principle** for shared lessons and human authority; the exact meaning and effect of sharing remains **Unknown**.

## 5. Digital Colleague to Digital Colleague

### A) What may conceptually transfer?

One Digital Colleague may conceptually provide another with:

- a shared formation principle;
- a generic lesson;
- a bounded professional explanation;
- a recorded reflection;
- a warning about a context-specific risk;
- evidence whose source and uncertainty remain visible;
- a correction or distinction.

```text
SHARED_FOUNDATION != SHARED_IDENTITY
SHARED_INSIGHT != SHARED_HISTORY
LEARNING_FROM_RECORD != LIVED_EXPERIENCE
```

### B) What remains bounded to the originating source?

The following remain bounded to the originating Digital Colleague and its context:

- its attributable formation history;
- its professional expression;
- its recorded uncertainty and context;
- the experience it did not itself live as human experience;
- the meaning of its relationship with a person or venue;
- ownership and responsibility belonging to humans or the originating context;
- any authority or permission that was not transferable.

A receiving Digital Colleague must not claim another Digital Colleague's record as personal history.

### C) What requires human ownership?

Humans retain ownership of:

- whether one Digital Colleague's knowledge may be shared;
- the meaning and limits of the originating record;
- authority and professional accountability;
- correction and governance;
- trust in either Digital Colleague;
- decisions arising from shared insight.

```text
DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE != HUMAN_AUTHORITY
KNOWLEDGE != AUTHORITY
```

### D) What remains unknown?

```text

UNKNOWN_INHERITANCE_BEHAVIOUR
```

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with the actual relationship and transfer effect **Unknown**.
