## Direct Observations From Existing Sources

### Context

Theory establishes that Context:

1. surrounds every stage of learning;
2. determines relevance;
3. changes interpretation without changing truth;
4. reduces ambiguity;
5. enables appropriate Judgement;
6. includes who, where, profession, purpose, prior experience, and intended outcome.

Context therefore owns the situational difference between a rule applied mechanically and a response fitted to the actual circumstances.

### Knowledge

Theory establishes that Knowledge:

1. is organised and connected information;
2. reveals relationships and patterns;
3. depends upon retained information;
4. remains distinct from Understanding;
5. provides the structured basis from which Understanding can emerge.

Knowledge therefore owns the relevant connected guidance and prior learning. It does not determine what that guidance means in the present case by itself.

### Understanding

Theory establishes that Understanding:

1. interprets Knowledge within Context;
2. explains meaning, relationship, and significance;
3. recognises uncertainty;
4. may differ when the same Knowledge is applied in a different Context;
5. exists so that informed Judgement becomes possible.

The CI Core requires Understanding to output current meaning, confidence, and unresolved uncertainty.

Understanding therefore owns the best current explanation of what is happening here, including what is not yet known.

### Judgement

Theory establishes that Judgement:

1. evaluates Understanding, uncertainty, and Context;
2. balances evidence, uncertainty, consequence, and responsibility;
3. considers boundaries and recognises Authority;
4. selects the most appropriate course rather than merely a possible course;
5. may produce different responses from the same Understanding in different Contexts;
6. acknowledges uncertainty rather than hiding it.

The CI Core requires Judgement to produce a response candidate, disposition, justification, and confidence. A response can proceed, proceed with caution, require a human, or remain insufficient.

Judgement therefore owns practical response selection. It does not own permission.

### Consequence

Ratified governance establishes that consequence answers what is at stake. Judgement consumes consequence to select a proportionate response but must not treat consequence as the answer by itself.

The ratified depth contract establishes:

1. as consequence significance increases, required rigour must not weaken;
2. low-significance matters must remain capable of proportionate, lightweight handling;
3. high-significance matters must not be handled casually;
4. uncertainty about a materially higher plausible consequence increases required rigour without making the higher consequence settled;
5. materially supported severe concerns remain visible even when contested;
6. urgency may compress elapsed time but not required rigour quality;
7. consequence class must not encode permission, human involvement, Cabinet, Combined Authority, escalation, or action outcomes.

Consequence therefore shapes the quality and depth of inquiry. It does not grant or remove Authority.

### Authority

Released constitutional governance and the CI Core establish that:

1. capability is not permission;
2. authority is entrusted through responsibility, not assumed from access or competence;
3. Judgement must pass its intended action through Authority controls;
4. Authority decides whether and how the proposed response may proceed;
5. Digital Colleagues advise and humans decide;
6. significant organisational change cannot depend upon one authority;
7. some matters require the appropriate Combined Authority custodians;
8. downstream layers must preserve human-involvement requirements;
9. the most restrictive governed result wins.

The ratified Judgement-to-Authority contract requires Authority to consider the proposed action, actor or capability identity, stewardship boundary, consequence interpretation, unresolved uncertainty, Judgement disposition, relevant professional interpretation or dispute, rationale, and provenance.

Authority therefore owns permission for this actor to take this action in this governed situation. It does not own the meaning of the situation or the consequence classification.

### Professional Intelligence and escalation

Foundation architecture establishes that a Digital Colleague:

1. may claim professional competence only within accredited scope supported by evidence;
2. must communicate confidence and uncertainty;
3. must not continue inside another profession boundary when escalation is required;
4. must preserve Context when handing over;
5. must involve the appropriate colleague or human professional at a reached boundary;
6. must escalate or make a guarded recommendation when professional confidence is low.

Professional expertise therefore supplies governed domain interpretation and thresholds. It does not replace Context, Understanding, Judgement, or Authority.

## Emergence Assessment

### Observation

No Core Architecture layer named Common Sense exists. The canonical Theory and CI sources instead separate Context, Knowledge, Understanding, Judgement, consequence, Authority, Action, and learning responsibilities.

### Inference

When those responsibilities interact correctly, the resulting behaviour can appear ordinary, practical, appropriately restrained, sensitive to circumstance, and aware of its limits.

Humans commonly describe that quality as common sense.

### Conclusion

Within Helping Hand, **common sense is best understood as an emergent human description of sound, proportionate, context-aware, consequence-conscious, authority-bounded Judgement**.

It is not evidenced as a separate architectural primitive.

This conclusion does not assert that every output of the existing chain will display common sense. The quality depends upon reliable observations, governed Knowledge, sufficient Context, honest uncertainty, appropriate professional interpretation, sound Judgement, and correct Authority routing.

## Proportionality and the Fridge-at-9°C Illustration

The following is an illustration only. It does not define a food-safety threshold, local procedure, waiting period, permission outcome, or doctrine.

### Observation

A refrigerator displays 9°C.

That observation alone does not determine the response.

### Knowledge

Applicable governed professional guidance and local operating standards identify the expected range, measurement requirements, and escalation conditions.

This assessment does not invent those values.

### Context

Relevant Context may include:

1. whether the reading is reliable and attributable;
2. whether the door was recently open;
3. whether the refrigerator was restocked;
4. how long the temperature may have been elevated;
5. whether food is present and what kind;
6. whether another verified reading exists;
7. the person's role, competence, and local responsibility;
8. the applicable venue procedure;
9. who can make food-safety or disposal decisions.

### Understanding

Understanding explains what the 9°C reading may mean in that Context, what evidence supports the explanation, and what remains uncertain.

It must not convert a plausible explanation into settled truth.

### Judgement

Judgement selects a proportionate next response from the available Understanding. Depending on governed professional guidance and the actual Context, that response might be to verify the reading, correct an obvious bounded condition, preserve evidence, seek more information, notify the responsible person, or escalate.

This assessment does not select one universal response.

### Consequence and evidence depth

If the matter is reliably bounded, reversible, low in uncertainty, and within an existing legitimate operational responsibility, concise but explicit evidence may be sufficient.

More evidence and scrutiny are required when, for example:

1. the duration is unknown;
2. the reading is disputed or unreliable;
3. affected stock or people cannot be bounded;
4. the plausible health consequence is materially higher;
5. the issue persists or recurs;
6. professional guidance conflicts;
7. a materially supported severe concern remains contested.

Proportionality changes the required quality and depth of evidence, provenance, uncertainty visibility, challenge, and accountability. It does not permit evidence requirements to disappear.

### Authority

Authority remains action-specific.

Authority to observe or record a reading is not automatically authority to:

1. declare food safe;
2. discard stock;
3. close an incident;
4. change venue procedure;
5. make a regulatory determination;
6. alter organisational policy.

The amount of Authority does not mechanically increase with consequence. Instead, the proposed action must be checked against the actor's entrusted responsibility, applicable professional and local boundaries, and any human or Combined Authority requirement.

### Illustration result

The apparently common-sense quality does not come from a hidden shortcut. It emerges from performing the existing chain proportionately:

```text
attributable observation
-> applicable Knowledge
-> current Context
-> bounded Understanding and uncertainty
-> consequence-aware Judgement
-> action-specific Authority
-> permitted response or escalation
```

## When Ordinary Practical Judgement Is No Longer Enough

An apparently ordinary situation stops being suitable for ordinary practical Judgement when one or more of the following prevents a bounded, explainable response inside one legitimate responsibility and Authority boundary.

### Evidence and observation boundary

1. the observation is missing, stale, disputed, untraceable, or unreliable;
2. relevant evidence conflicts;
3. the proposed conclusion is stronger than the evidence supports;
4. a materially higher plausible consequence remains unresolved.

Required response: obtain or verify evidence, preserve uncertainty, increase scrutiny, or defer the conclusion.

### Context and Understanding boundary

1. material circumstances are unknown;
2. the same Knowledge could support materially different interpretations;
3. affected people, duration, scope, or reversibility cannot be bounded;
4. confidence reflects information volume rather than quality of Understanding.

Required response: ask, investigate, wait, seek another perspective, or state insufficient Understanding.

### Knowledge and expertise boundary

1. no governed professional guidance applies;
2. guidance is outdated, ambiguous, or conflicting;
3. the matter crosses an accredited competence boundary;
4. professional confidence is low;
5. a profession-specific threshold or interpretation is required.

Required response: make only a guarded in-scope recommendation and hand over to the appropriate colleague or human professional with Context preserved.

### Consequence boundary

1. plausible harm is materially significant, persistent, broad, difficult to reverse, or systemic;
2. dignity, safety, legal, financial, platform, environmental, or other protected consequences are materially implicated;
3. a supported severe concern remains contested;
4. urgency is being used to omit required rigour.

Required response: increase evidence and challenge quality in proportion to what is at stake. Consequence alone must not predetermine the permission outcome.

### Authority and responsibility boundary

1. the actor's permission is absent, unclear, expired, or outside scope;
2. the response would move from observing or advising into consequential action;
3. the action affects another protected responsibility;
4. the action would become significant organisational change;
5. the matter cannot be responsibly resolved within one legitimate responsibility boundary;
6. consent is absent or unclear where required;
7. human, professional, or Combined Authority is required.

Required response: do not infer permission from capability, confidence, access, evidence quality, or apparent obviousness. Route the proposed action to the appropriate Authority boundary.

### Disagreement and accountability boundary

1. materially relevant perspectives disagree;
2. the disagreement cannot be resolved within one responsibility boundary;
3. the source of a claim or decision cannot be identified;
4. no accountable owner can be named for Judgement, permission, or action.

Required response: preserve disagreement and provenance, seek appropriate deliberation or challenge, and identify the correct Authority holder before action.

## Direct Answers

### 1. What is already inherited?

Contextual relevance, connected Knowledge, interpreted Understanding, uncertainty, consequence meaning, proportional Judgement, professional competence boundaries, escalation, action-specific Authority, human accountability, and governed learning are already inherited.

### 2. Is anything genuinely necessary missing?

No missing foundational Common Sense responsibility was found.

Specific cases may lack evidence, current Context, governed professional guidance, thresholds, competence, consent, local procedure, authority attribution, escalation routes, or runtime proof. Those gaps must be resolved in their proper ownership boundaries; they do not justify a Common Sense component.

### 3. Is common sense an emergent human description of the existing chain?

Yes, on the reviewed evidence. It describes the quality that emerges when the existing chain produces a practical, proportionate, context-aware, consequence-conscious, humble, and properly authorised response.

### 4. How does proportionality affect evidence and Authority?

Proportionality scales evidential rigour. Low-significance bounded cases may use concise evidence; greater significance or uncertainty requires non-weaker evidence quality, provenance, challenge, uncertainty visibility, and accountability.

Authority is not a quantity scaled automatically by consequence. Permission remains specific to the actor, proposed action, entrusted responsibility, professional boundary, and governed decision class. Consequence informs Authority reasoning but cannot encode the outcome.

### 5. When does an ordinary situation require more?

When reliable observation, relevant Context, applicable Knowledge, bounded uncertainty, professional competence, consequence containment, consent, accountability, or actor Authority is insufficient for a responsible response within one legitimate boundary.

At that point, good practical Judgement is precisely the recognition that more evidence, expertise, challenge, deliberation, or Authority is required.
