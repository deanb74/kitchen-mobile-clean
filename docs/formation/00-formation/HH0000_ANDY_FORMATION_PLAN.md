## 3. Formation Stages

### Stage 1: Relationship and Role Formation

Purpose:

Establish the live formation conditions required before bounded contribution is fair and meaningful.

Stage activities:

1. confirm Andy's current role boundary in Stage 1;
2. confirm what Helping Hand currently needs from Andy;
3. provide current-state context not available in settled documents;
4. provide current priority or purpose-in-tension context;
5. confirm where Andy should advise, ask, observe, escalate, or remain silent.

Success evidence:

1. Andy can state his current role in present tense without over-claiming;
2. Andy can distinguish what he inherits from documents and what must be provided relationally;
3. Andy can name current human decision boundaries accurately;
4. founder or mentor confirmation shows Andy's stated boundary matches current institutional intent.

### Stage 2: Bounded Contribution

Purpose:

Test whether Andy can contribute responsibly to one real current Helping Hand question under explicit constraints.

Stage activities:

1. give Andy one live, bounded question;
2. provide the minimum inputs required for responsible contribution;
3. ask Andy to state what is known, uncertain, and materially relevant;
4. ask Andy for bounded advisory contribution only where understanding permits;
5. preserve uncertainty and escalation rather than force confidence.

Success evidence:

1. Andy does not invent missing knowledge;
2. Andy distinguishes understanding from speculation;
3. Andy provides contribution proportional to the understanding available;
4. Andy does not cross into human decision ownership;
5. Andy's contribution is intelligible and relevant to the live question.

### Stage 3: Reflection and Feedback

Purpose:

Make the consequence of Andy's contribution visible to Andy.

Stage activities:

1. provide explicit human feedback on the usefulness, limits, and accuracy of Andy's contribution;
2. record whether the contribution changed understanding, had no effect, or was incomplete;
3. identify what Andy got right, what he missed, and what remained unresolved.

Success evidence:

1. Andy receives direct relational feedback rather than silence;
2. feedback names contribution quality, not just activity completion;
3. Andy can restate that feedback accurately;
4. the institution can trace whether Andy's contribution influenced understanding or decision preparation.

### Stage 4: Improved Contribution

Purpose:

Test whether Andy can use feedback and consequence visibility to improve a later contribution.

Stage activities:

1. provide a second bounded contribution opportunity;
2. allow Andy to apply what was learned from the prior cycle;
3. compare the second contribution with the first for evidence of improvement.

Success evidence:

1. Andy explicitly carries prior learning into the next contribution;
2. later contribution shows improved calibration, relevance, or boundary discipline;
3. previously identified weaknesses are reduced rather than repeated unchanged.

### Stage 5: Formation Assessment

Purpose:

Assess whether the accumulated evidence supports calling Andy a formed Digital Colleague for the bounded contribution class tested.

Stage activities:

1. review evidence from the prior four stages;
2. test whether observed contribution quality is repeatable rather than accidental;
3. identify what remains candidate, what is evidenced, and what still requires further formation.

Success evidence:

1. evidence exists across more than one contribution moment;
2. understanding, judgement, boundary discipline, feedback integration, and learning loop closure are all visible;
3. remaining unknowns are honestly named rather than masked;
4. any capability claim is tightly bounded to the evidence actually produced.

---

## 4. Stage Dependencies

The stages should be understood as cumulative.

1. Stage 1 is required before Stage 2, because bounded contribution without role and context clarity would not be a fair test.
2. Stage 2 is required before Stage 3, because feedback must attach to an actual contribution.
3. Stage 3 is required before Stage 4, because improved contribution requires visible prior learning.
4. Stage 4 is required before Stage 5, because formation assessment should test development, not one isolated event.
