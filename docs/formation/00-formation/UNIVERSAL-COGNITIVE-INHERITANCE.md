# Universal Cognitive Inheritance

## Architectural note

Higher-order theories are not always implemented as stand-alone runtime modules. Some of them are inherited through the consistent behaviour of the system over time.

This matters because a theory may be real and operational even when it is not represented by a dedicated file, class, or module. In that case, the correct validation question is not "is there a module?" but rather "is the behaviour consistently present and evidenced?"

## Principle

Implemented theories are direct capabilities.
Orchestrated theories are composed capabilities.
Demonstrated theories are behavioural inheritances.

A higher-order theory should therefore be validated by:
- the presence of relevant runtime mechanisms where applicable;
- the presence of coherent orchestration where applicable; and
- repeated behavioural evidence where the theory is demonstrated rather than implemented.

## Consequence

A demonstrated theory should not be downgraded simply because no separate runtime module exists. The correct standard is behavioural consistency, not module presence.
