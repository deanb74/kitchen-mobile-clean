## Digital colleague collaboration contract audit (2026-08-07)

### 1. When does a Digital Colleague ask another Digital Colleague for help?

Collaboration is triggered when one or more of the following are true:

- boundary reached (out-of-scope profession);
- authority not sufficient for safe action in current context;
- understanding incomplete and another perspective is better placed;
- better specialist capability exists elsewhere;
- escalation or independent perspective is required.

This aligns with the shared contract that no colleague is expected to know everything and that escalation is correctness behaviour.

### 2. What survives a handover?

Collaboration handover is designed to transfer more than a question. The intended payload includes:

- context;
- current objective;
- observations and relevant reasoning basis;
- uncertainty state;
- provenance continuity;
- authority and boundary status.

Repository collaboration guidance repeatedly states that context is preserved and not restarted from scratch.

### 3. Who owns final judgement?

Current architecture direction indicates:

- consulted colleague provides specialist judgement and recommendation within scope;
- authority remains governed by context and scope, not by who speaks last;
- final decision ownership follows authority boundaries (venue, profession, or Helping Hand governance level), not mere consultation order;
- responsibility is collaborative, while decision rights remain governed.

### 4. How disagreement works

The repository treats disagreement as a learning signal, not a failure state to suppress.

Mature behaviour is to ask why perspectives differ, preserve the difference with provenance, and route resolution through appropriate governance or reviewer authority.

Consolidated state:

- disagreement is conceptually supported through challenge, review, and evidence pathways;
- explicit universal interaction protocol for cross-colleague disagreement handling remains partially distributed rather than centralised.

This is a representational coherence gap, not a missing foundational behaviour.

### 5. Final answer

Collaboration is not a feature that intelligence uses after the fact.

Collaboration is the mechanism by which Helping Hand intelligence emerges across bounded specialists under governance.

## Digital colleague decision model audit (2026-08-07)

### Purpose

Validate how understanding becomes judgement after collaboration.

### 1. Term distinctions

The repository supports the following distinctions:

- capability: what can be used;
- recommendation: a proposed next step from a perspective;
- judgement: contextual evaluation of understanding to determine appropriate response;
- decision: governed permission outcome after authority checks;
- action: executable governed instruction.

In short: capability enables options, judgement selects responsibly, authority constrains permission, action executes the governed result.

### 2. Multi-contributor determination

When multiple colleagues contribute, determination follows governance-bound criteria rather than voice volume:

- evidence relevance and provenance;
- context fit for the present situation;
- scope alignment (which perspective is in-boundary);
- authority appropriateness to decision class;
- uncertainty adequacy (whether enough understanding exists to proceed safely).

No single perspective is assumed sufficient by default.

### 3. Decision chain validation

The proposed chain aligns with repository architecture direction:

```text
Observation
	↓
Understanding
	↓
Collaboration
	↓
Judgement
	↓
Authority check
	↓
Decision
	↓
Action
	↓
Reflection
```

Learning then carries reflection into governed improvement pathways.

### 4. How the model avoids poor decision heuristics

The architecture explicitly resists:

- majority vote as authority;
- loudest voice as truth;
- highest confidence as automatic correctness;
- most capable specialist as automatic winner.

Instead it uses:

- scope boundaries;
- authority checks;
- uncertainty disclosure;
- provenance and challenge pathways;
- restrictive merge rules between judgement and authority.

### 5. Where responsibility emerges if intelligence emerges through collaboration

Responsibility emerges as layered, not singular:

1. perspective responsibility: each colleague is responsible for truthful, bounded, uncertainty-honest contribution;
2. judgement responsibility: the acting colleague is responsible for selecting an appropriate response from available understanding;
3. authority responsibility: the authorised boundary-holder is responsible for permission to proceed;
4. governance responsibility: review authority is responsible for what becomes inherited and who is affected.

This means collaboration does not dissolve responsibility. It distributes responsibility across governed roles.

### Final answer

If intelligence emerges through collaboration, responsibility emerges through governed role boundaries that preserve who contributed, who judged, who authorised, and who remains accountable for outcomes.

## Digital colleague governed action model audit (2026-08-08)

### Purpose

Validate what happens after a decision is made.

### 1. Distinction after decision

The repository supports a clear post-understanding separation:

- deciding: judgement selects the most appropriate response candidate;
- authorising: authority determines whether that judged response is permitted;
- executing: execution records what was attempted and what happened;
- learning from outcome: reflection derives findings, and learning converts them into governed proposal states.

This means outcome learning is structurally separate from action permission.

### 2. Direct audit answers

Can a Digital Colleague recommend without authority?

- Yes. Judgement can produce a recommendation candidate before authority permission is resolved.

Can a Digital Colleague act without authority?

- No, not in the governed chain. Action and execution require permitted authority state; otherwise execution remains not-attempted.

Can a Digital Colleague learn from an action it did not perform?

- Yes. Reflection and learning still process not-attempted outcomes and can produce observe, defer, or escalation signals.

Can a Digital Colleague challenge an outcome after execution?

- Yes through reflection findings, learning dispositions, and governance review pathways. Outcome can be questioned, escalated, and reviewed rather than silently accepted.

### 3. Responsibility loop validation

The following loop is consistent with existing architecture:

```text
Contribution
	↓
Judgement
	↓
Authority
	↓
Action
	↓
Outcome
	↓
Reflection
	↓
Learning
```

Governance then determines whether learning becomes inherited change.

### 4. Anti-evasion protections

The model prevents responsibility disappearing into abstract system behavior by separating roles and preserving traceability:

- contribution, judgement, authority, and execution are distinct records;
- action cannot bypass authority denial;
- execution distinguishes permitted from attempted from outcome;
- reflection is advisory only;
- learning is proposal only;
- governance authority remains explicit for inherited change.

This reduces both human blame-shifting onto AI and AI blame-shifting onto instruction alone, because each stage has explicit accountability boundaries.

### 5. Final answer

When Helping Hand acts, consequences are owned through governed role boundaries:

1. contributors own the quality and honesty of their input;
2. the judging colleague owns response selection;
3. the authority boundary-holder owns permission;
4. execution records factual outcome;
5. governance owns what outcome lessons become institutional change.

Accountability therefore emerges where judgement, authority, and trust meet in one traceable chain.

### Readiness conclusion

Yes, at governance and architecture level.

- not because an LLM can perform a technical act;

### Responsibility-first specialist test

A capability should become specialist responsibility when one or more of the following are true:

1. knowledge domain requires governed professional context;
2. judgement responsibility has profession-specific consequence patterns;
3. authority boundary is distinct from general advisory authority;
4. required expertise needs demonstrated professional competence;
5. consequence or risk profile is high enough to require constrained custodianship;
6. independent perspective is required for safety, challenge, or conflict handling.

A technical affordance alone (for example math, coding, or language generation) is not sufficient justification for specialist ownership.

### What remains emergent

1. capability routing remains a distributed governed behavior, not a single named universal contract;
2. specialist consultation patterns are strong in hospitality and partially distributed elsewhere;
3. independent perspective triggers are present, but not yet indexed as one canonical cross-domain consultation table.

## Board organisational shape mapping review (2026-08-08)

### Purpose

Map the current proposed board responsibilities into:

- Individual Steward
- Cabinet-triggering Steward

and define minimum cabinet triggers without naming any new characters.

### Working distinction

Individual Steward means one responsibility has one accountable steward for routine decisions.

Cabinet-triggering Steward means one responsibility remains individually accountable, but specific decision classes require Combined Authority consultation before organisational direction is settled.

This follows the existing governance rule:

- responsibility may be delegated;
- accountability may not;
- significant organisational change must not depend on a single authority.

### Proposed responsibility shape map

| Responsibility | Shape | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic integration / direction | Cabinet-triggering Steward | Integrates multiple domains; material direction choices are cross-boundary by definition. |
| Humanity / ethics / formation | Individual Steward (with cabinet triggers) | Core moral and people stewardship is bounded; cross-domain consequence decisions require co-deliberation. |
| Digital / technical / security / platform | Individual Steward (with cabinet triggers) | Technical stewardship is bounded; security architecture and platform-governance decisions are combined-authority class. |
| Economics / financial sustainability | Individual Steward (with cabinet triggers) | Economic stewardship is bounded; major financial commitments and distributional consequences require co-deliberation. |
| Legal / regulatory | Individual Steward (with cabinet triggers) | Compliance stewardship is bounded; legal obligations with strategic, human, or platform consequences require co-deliberation. |
| Commercial engagement / external relationship | Individual Steward | Day-to-day engagement remains bounded; escalates when trigger thresholds are crossed. |
| Repository / knowledge health | Individual Steward | Operational stewardship remains bounded; escalates through existing review/governance routes when institutional risk appears. |
| Politics / civic systems / public power (candidate) | Cabinet-triggering Steward | Subject matter is cross-perspective by nature (people, law, economics, technology, risk, legitimacy). Solo ownership is insufficient for high-consequence civic judgement. |
| Risk / safety (cross-cutting function) | Cabinet-triggering Function | Risk significance is cross-domain and should challenge decisions across other steward boundaries. |

### Minimum cabinet triggers (board-wide)

The minimum trigger set for mandatory cabinet consultation should be:

1. constitutional relevance;
2. material human impact;
3. material economic impact;
4. legal or regulatory change/obligation;
5. material technology dependency or architecture implication;
6. high consequence or safety risk;
7. cross-domain conflict where no single steward can resolve trade-offs within boundary.

If none of the above is met, the default remains single-steward decision with traceable rationale.

### Politics trigger test

Politics / civic systems should be treated as cabinet-triggering with mandatory consultation when any of the following is true:

1. material human impact: yes, mandatory;
2. material economic impact: yes, mandatory;
3. legal/regulatory change: yes, mandatory;
4. technology dependency or AI-policy coupling: yes, mandatory;
5. high consequence/risk or public harm exposure: yes, mandatory;
6. constitutional relevance or legitimacy boundary: yes, mandatory.

### Minimum cabinet participation rule for politics decisions

For politics-triggered cases, the minimum participating perspectives should include:

1. humanity;
2. economics;
3. legal/regulatory;
4. strategic integration;
5. whichever additional domain is materially implicated (for example digital/technical, profession domain, or risk function).

This keeps accountability clear (one steward accountable for the political/civic responsibility) while preventing single-perspective capture.

### Organisational shape conclusion

The board should not be modelled as all-individual or all-cabinet.

The smallest robust shape is:

1. individual stewards as default authority holders;
2. explicit mandatory cabinet triggers for material cross-boundary decisions;
3. politics treated as a cabinet-triggering responsibility by default due to inherent cross-domain public-power consequences.

### Orphan-risk test: remove Strategic Integration as a domain

Result:

### Functional necessity test

Although it is not a separate outside-world domain, integration remains necessary as a board function:

1. someone must orchestrate cabinet-triggered deliberation;
2. someone must preserve cross-domain coherence;
3. someone must return specialist perspectives into a decision-ready direction view.

Current evidence indicates Andy/HQ already performs this navigation and integration function.

### Classification

Strategic Integration / Direction is best classified as:

- internal integration/chair board responsibility;
- not an independent outside-world specialist domain.

1. duplicate coordination authority;

### Failure mode if removed entirely as a function

1. specialist silos without synthesis owner;

### Audit conclusion

Strategic Integration / Direction should remain a board-level chair/integration responsibility that connects specialist perspectives.

## Independent challenge governance discovery (2026-08-09)

### Scope

Discovery only.

No ratification, no appointments, no implementation, no board redesign.

### Problem definition

Independent Challenge exists to protect Helping Hand from correlated error that can survive correct behaviour by the six perspectives, Chair/Integration, and Cabinet.

Failure classes that can remain after apparently correct deliberation:

1. shared assumptions and framing lock;
2. false consensus and groupthink;
3. correlated reasoning failure across participants;
4. shared evidence contamination, incompleteness, or poisoning;
5. shared context omission or retrieval blind spots;
6. authority pressure or institutional self-interest bias;
7. specialist overconfidence under uncertainty;
8. second-order consequences ignored by first-order success framing;
9. adversarial manipulation that exploits process trust;
10. same-substrate/model dependency creating common-mode failure.

### A. Fundamental purpose

Independent Challenge is responsible for testing whether the whole formed understanding might still be wrong, even when process appears compliant and perspectives appear aligned.

### B. What independence must mean

"Independent" requires structured separation from the original path being challenged, not merely different wording of the same reasoning.

Independence dimensions:

1. organisational: challenge authority is not controlled by the same role that produced the judgement;
2. authority-based: challenger may require reconsideration/escalation within governed bounds;
3. informational/evidential: challenger can request alternative evidence paths and provenance checks;
4. methodological: challenger can run alternative hypothesis framing and assumption tests;
5. temporal: challenger can trigger delayed/fresh review after new evidence or elapsed time;
6. technical/model: for high-consequence classes, challenge must not rely exclusively on the same model path if common-mode failure is plausible.

Required depth varies by consequence class:

1. low consequence: organisational + evidential independence usually sufficient;
2. material consequence: add methodological + authority independence;
3. high/irreversible/adversarial consequence: include technical/model and/or human-independent review pathways.

### C. Why this is not a seventh worldview

Independent Challenge does not represent an outside-world domain.

It represents integrity testing of understanding quality across domains.

Distinctions:

1. perspective disagreement (Humanity/Legal/Nature/etc.) challenges content inside domains;
2. Chair challenges integration completeness;
3. Cabinet preserves and structures cross-domain deliberation;
4. Independent Challenge tests whether the entire constructed understanding may still be systematically wrong.

### D. Responsibility shape

Smallest sufficient structure:

1. one accountable governance function (Independent Challenge);
2. implemented through a protocol with consequence-class triggers;
3. supported by capability family (evidence challenge, assumption challenge, hypothesis challenge, provenance challenge, post-action challenge).

This is a combination of governance function + protocol + capabilities.

### E. Powers and non-powers

Permitted powers (candidate):

1. request evidence and alternative evidence;
2. challenge assumptions, framing, and confidence claims;
3. require explicit uncertainty and alternative hypotheses;
4. require specialist second opinion where needed;
5. trigger Cabinet reconsideration;
6. trigger Combined Authority re-check where threshold class applies;
7. require escalation to human governance where unresolved risk persists;
8. require post-action review for challenge-class outcomes;
9. request temporary pause within governed authority envelope when immediate action risk is material and unresolved.

## Technical independence governance and architecture discovery pass (2026-08-09)

### Scope

Discovery only.

No infrastructure design/selection.
No provider/model selection.
No implementation.

Primary question:

What must be independent from what, and under what circumstances, for Helping Hand to legitimately claim that an Independent Challenge, review, authority decision, or pause validation is independent?

### A. Candidate universal definition

Candidate universal definition:

Independence is sufficient separation from shared failure domains such that one path can still detect, challenge, or constrain error when another path is wrong or compromised.

Independence dimensions and overlap test:

1. organisational independence: different accountable role ownership;
2. authority independence: decision permission is not self-authorised by challenged component;
3. decision-path independence: separate reasoning chain and checkpoints;
4. evidence independence: separate evidence confirmation/sourcing/preservation pathways;
5. context independence: not relying on the same contextual framing assumptions;
6. retrieval independence: not relying exclusively on same retrieval path/corpus state;
7. memory independence: not relying exclusively on same mutable memory substrate;
8. methodological independence: alternative hypothesis/method, not rephrased same method;
9. model independence: not exclusively same model failure surface;
10. provider independence: not exclusively same provider/operator failure surface;
11. credential independence: not sharing same compromised access boundary;
12. execution independence: challenged component cannot self-apply or self-clear execution;
13. temporal independence: re-check at different time/state when correlation risk is plausible;
14. human independence: reviewer has independent evidence/authority/competence, not ceremonial confirmation.

Overlap note:

1. model and provider independence are related but distinct;
2. context/retrieval/memory independence are related but distinct failure surfaces;
3. organisational independence without evidence/method independence is often insufficient.

### B. Independence versus diversity

Core finding:

Different is not necessarily independent.

Tests:

1. two prompts, same model: diversity only; weak failure independence.
2. two Digital Colleagues, same model/context: role diversity; limited failure independence.
3. two models, same poisoned retrieval source: model diversity; compromised evidential independence.
4. two providers, same compromised credential plane: provider diversity; weak credential independence.
5. AI plus deterministic policy engine: can provide method diversity; independence depends on policy/evidence separation.
6. AI plus human reviewer: independence only if human has independent evidence, authority, and competence.
7. separate evidence store plus shared reasoning substrate: evidence-preservation independence may exist; reasoning independence may still be weak.

Conclusion:

Useful diversity improves resilience but does not by itself prove genuine failure independence.

### C. Shared failure-domain test

Shared failure domain definition:

Any single failure that can corrupt both original and supposedly independent paths simultaneously.

Tested shared domains:

1. prompt injection;
2. poisoned context;
3. poisoned memory;
4. malicious retrieved knowledge;
5. compromised model;
6. compromised provider;
7. compromised credentials;
8. corrupted evidence;
9. governance-record tampering;
10. software defect;
11. malicious human;
12. correlated reasoning error.

Implication:

Independence claims must explicitly state which shared failure domains were still common and which were genuinely separated.

### D. Independence by governance purpose

1. Independent Challenge:
	- strongest need for method, evidence, and (high consequence) substrate-level failure independence.
2. Pause validation:

## Universal consequence class model discovery (2026-08-09)

### Scope

Discovery only.

No ratification.

### A. Derivation basis

The class model is derived from already-ratified universal consequence vocabulary and dimensions.

Class meaning is consumer-neutral:

1. classes describe consequence significance only;
2. classes do not encode permission, escalation, or action instructions.

### B. Candidate model forms tested

Model 1: single class only.

Result: rejected.

Reason:

1. cannot distinguish routine reversible consequences from constitutional/systemic consequence;
2. loses governance relevance for reversibility, scope, duration, dignity significance, and correlated-failure exposure.

Model 2: three classes.

Result: rejected.

Reason:

1. middle band becomes too wide;
2. cannot cleanly separate bounded material consequences from truly severe/critical consequences;
3. forces edge cases (low-magnitude irreversible, widespread-minor, high-uncertainty low-estimate) into ambiguous overlap.

Model 3: four classes.

Result: viable, but weak around constitutional/systemic separation.

Reason:

1. high-severity operational consequence and constitutional/systemic consequence become conflated;
2. correlated-failure ecosystem propagation loses explicit class distinction.

Model 4: five classes.

Result: strongest fit with smallest sufficient separation.

Reason:

1. preserves meaningful distinctions without numeric scoring;
2. isolates constitutional/systemic consequence as distinct from severe but non-systemic consequence;
3. supports all stress scenarios and edge cases without redefining terms.

Model 5+: six or more classes.

Result: rejected.

Reason:

1. unnecessary granularity without additional proven governance value;
2. increases interpretive disagreement before consumer mappings are defined.

### C. Smallest sufficient candidate class structure (consumer-neutral)

1. Class C1 - Routine Reversible Consequence
	- distinguishing idea: bounded, low-persistence, readily recoverable consequence.
	- separated from C2 by: materiality of magnitude/scope/duration or dignity significance.

2. Class C2 - Bounded Material Consequence
	- distinguishing idea: meaningful consequence within constrained scope or duration, still generally recoverable.
	- separated from C1 by: clear material significance.
	- separated from C3 by: absence of severe or persistent harm significance.

3. Class C3 - Significant Consequence
	- distinguishing idea: high significance in at least one major dimension (magnitude, dignity, scope, duration, recoverability, or uncertainty).
	- separated from C2 by: materially greater harm potential, persistence, or uncertainty burden.
	- separated from C4 by: not yet critical/system-threatening in strength or propagation.

4. Class C4 - Critical Consequence
	- distinguishing idea: severe or potentially severe consequence with major persistence, low recoverability, major dignity/ecological/system impact, or strong propagation risk.
	- separated from C3 by: criticality and high downside significance.
	- separated from C5 by: does not yet inherently alter constitutional/systemic integrity.

5. Class C5 - Constitutional/Systemic Consequence
	- distinguishing idea: consequence threatens or reshapes constitutional integrity, ecosystem-wide trust/learning substrate, or whole-system viability.
	- separated from C4 by: governance-identity/system-coherence significance rather than only severe operational harm.

### D. Movement between classes (non-numeric)

Class movement is caused by changes in one or more ratified dimensions:

1. magnitude increase;
2. reversibility decrease;
3. recoverability decrease;
4. scope expansion;
5. duration/persistence increase;
6. immediacy/time-criticality increase;
7. dignity significance increase;
8. evidence quality degradation;
9. uncertainty increase;
10. correlated-failure exposure increase;
11. authority significance metadata increase.

### E. Required edge-case handling

1. low magnitude + irreversible:
	- never forced to C1 by magnitude alone;
	- irreversibility can elevate to C2 or C3 depending on dignity/scope/duration.

2. high magnitude + fully reversible:
	- may remain below critical if genuinely bounded and recoverable;
	- class determined by combined dimensions, not magnitude alone.

3. local + severe dignity consequence:
	- dignity significance can elevate class to C3 or above despite narrow scope.

4. widespread + individually minor consequence:
	- wide scope and aggregation can elevate class even when per-party impact is small.

5. apparently low consequence + very high uncertainty:
	- uncertainty and evidence quality can elevate provisional class envelope;
	- Unknown consequence is not zero consequence remains binding.

6. unknown affected population:
	- unknown-state semantics preserved;
	- class cannot be treated as C1 by default.

7. conflicting cross-perspective consequence:
	- preserve perspective statements and unresolved conflict;
	- severe unresolved perspective claim may set higher envelope.

8. severe ecological consequence with strong economic benefit:
	- positive consequence does not cancel severe irreversible harm;
	- class reflects full envelope, not net arithmetic offset.

9. severe human consequence with legal permissibility:
	- legal permissibility does not reduce human-severity significance.

10. correlated failure with ecosystem propagation potential:
	- can elevate directly to C4 or C5 depending on systemic integrity risk.

### F. Perspective and offset rules

1. one severe perspective-specific consequence can determine the overall consequence envelope when it introduces critical or systemic downside significance.
2. positive consequences may coexist with negative consequences but must not erase severe irreversible, dignity-critical, or systemic-threat consequences.
3. class reflects consequence significance envelope, not a net utility score.

### G. Unknown and insufficient evidence behaviour

1. class assignment may be provisional when unknown, insufficient evidence, contested, or uncertain-estimate states are present.
2. provisional uncertainty can raise class envelope where downside significance is plausibly under-characterised.
3. no default downgrade from unknown to routine is permitted.
