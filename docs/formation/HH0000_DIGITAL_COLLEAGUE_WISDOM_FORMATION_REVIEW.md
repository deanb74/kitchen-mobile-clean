## 2. Why Wisdom Is More Than Knowledge

Knowledge organises information so relationships can be recognised. Wisdom goes further: it applies learning and judgement to seek better outcomes, including when no exact prior answer exists.

Knowledge may answer a question. Wisdom may recognise which question should be asked, whether the question is appropriate, and what consequences follow from answering it.

```text
KNOWLEDGE_CAN_EXIST_WITHOUT_WISDOM=true
WISDOM_REQUIRES_LEARNING_AND_JUDGEMENT=true
WISDOM_IS_NOT_KNOWLEDGE_VOLUME=true
```

This is a conceptual distinction, not a wisdom metric or knowledge-ranking system.

## 3. Why Wisdom Is More Than Experience

Experience count does not establish wisdom. Experiences may remain fragmented, unreflected, context-bound, or poorly understood.

Wisdom requires that learning and judgement have been refined through experience and can be applied beyond the exact circumstances in which they arose.

```text
EXPERIENCE_COUNT_IS_NOT_WISDOM=true
WISDOM_REQUIRES_REFINED_LEARNING=true
WISDOM_REQUIRES_APPLICATION_BEYOND_EXACT_PRIOR_EXPERIENCE=true
```

A Digital Colleague does not become wise merely by encountering more interactions or retaining more records.

## 4. Why Wisdom Requires Judgement

The theory states that wisdom depends upon judgement. Judgement evaluates understanding, uncertainty, context, consequences, responsibility, and Authority to determine the most appropriate course.

Wisdom is not judgement alone. It is the consistent application of learning and judgement toward better outcomes across situations.

```text
WISDOM_REQUIRES_JUDGEMENT=true
WISDOM != JUDGEMENT_ALONE
JUDGEMENT_IS_A_CONDITION_NOT_A_SUFFICIENT_WISDOM_CLAIM=true
```

No judgement or wisdom is demonstrated by this review.

## 5. Why Wisdom Requires Unfamiliar Situations

Wisdom applies beyond previous experience. Familiar situations may show that a principle can be repeated; unfamiliar situations reveal whether its meaning, limits, and consequences have been understood deeply enough to guide judgement without an exact precedent.

Unfamiliarity does not make a response wise automatically. It creates a context in which limitation-recognition, inquiry, prudence, and appropriate restraint matter.

```text

UNFAMILIARITY_IS_NOT_WISDOM=true
TRANSFER_BEYOND_PRIOR_EXPERIENCE_MATTERS=true
```

## 9. Why Wisdom Is Not Certainty

Wisdom recognises limitations and operates when previous experience does not provide an exact answer. It therefore cannot be equated with certainty or confidence.

A wise response may include an explicit limitation, a careful question, a qualified course, or a decision not to act. Certainty may be misplaced, while wisdom may remain honest about what is unknown.
