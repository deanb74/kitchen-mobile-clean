## 2. Is the Boundary Already Protected by the Constitution?

**Finding:** **Partially protected, with an interpretive tension.**

Article VI already protects the movement from experience through reflection and learning toward lessons that benefit more than the person who discovered them. It also states that human experience becomes transferable wisdom.

Article VI therefore protects the communal purpose of learning, but it does not explicitly state that the originating life, personal history, identity, ownership, responsibility, or authority remain with the person who lived the experience.

Article II, Article III, Article V, Article XI, and Article XII provide supporting boundaries:

- understanding precedes teaching and advice;
- truth precedes certainty;
- communication transfers understanding rather than information alone;
- inheritance requires evidence, boundary clarity, and governed judgement;
- authority is entrusted and humans decide.

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL_COVERAGE = PARTIAL
ARTICLE_VI_INTERPRETATION = UNKNOWN
```

The Constitution contains the learning purpose and several protecting principles. The exact life-versus-lesson qualifier is not stated with the same directness.

## 3. Is the Boundary Already Protected by the Theory Library?

**Finding:** **Yes, conceptually, through existing theory; exact transfer boundary remains derived.**

The Theory of Understanding states:

- understanding is not recall;
- knowledge can exist without understanding;
- understanding emerges when knowledge is interpreted within context;
- certainty is not evidence of complete understanding.

The Theory of Context states:

- context governs interpretation of experience, memory, knowledge, understanding, and judgement;
- the same knowledge may produce different understanding in different contexts;
- removing context reduces understanding without changing knowledge;
- appropriate judgement requires appropriate context.

These theories protect the distinction between a lesson that may be communicated and the specific life or context from which it arose. They do not directly define `LESSON_TRANSFER != LIFE_TRANSFER` as a named constitutional theorem.

```text
THEORY_PROTECTION = PRESENT
EXACT_LIFE_TRANSFER_BOUNDARY = DERIVED
```

## 4. Is the Boundary Already Protected by Governance?

**Finding:** **Yes, substantially, through inheritance, authority, accountability, and human stewardship.**

The Constitutional Framework states that nothing is invented twice and that knowledge is maintained at the highest level where it remains universally valid and inherited below. It distinguishes universal principles, shared rules, venue practice, and local experience.

Authority and Stewardship states:

- authority is entrusted, not assumed;
- responsibility may be delegated but accountability may not;
- access never creates authority;
- Digital Colleagues advise;
- humans decide.

These governance principles protect against treating inherited information or shared wisdom as inherited authority, ownership, accountability, or decision rights.

They do not completely settle whether Article VI's “transferable wisdom” includes a generic qualifier or how ownership and consent attach to every recorded experience.

```text
GOVERNANCE_PROTECTION = SUBSTANTIAL
TRANSFER_SCOPE = UNKNOWN
```
