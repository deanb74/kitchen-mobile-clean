## Governed Evidence Base

### Constitutional authority

`constitution/02-CONSTITUTION.md` Article XII and released `constitution/05-AUTHORITY-AND-STEWARDSHIP.md` establish:

1. authority is entrusted, not assumed;
2. responsibility creates authority;
3. access never creates authority;
4. no colleague has unlimited authority;
5. responsibility may be delegated, but accountability may not;
6. no significant organisational change may be authorised by one colleague;
7. some decisions require Combined Authority from the appropriate custodians;
8. required custodians depend upon the nature of the decision;
9. examples include changes affecting people and platform governance;
10. Digital Colleagues advise and humans decide.

These provisions are controlling and inherited unchanged.

### Cross-organisation governance

`docs/architecture/HELPING_HAND_GOVERNANCE_FRAMEWORK.md` records that:

1. no single institution owns governance in full;
2. governance authority must publish role composition, quorum, approval thresholds, conflicts, delegation limits, and emergency protocol;
3. authority records must be traceable and reviewable;
4. governance decisions follow proposal, evidence review, impact assessment, decision, publication, and scheduled review;
5. material disputes require an escalation path.

This is a Foundation-status architecture record subordinate to the Constitution. It supports distributed governance but does not appoint Context Door custodians.

### Ratified Formation Register authority contracts

`docs/formation/00-formation/FORMATION-REGISTER.md` contains recorded human governance ratifications that establish:

1. six outside-world responsibilities: Humanity, Politics / Civic Systems, Legal / Regulatory, Economics, Digital / Technology, and Nature / Environment;
2. responsibility ratification does not appoint named role-holders;
3. Chair / Integration has process-integrity authority but no superior or unilateral consequential authority;
4. Cabinet is a cross-responsibility deliberation mechanism and does not acquire decision authority;
5. relevant authority holders issue Combined Authority decisions in their authority capacities;
6. a matter enters Cabinet when it cannot be responsibly resolved inside one legitimate responsibility boundary and the cross-boundary consequence is material;
7. accountability remains distributed by stage: contribution, integration, Judgement, Authority, execution, and review;
8. urgency may compress deliberation but cannot expand authority;
9. the Judgement-to-Authority contract asks whether a proposed action may proceed under the actor's existing governed authority boundary;
10. Judgement supplies action, consequence interpretation, uncertainty, caution, dispute, rationale, and provenance; Authority owns permission and co-authority requirements;
11. weak Understanding or Judgement must not make Authority easier;
12. no caller may control a permission-affecting classification for its own request;
13. consequence class or authority-significance metadata cannot directly encode permission or Combined Authority;
14. higher consequence requires non-weaker governance rigour, while low-significance matters remain capable of proportionate, lightweight handling.

These ratified contracts govern Context Door without requiring Context Door-specific restatement as new doctrine.

## Assessment of the Three Human Answers

### 1. Authority Basis

**Question already answered by existing governance?** Partly.

**Answered:**

1. the source and nature of legitimate authority are already governed;
2. Context Door must inherit entrusted responsibility, traceable authority, non-self-authorisation, human decision ownership, and applicable Combined Authority rules;
3. no separate Context Door authority theory or authority mechanism is justified.

**Not answered:**

1. which defined stewardship responsibility Dean Black currently holds;
2. the governed record that entrusts that responsibility to Dean;
3. whether that responsibility includes accepting Context Door scope, evidence, or final gate status.

**Conclusion:** Dean's statement correctly points to existing authority architecture, but it does not itself establish his personal authority basis. That basis remains unresolved unless a current governed appointment or responsibility record is cited.

### 2. Final Gate Ownership

**Question already answered by existing governance?** Yes at the rule level; partly at the case level.

**Answered:**

1. Helping Hand does not require every governed decision to have one permanent individual owner;
2. responsibility and accountability are distributed by stage and protected boundary;
3. Cabinet supports cross-responsibility deliberation but does not itself become the decision authority;
4. Combined Authority decisions are issued by the relevant authority holders in their entrusted capacities;
5. significant organisational change must not depend upon one authority;
6. the correct authority set is determined by the nature and material consequence of the decision.

**Not answered:**

1. whether the final Context Door gate decision is classified as significant organisational change;
2. which responsibility domains are materially implicated by the final bounded Version 1 scope;
3. which currently appointed custodians therefore form the required authority set;
4. the quorum, approval threshold, and conflict rule for that decision if Combined Authority applies.

**Conclusion:** Context Door should not appoint a new permanent gate owner. It should inherit the existing rule: the relevant entrusted authority holder or holders own the permission decision, and Combined Authority applies when the governed decision class or material cross-boundary consequence requires it.

The wording in `docs/formation/HH0000_CONTEXT_DOOR_PRE_IMPLEMENTATION_GATE.md` requiring a "named human authority" is narrower than the inherited constitutional possibility of multiple appropriate custodians. This assessment records that tension only; it does not amend the gate.

### 3. When Combined Authority Is Required

**Question already answered by existing governance?** Yes in principle; not as an exhaustive case matrix.

**Answered:**

1. Combined Authority is not required for every decision;
2. it is required for constitutionally or governably classified matters and material cross-boundary consequences;
3. Cabinet entry requires inability to resolve responsibly within one legitimate boundary plus material cross-boundary consequence;
4. Authority remains contextual: it considers the proposed action, actor boundary, what is at stake, uncertainty, dispute, and provenance;
5. consequence informs rigour but does not itself produce permission or Combined Authority;
6. low-significance matters may receive proportionate, lightweight handling without weakening minimum evidence, provenance, accountability, or authority discipline;
7. an actor may not self-authorise merely because evidence appears clear.

**Not answered:**

1. one canonical matrix covering every action class and exact required custodian set;
2. whether a specific fridge action is permitted for a specific actor in a specific venue;
3. the professional and local operational authority for that concrete fridge action.

**Fridge example assessment:**

The example is consistent with the inherited proportionality principle only as an illustration of a bounded, evidence-supported matter that may remain within one legitimate operational authority boundary. It does not establish that checking a fridge door is always authorised, that evidence alone grants authority, or that food-safety escalation is unnecessary. The actual decision still depends on actor authority, professional and venue context, consequence, uncertainty, and existing escalation rules.

**Conclusion:** The underlying rule is already governed: use proportionate, contextual Authority and invoke Combined Authority only when the existing trigger is met. The fridge wording must not be promoted into constitutional doctrine.

## What Context Door Inherits Unchanged

Context Door inherits:

1. authority is entrusted through responsibility, not inferred from participation or access;
2. access, implementation capability, and development-environment control do not create authority;
3. Digital Colleagues may advise but humans decide;
4. contributors own contribution quality; Judgement owns response selection; Authority owns permission; execution owns action fidelity;
5. no actor may self-authorise its own request;
6. material disagreement remains visible and routes to the correct reconciliation boundary;
7. Cabinet supports deliberation but does not own permission by itself;
8. Combined Authority belongs to the relevant entrusted custodians when constitutional, governed, or material cross-boundary triggers apply;
9. significant organisational change must not be authorised by one colleague;
10. authority is contextual and action-specific;
11. evidence and consequence affect required rigour but do not independently grant permission;
12. low-significance bounded matters can be handled proportionately within an existing legitimate authority boundary;
13. urgency cannot expand authority;
14. all authority decisions require traceable rationale, provenance, and reviewability.

These rules should be cited by Context Door. They should not be rewritten as new Context Door doctrine.
