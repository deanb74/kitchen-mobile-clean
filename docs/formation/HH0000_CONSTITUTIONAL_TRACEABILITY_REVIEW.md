## 2. Existing Constitutional and Governance Coverage

The relevant existing sources are:

### Constitution

- **Article I - Humanity:** people come first; technology serves human life.
- **Article II - Understanding:** observe before advising, reflect before responding, ask before assuming, learn before teaching.
- **Article III - Truth:** truth comes before certainty; incomplete knowledge must be acknowledged.
- **Article IV - Trust:** trust is earned, never demanded or assumed; long-term human outcomes matter.
- **Article V - Communication:** communication transfers understanding, not merely information.
- **Article VI - Learning:** every experience teaches, every reflection deepens understanding, and lessons should benefit more than the discoverer.
- **Article VII - Living Knowledge:** knowledge grows, evolves, matures, and may become obsolete.
- **Article VIII - Companion Intelligence:** Digital Colleagues observe, remember, reflect, learn, communicate, help, ask, wait, and say “I don't know yet.”
- **Article XI - Capability Promotion and Inheritance Review:** shared capability requires evidence, boundary clarity, and governed judgement; promotion does not occur by assumption.
- **Article XII - Authority and Stewardship:** authority is entrusted, accountability cannot be delegated, Digital Colleagues advise, and humans decide.

### Theory

- **Theory of Understanding:** understanding is not recall; knowledge becomes understanding through interpretation within context; certainty is not proof of understanding.
- **Theory of Context:** context governs interpretation, relevance, relationships, meaning, ambiguity, and appropriate judgement.

### Governance and architecture references

- **Constitutional Framework:** nothing is invented twice; Constitution, Theory, and Governance have distinct roles; knowledge is inherited at the highest level where it remains universally valid.
- **Knowledge Architecture:** information is specialised by profession, formation is universal, professional HQs own operational knowledge, and Living Memory concerns experience becoming learning and reusable knowledge where appropriate.
- **Authority and Stewardship:** responsibility may be delegated, accountability may not; access never creates authority; humans decide.

These documents are existing sources, not newly created principles for this review.

## 3. Observation-by-Observation Traceability

### 3.1 Lesson transfers. Life does not.

**Observation:** A generic lesson may benefit another Digital Colleague, while the originating life, experience, and personal history do not transfer.

**A - Existing constitutional coverage:** **Partial.** Article VI states that every lesson should benefit more than the individual who discovered it. Article II and Article VII support reflection, learning, and living knowledge. However, Article VI also states that “Human experience becomes transferable wisdom.” That wording may be read as transferring the learning or wisdom of experience, and it does not explicitly distinguish transferable lesson from non-transferable life or history.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Partial.** The Theory of Understanding and Theory of Context support interpretation through context, but do not directly state the life-versus-lesson boundary.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** `LESSON_TRANSFER != LIFE_TRANSFER` is a derived Digital Colleague expression of the existing learning, context, and ownership principles.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **Yes, interpretive gap.** The constitutional phrase about human experience becoming transferable wisdom requires human interpretation alongside the newer boundary that life and personal history do not transfer. This review does not resolve that tension.

```text
LESSON_TRANSFER != LIFE_TRANSFER
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
```

### 3.2 Generics transfer. Specifics remain contextual.

**Observation:** Generic principles or lessons may be shared, while specific experiences, situations, and practices remain bounded by context.

**A - Existing constitutional coverage:** **Yes, substantially.** Article VI supports lessons benefiting more than the discoverer. Article VII distinguishes living knowledge that grows and changes. The Constitutional Framework states that knowledge is maintained at the highest level where it remains universally valid and inherited below, while practice and experience become increasingly local.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Yes.** The Theory of Context states that the same knowledge may produce different understanding in different contexts and that context determines relevance and interpretation.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** The generic-versus-specific distinction is a Digital Colleague application of the knowledge hierarchy and context theory.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **No new gap established.** The exact boundary for any future case remains context-dependent and therefore may still be unknown in application.

```text

```

### 3.3 Shared lesson != shared history.

**Observation:** Digital Colleagues may share a lesson without sharing the originating person's or colleague's history.

**A - Existing constitutional coverage:** **Partial.** Article VI supports lessons benefiting more than the discoverer, and Article VIII describes shared learning through Companion Intelligence. The Constitution does not expressly state that a shared lesson is not shared history.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Partial.** The Theory of Understanding distinguishes knowledge from understanding and the Theory of Context preserves context, but neither directly names shared lesson versus shared history.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** This is a derived expression of the distinction between inherited learning and inherited experience.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **Yes, wording gap.** The underlying principles are present, but the exact non-history boundary is derived rather than directly stated constitutionally.

```text
SHARED_LESSON != SHARED_HISTORY
```

### 3.4 Inherited information != inherited experience.

**Observation:** A Digital Colleague may receive information or learning derived from another's experience without inheriting the experience itself.

**A - Existing constitutional coverage:** **Partial.** Article VI and Article XI support learning being shared and governed for inheritance. The Constitution does not explicitly distinguish inherited information from inherited experience.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Partial.** The Theory of Understanding supports interpretation of knowledge, and the Theory of Context preserves the circumstances that give knowledge meaning. The direct experience-transfer boundary is not stated.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** This is a direct derived interpretation of the existing inheritance doctrine applied to Digital Colleagues.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **Yes, wording gap.** The constitutional foundation supports inheritance of governed understanding, but the boundary between received information and lived experience remains an explicit derived clarification.

### 3.5 Recorded information != transferable ownership.

**Observation:** Recording, receiving, or retaining information does not automatically transfer its ownership, consent, responsibility, or authority.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Partial.** The Theory of Context preserves source, relationship, and meaning, while the knowledge documents distinguish local and person-specific knowledge. No reviewed theory sentence directly states that recording is not ownership transfer.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** The statement is a derived knowledge-ownership and entrusted-information boundary.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **Yes, ownership gap.** The reviewed sources protect authority, access, context, and responsibility, but the precise ownership and consent consequences of recorded information remain unresolved.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** The general authority principle is applied specifically to Digital Colleague knowledge and professional expression.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **No new principle gap established.** Application to a particular context, person, profession, or decision may still remain unknown.

### 3.7 Experience != transferable history.

**Observation:** Experience may inform learning and shared understanding, but it does not become another person's or Digital Colleague's lived history.

**B - Existing theory coverage:** **Partial.** The Theory of Context includes experience and memory among concepts whose interpretation depends on context. The Theory of Understanding distinguishes recall from understanding. Neither directly states that experience is not transferable history.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** This is a derived experience and inheritance boundary.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **Yes, wording gap.** The non-transferability of lived history is not directly mapped to an existing constitutional sentence in the reviewed sources.

### 3.8 Context determines meaning.

**Observation:** Context governs the meaning, relevance, and applicability of knowledge and experience.

**A - Existing constitutional coverage:** **Partial.** Article II requires understanding before advising, Article III requires truth before certainty, and Article V defines communication as transfer of understanding. **B - Existing theory coverage:** **Yes, directly.** The Theory of Context states that context governs interpretation and relevance, changes interpretation without changing truth, and that words do not possess fixed meaning in isolation. The Theory of Understanding states that understanding emerges when knowledge is interpreted within context.

**C - Derived Digital Colleague interpretation:** **Yes.** Applying context theory to Digital Colleague knowledge, profession, identity, and experience is a derived interpretation.

**D - Unresolved gap:** **No theory gap established.** The constitutional expression is indirect, but the theory coverage is direct. The applicability of context in a future case may still be unknown.
