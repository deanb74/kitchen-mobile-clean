## 2. Exact Amendment Under Review

The amendment unit consists of the following heading, theorem, and paragraph together:

```markdown
### Fifth Judgement Theorem

Judgement determines whether available understanding, including its uncertainty, is adequate in context for a course of action and its reasonably foreseeable consequences.

Uncertainty is material when it could change the appropriate course or its reasonably foreseeable consequences. Whether to seek more evidence is itself a judgement: inquiry must be feasible, proportionate, responsible, timely and within authority. If material uncertainty cannot responsibly be reduced, it must remain explicit and constrain the course chosen. Judgement may conclude that no further inquiry is presently warranted, but must consider the consequences of action, inaction, delay and inquiry. Such a conclusion neither resolves uncertainty nor closes the matter; materially changed context or evidence requires fresh judgement.
```

The heading and two substantive passages exactly match Section 17 of the wording review.

The heading, theorem, and paragraph are one amendment unit. No part may be inserted, omitted, rewritten, reordered, or authorised independently.

## 4. Existing Judgement Theory

The canonical Theory of Judgement currently establishes that:

1. Judgement evaluates understanding, uncertainty, and context;
2. Judgement determines the most appropriate course of action;
3. good Judgement balances evidence, uncertainty, consequence, and responsibility;
4. Judgement operates within boundaries and recognises Authority;
5. Understanding may exist without Judgement, but Judgement requires Understanding;
6. the same Understanding may produce different Judgements in different Contexts;
7. good Judgement acknowledges uncertainty;
8. Judgement chooses the most appropriate action, not necessarily the only possible action;
9. decisions can be explained, challenged, and improved;

The proposed unit inherits these statements. It does not replace or reinterpret them.

## 5. Relationship Gap Test

Existing Theory identifies the ingredients but leaves one material relationship under-explicit: how Judgement evaluates whether available Understanding is adequate, how material uncertainty affects that decision, whether further inquiry is itself subject to Judgement, and why ending inquiry neither resolves uncertainty nor closes future reconsideration.

The amendment fills that relationship gap by stating:

1. adequacy is judged for a course and foreseeable consequences;
2. materiality concerns whether uncertainty could change that course or its consequences;
3. further inquiry is evaluated rather than triggered automatically;
4. unresolved material uncertainty constrains the course;
5. no further inquiry may be appropriate without becoming permanent closure;
6. materially changed Context or evidence requires fresh Judgement.

Leaving Theory unchanged would permit a reasonable reader to acknowledge uncertainty while still misunderstanding these six relationships. The benefit is therefore material rather than rhetorical.

## 7. Understanding Compatibility

Understanding remains the owner of interpreted meaning and recognised uncertainty. The proposed theorem refers to `available understanding, including its uncertainty` as Judgement's input.

It does not make adequacy an intrinsic state of Understanding. Judgement remains responsible for determining whether that Understanding is adequate for the course and consequences in Context.

No amendment to the Theory of Understanding is required.

## 8. Context Compatibility

Context remains the owner of surrounding circumstances, relevance, purpose, ambiguity, and the conditions under which risk and consequence are interpreted.

The proposed wording uses `in context` and `reasonably foreseeable consequences`. Judgement evaluates within those conditions; it does not absorb Context's responsibility.

Materially changed Context requires fresh Judgement. This applies the existing Context dependence without changing Context Theory. No amendment to the Theory of Context is required.

## 9. Learning Compatibility

Learning remains reflection on Judgement and outcomes that improves future capability. The amendment does not make reconsideration automatic Learning or claim that a no-inquiry decision is correct.

By preserving challenge and fresh Judgement when Context or evidence materially changes, the proposal keeps later reflection possible. No Learning result or amendment is created or required.

## 10. Wisdom Compatibility

Wisdom remains the generalised application of Learning and Judgement and may improve which questions are worth asking over time.

The immediate decisions about adequacy, material uncertainty, and inquiry remain within Judgement. The amendment neither transfers those decisions to Wisdom nor makes Wisdom a prerequisite for every inquiry decision.

No amendment to the Theory of Wisdom is required.

## 11. Trust Compatibility
