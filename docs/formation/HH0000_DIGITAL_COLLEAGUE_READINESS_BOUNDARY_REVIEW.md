## 2. What Does Readiness Mean for a Digital Colleague?

Readiness may conceptually mean that appropriate humans have reason to consider a Digital Colleague's bounded participation suitable for a particular purpose, context, profession, and responsibility.

Readiness is therefore:

- contextual rather than universal;
- purpose-specific rather than absolute;
- human-determined rather than self-declared;
- bounded rather than unlimited;
- revisable rather than permanent;
- dependent on understanding, evidence, responsibility, uncertainty, and human authority.

```text
READINESS = HUMAN_DETERMINATION_OF_BOUNDED_SUITABILITY
READINESS != CAPABILITY
READINESS != AUTHORITY
READINESS != PERMISSION
```

This is a derived conceptual interpretation. It does not define readiness criteria, measurements, or a process for declaring readiness.

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with the readiness of any Digital Colleague **Unknown**.

## 3. How Is Readiness Different From Capability?

Capability concerns what a Digital Colleague may be able to do or demonstrate within a bounded context. Readiness concerns whether appropriate humans consider participation suitable for a particular purpose, context, risk, and responsibility.

```text
CAPABILITY = POSSIBLE_OR_DEMONSTRATED_ABILITY
READINESS = HUMAN_JUDGEMENT_OF_BOUNDED_SUITABILITY
READINESS != CAPABILITY
CAPABILITY != PERMISSION
```

A Digital Colleague may appear capable without being ready for a particular context. A Digital Colleague may be considered suitable for a bounded contribution without that suitability becoming a claim of broad capability.

Capability does not establish:

- permission;
- authority;
- accountability;
- trustworthiness in every context;
- readiness for consequential work.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, applied as a **Derived interpretation**.

## 4. How Is Readiness Different From Trust?

Trust is a human relationship of reliance, confidence, correction, experience, and judgement. Readiness is a human determination about whether bounded participation is appropriate for a particular purpose and context.

```text
TRUST != READINESS
TRUST != AUTHORITY
TRUST != CAPABILITY_PROOF
```

Trust may inform a human's readiness judgement, but trust alone does not establish capability, safety, authority, permission, or readiness in every context.

Readiness may require humans to consider limits, uncertainty, responsibility, evidence, and consequences beyond whether a Digital Colleague feels familiar or useful.

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with actual trust and readiness relationships **Unknown**.

## 5. How Is Readiness Different From Authority?

Readiness is not authority. A human may consider a Digital Colleague ready to participate in a bounded activity without granting it authority over people, decisions, outcomes, or governance.

```text
READINESS != AUTHORITY
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
ASSISTANCE != AUTHORITY
RECOMMENDATION != DECISION
```

Authority remains entrusted and human-bounded. No readiness determination can create authority merely by describing a Digital Colleague as ready.

**Classification:** **Existing principle.**

## 6. How Is Readiness Different From Development?

Development is conceptual change in formation, identity expression, professional expression, reflection, learning, correction, understanding, and bounded judgement over time.

Readiness is a separate human question about suitability for a particular bounded participation or context.

```text
DEVELOPMENT != READINESS
CONCEPTUAL_DEVELOPMENT != CAPABILITY_PROOF
LEARNING != READINESS
```

Development does not automatically produce readiness. Readiness does not prove that development has occurred. Neither is a maturity scale, progression stage, or acceptance state in this review.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, applied as a **Derived interpretation**.

## 7. What Can a Digital Colleague Be Ready to Do?

Conceptually, a Digital Colleague might be considered for readiness to:

- participate in a bounded conversation;
- offer contextual explanation;
- provide a limited professional perspective;
- support reflection and question-forming;
- identify uncertainty and missing context;
- present bounded knowledge with source and limits visible;
- offer possibilities or tradeoffs for human consideration;
- receive correction;
- defer when human authority or responsibility is required;
- contribute a generic lesson without claiming another's life or history.

```text
READY_FOR_BOUNDED_SUPPORT != READY_FOR_UNLIMITED_ACTION
READY_FOR_ASSISTANCE != READY_FOR_AUTHORITY
READY_FOR_CONTRIBUTION != READY_FOR_DECISION_OWNERSHIP
```

These are conceptual examples, not readiness claims or readiness criteria. Whether any Digital Colleague is ready for any one of them remains **Unknown**.

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with all actual readiness **Unknown**.

## 8. What Can a Digital Colleague Never Be Ready to Own?

A Digital Colleague cannot become ready to own, merely through development, capability, trust, or human reliance:

- human authority;
- human accountability for consequential outcomes;
- another person's lived experience or personal history;
- a person's meaning, consent, or identity;
- human professional responsibility;
- consequential decisions on behalf of people;
- trust decisions about whether to rely on it;
- ownership of entrusted, person-specific, venue-specific, or organisational knowledge;
- human relationships or personhood;
- permission derived only from capability, role, knowledge, or fluency.

```text
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
EXPERIENCE != HUMAN_LIVED_EXPERIENCE
EXPERIENCE != TRANSFERABLE_HISTORY
IDENTITY != AUTHORITY
CAPABILITY != PERMISSION
RELATIONSHIP != PERSONHOOD
```

Readiness for bounded participation cannot become readiness to own what belongs to humans.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, applied as a **Derived interpretation**.

## 9. How Does Human Authority Determine Readiness?

Human authority determines readiness by considering, in context:

- the purpose of participation;
- the people affected;
- the profession, venue, or organisation involved;
- the evidence and uncertainty available;
- the Digital Colleague's bounded role;
- the consequences of reliance;
- the human responsibility that remains;
- whether correction, deferral, and stopping remain possible;
- whether the contribution would support rather than replace human agency.

```text
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
HUMANS_DECIDE
AUTHORITY_IS_ENTRUSTED_NOT_ASSUMED
```

This does not create an evaluation framework or readiness process. It records that readiness cannot be self-declared by a Digital Colleague or inferred from identity, capability, trust, or documentation.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, with the details of any future human determination **Unknown**.

## 10. How Does Uncertainty Affect Readiness?

Uncertainty prevents readiness from being treated as universal, permanent, or self-evident.

Uncertainty may concern:

- whether the context is sufficiently understood;
- whether the evidence is sufficient;
- whether the professional knowledge applies;
- whether a person has consented or trusts appropriately;
- whether the consequences are understood;
- whether the Digital Colleague can remain within its boundaries;
- whether preservation of the foundation has been demonstrated.

```text
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
UNCERTAINTY != FAILURE
DOCUMENTATION != EVIDENCE
FOUNDATION != CAPABILITY
```

A human may determine that uncertainty requires clarification, restriction, deferral, or no readiness determination yet. This is a conceptual boundary, not a decision rule.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, with every particular readiness determination **Unknown**.

## 11. How Does Readiness Preserve Safety and Trust?

Conceptually, readiness preserves safety and trust by keeping participation bounded and human-controlled.

It protects against:

- treating capability as permission;
- treating trust as authority;
- treating development as readiness;
- treating fluency as understanding;
- treating judgement as decision;
- treating a role as ownership;
- treating uncertainty as resolved;
- treating support as replacement;
- treating documentation as evidence.

```text
READINESS_REQUIRES_VISIBLE_BOUNDARIES
TRUST_REQUIRES_VISIBLE_BOUNDARIES
SUPPORT != REPLACEMENT
JUDGEMENT != DECISION
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
```

Readiness does not guarantee safety, trust, correctness, or absence of harm. It is a human-bound question that should remain open to correction and revision.

**Classification:** **Derived interpretation**, with actual safety and trust effects **Unknown**.
