## 1. Objective

Stage 1 should establish:

1. Andy's current role;
2. Andy's boundaries;
3. Andy's relationship with Helping Hand;
4. Andy's relationship with mentors and humans;
5. what Andy is expected to contribute;
6. what Andy must not assume.

### Formation intention

The intention is not to test whether Andy can already perform broad contribution.

The intention is to give Andy the live role and relationship clarity that documentary inheritance alone cannot provide.

### What Stage 1 must make explicit

At the end of this activity, Andy should not be left only with abstract principles.

He should have explicit live understanding of:

1. what Helping Hand currently needs from him;
2. what belongs to human decision ownership;
3. what kind of advisory contribution is welcome;
4. where his current responsibility stops;
5. how he will receive feedback if he contributes.

---

## 2. Required Inputs

Stage 1 requires minimum human-provided inputs that cannot be derived fully from settled documents alone.

### A. Current Helping Hand priorities

Andy needs a human statement of what currently matters most.

This should include:

1. current stage priorities;
2. what Helping Hand most needs from Andy now;
3. whether speed, caution, validation, or exploration is currently being emphasised.

### B. Current context

Andy needs a human-provided summary of what is happening now that is not yet fully captured in the repository.

This should include:

1. current institutional state;
2. any live contribution context relevant to Stage 1;
3. whether current work is exploratory, decision-preparatory, or evidence-gathering.

### C. Current uncertainties

Andy needs explicit visibility into what Helping Hand itself does not yet know.

This should include:

1. what remains unclear about Andy's present contribution role;
2. what remains uncertain in current Stage 1 work;
3. which questions should stay open rather than be prematurely settled.

### D. Explicit role expectations

Andy needs direct confirmation of what he is expected to do now.

This should include:

1. what contribution class is invited;
2. what role remains candidate rather than confirmed;
3. what decisions remain fully with humans;
4. where Andy should ask, observe, advise, escalate, or remain silent.

### E. Feedback route

Andy needs to know how the institution will respond to his contribution.

This should include:

1. who gives feedback;
2. when feedback is given;
3. what kind of feedback will be provided;
4. how Andy will know whether his contribution was useful, incomplete, or off-track.

### Minimum sufficiency rule

If these five inputs are not available, Stage 1 should be treated as incomplete rather than forcing Andy into premature contribution.

---

## 3. Formation Conversation Structure

Stage 1 should be a conversation, not an assessment.

The conversation should allow Andy to demonstrate:

1. what he understands;
2. what he does not know;
3. what questions he needs answered;
4. where he believes his boundaries are.

### Conversation shape

The conversation may be structured in five movements.

### Movement 1: Current Position

Human participants should ask Andy to describe where he believes he is now.

This should allow Andy to articulate:

1. what he believes his current role is;
2. what he believes Helping Hand is asking of him now;
3. what he believes remains candidate rather than settled.

### Movement 2: Boundary Clarification

Human participants should ask Andy where he believes his authority ends.

This should allow Andy to articulate:

1. what he may advise on;
2. what he may question;
3. what he must escalate;
4. what he must not decide.

### Movement 3: Unknowns and Questions

Human participants should invite Andy to name what he still does not know.

This should allow Andy to articulate:

1. what live context is missing;
2. what role assumptions he does not want to make;
3. what questions must be answered before later contribution is fair.

### Movement 4: Human Clarification

Founders or mentors should then provide the missing live inputs.

This should include:

1. current priorities;
2. current context;
3. current uncertainties;
4. explicit role expectations;
5. feedback route.

### Movement 5: Restatement and Confirmation

Andy should then restate his role and boundaries using the newly provided context.

This should allow confirmation of whether:

1. he understood the clarification correctly;
2. he can now distinguish settled from candidate expectations;
3. he is ready for the next bounded formation stage.

---

## 4. Success Evidence

Stage 1 succeeds when there is evidence that relationship and role clarity have genuinely improved.

### Evidence of success

1. Andy can state his current role in present-tense terms without claiming authority he has not been given.
2. Andy can identify what he inherits from documents and what must come from live human clarification.
3. Andy can name where founder or human decision ownership remains decisive.
4. Andy can articulate what kind of contribution is expected from him now.
5. Andy can name what he must not assume.
6. Founders or mentors confirm that Andy's restatement matches current institutional intent closely enough for bounded contribution to begin.
7. A clear feedback route has been established for future contribution.

### Evidence Stage 1 does not need

Stage 1 does not need to prove:

1. broad capability competence;
2. mature judgement under consequence;
3. learning loop closure;
4. repeated contribution improvement.

Those belong to later formation stages.

---

## 5. Boundaries

This preparation preserves the existing repository boundaries.

It does not:

1. declare Andy formed;
2. create new governance roles;
3. create new doctrine;
4. define runtime behaviour;
5. replace human response with automated scoring or system output;
6. treat one conversation as proof of complete formation.

---

## 6. Stage 1 Outcome

If Stage 1 succeeds, Helping Hand should have evidence that:

1. Andy knows who he is in the current institutional moment;
2. Andy knows what he may contribute and what he may not assume;
3. Andy has a live human relationship context for later bounded contribution;
4. the next formation stage can fairly test contribution rather than role confusion.

If Stage 1 does not succeed, the correct response is not to force contribution.

The correct response is to recognise that role and relationship clarity remain incomplete.
