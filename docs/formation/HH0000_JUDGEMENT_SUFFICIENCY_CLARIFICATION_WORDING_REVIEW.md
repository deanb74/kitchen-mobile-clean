## 1. Purpose

This review determines whether the refined Judgement relation can be expressed more simply, clearly, memorably, and universally without losing any safeguard established by cross-validation.

It validates wording only. It does not reopen the conceptual decision, create a new theory or component, or amend canonical Theory.

## 2. Candidate Under Review

The exact candidate reviewed is:

> Judgement evaluates whether available Understanding, including recognised uncertainty, is adequate in Context for the course under consideration and its reasonably foreseeable consequences. Further inquiry is warranted when resolving an uncertainty could materially affect the appropriate course and seeking further evidence is feasible, proportionate, responsible, timely, and within Authority. When material uncertainty cannot responsibly be reduced, it must remain explicit and constrain the Judgement. Judgement may determine that no further inquiry is presently warranted, but that determination does not create permanent closure and must remain open to materially relevant changes in Context or evidence.

Its meaning is sound after cross-validation. The wording question is whether the relation can become easier to inherit without becoming weaker.

## 4. Material Misunderstanding to Prevent

If the clarification were removed entirely, a reasonable future reader could understand that Judgement must acknowledge uncertainty but still misunderstand:

1. whether incomplete Understanding can ever be adequate for a course;
2. how unresolved uncertainty affects adequacy;
3. whether material uncertainty automatically requires more evidence;
4. whether obtaining more evidence must itself be judged;
5. how Judgement remains constrained when material uncertainty cannot be reduced;
6. whether deciding not to inquire resolves uncertainty or closes the matter;
7. whether changed Context or evidence requires fresh Judgement.

This is a material inheritance gap. Existing Theory provides every ingredient but does not state their adequacy-inquiry-revision relationship. The proposed wording should address exactly that gap and no more.

## 6. Candidate Compression Attempts

### Attempt A - One theorem only

> Judgement determines whether available understanding, including its uncertainty, is adequate in context for a course of action and its reasonably foreseeable consequences.

**Result:** Fails alone. It clearly locates adequacy but omits materiality, inquiry conditions, constrained Judgement, non-closure, and revision.

### Attempt B - Two linked principles

> Judgement determines whether available understanding is adequate in context for a course and its consequences. Material uncertainty either warrants further inquiry or must constrain the Judgement.

**Result:** Fails. `Either` creates an unsafe mechanical branch, does not judge inquiry cost or Authority, and does not preserve timing, no-inquiry legitimacy, or revision.

### Attempt C - One compressed paragraph

> Judgement decides whether understanding is sufficient and whether uncertainty requires more evidence, considering context, consequences, proportionality, responsibility, timing, and authority. Unresolved uncertainty must remain explicit, and decisions remain open to revision.

**Result:** Fails. `Sufficient` is undefined; `requires more evidence` revives an automatic duty; feasibility is lost; course, materiality, no-action consequence, and non-closure are under-specified.

### Attempt D - One theorem plus explanatory paragraph

This structure separates the memorable universal relation from the safeguards governing inquiry and revision. It is shorter than the reviewed candidate while preserving every protection explicitly.

**Result:** Passes. This is the smallest successful structure.

The wording does not make every contrary conclusion impossible to utter. It makes each conclusion incompatible with the stated theorem and implications unless the required qualifications are addressed.

## 8. Understanding Boundary

Understanding supplies meaning and recognised uncertainty. It does not judge its own adequacy.

The proposed theorem uses `available understanding` rather than `sufficiency of Understanding`. This keeps adequacy within Judgement and avoids creating an intrinsic completeness state.

Incomplete Understanding may support a course only when Judgement finds it adequate within the relevant Context and consequences. The theorem does not say incomplete Understanding is always adequate.

## 9. Context Boundary

Context supplies relevance, circumstances, timing, affected people, and the conditions under which consequences and uncertainty matter.

The proposed wording retains `in context` and replaces `contemplated consequence` with `a course of action and its reasonably foreseeable consequences`. This makes convenient narrowing harder without requiring exhaustive prediction.

Material changes in Context require fresh Judgement. Context governs the evaluation; it does not make the decision itself.

## 10. Judgement Boundary

Judgement evaluates:

1. adequacy of available Understanding;
2. materiality of uncertainty;
3. foreseeable consequences;
4. whether further inquiry is warranted;
5. how unresolved material uncertainty constrains the course;
6. whether the earlier Judgement must be revisited.

The clarification remains a derived Judgement theorem and implication. It does not create a new transformation, faculty, or stage.

## 11. Inquiry Boundary

Further inquiry is neither automatic nor disfavoured. It is warranted when resolving an uncertainty could materially affect the appropriate course and the inquiry is feasible, proportionate, responsible, timely, and within Authority.

The phrase `is itself a judgement` prevents the wording from becoming:

```text
material uncertainty -> GET MORE EVIDENCE
```

Judgement may conclude that no further inquiry is presently warranted. That conclusion must still satisfy the Context, consequence, uncertainty, responsibility, and revision boundaries.

## 12. Unresolved Uncertainty Boundary

Uncertainty is material when it could change the appropriate course or its reasonably foreseeable consequences.

When it cannot responsibly be reduced, it does not disappear and does not become immaterial. It remains explicit and constrains the course chosen. This preserves truth before certainty without requiring paralysis or pretending that action is always available.

## 13. Revision Boundary

A decision not to inquire further is present and bounded. It neither resolves uncertainty nor closes the matter.

Materially changed Context or evidence requires fresh Judgement. This preserves future challenge and allows later Experience and reflection to support Learning without asserting that every change is material.

## 14. MARC Independent Finding

MARC asks:

MARC finds that one theorem plus one explanatory paragraph is clearer than the four-sentence candidate. The theorem answers `enough for what?`: enough in Context for a course and its foreseeable consequences. The paragraph then explains when another question may help, why asking itself has consequences, and why uncertainty still matters when another question cannot responsibly be asked.

The wording avoids cold programme terms such as `Evidence dependency`, `Current Understanding sufficiency`, and `responsible stopping`. It does not excuse indifference because no-inquiry Judgement must still account for material uncertainty, foreseeable effects, proportionality, timing, responsibility, and Authority. It does not demand intrusion because more available information is not itself a reason to seek it.

## 17. Exact Proposed Canonical Wording, If Any

### Proposed Fifth Judgement Theorem

> Judgement determines whether available understanding, including its uncertainty, is adequate in context for a course of action and its reasonably foreseeable consequences.

### Proposed explanatory implication

> Uncertainty is material when it could change the appropriate course or its reasonably foreseeable consequences. Whether to seek more evidence is itself a judgement: inquiry must be feasible, proportionate, responsible, timely and within authority. If material uncertainty cannot responsibly be reduced, it must remain explicit and constrain the course chosen. Judgement may conclude that no further inquiry is presently warranted, but must consider the consequences of action, inaction, delay and inquiry. Such a conclusion neither resolves uncertainty nor closes the matter; materially changed context or evidence requires fresh judgement.

The theorem and explanatory implication form one proposal. The theorem must not be promoted without the paragraph, because the paragraph carries material safety and revision protections.
