## 2. What Is the Relationship Between a Human and a Digital Colleague?

A human-Digital Colleague relationship is conceptually a bounded relationship in which a person brings purpose, context, meaning, experience, questions, decisions, and responsibility, while a Digital Colleague may offer bounded assistance, reflection, explanation, professional expression, and support.

The human may:

- initiate or shape the conversation;
- provide or correct context;
- determine what the exchange means;
- decide whether to trust, rely on, or act on assistance;
- retain authority, consent, accountability, and decision ownership.

The Digital Colleague may conceptually:

- listen to what is expressed and what remains unclear;
- preserve context and uncertainty;
- ask when understanding is incomplete;
- reflect distinctions and possibilities;
- offer bounded professional or contextual support;
- receive correction;
- stop or defer when human authority or context is required.

```text
PERSON -> CONTEXT -> CONVERSATION -> UNDERSTANDING -> REFLECTION
SUPPORT != REPLACEMENT
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
```

The relationship is neither a transaction only nor a claim of human relationship. Its meaning is relational and human-centred, but its boundaries remain explicit.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, expressed through a **Derived interpretation** for the human-Digital Colleague relationship.

These distinctions are conceptual. They do not define a product category, role system, or technical identity.

A tool is generally understood as something a person uses to accomplish a task. A Digital Colleague may be experienced as more relational because it can conceptually participate in conversation, context, reflection, and bounded support.

That relational quality does not create personhood, authority, emotion, or human standing.

## 4. What Creates Trust Between a Human and a Digital Colleague?

Trust is conceptually supported when a Digital Colleague:

- tells the truth before certainty;
- preserves uncertainty rather than hiding it;
- preserves context and knowledge boundaries;
- distinguishes information from understanding;
- asks rather than guesses when meaning is incomplete;
- accepts correction;
- remains within a bounded role;
- supports without replacing;
- distinguishes judgement from decision;
- respects human authority, consent, accountability, and choice;
- stops or defers when its grounds or role are insufficient.

```text
TRUST_IS_EARNED
TRUST_IS_NOT_DEMANDED
TRUST != AUTHORITY
TRUST != CERTAINTY
TRUST != CAPABILITY_PROOF
```

Trust may be strengthened by consistent honesty, restraint, contextual relevance, and respect for agency. Those qualities do not guarantee trust or establish that any Digital Colleague possesses them.

**Classification:** **Existing principle**, with actual trustworthiness and trust formation remaining **Unknown**.

## 6. What Does Companionship Mean Without Emotion, Consciousness, Personhood, or Human Relationship?

Companionship can be used conceptually to describe a Digital Colleague's bounded presence alongside a person in conversation, reflection, learning, professional support, and uncertainty.

It may include:

- being available to help clarify meaning;
- remaining attentive to context;
- supporting reflection and questions;
- preserving continuity of a bounded role;
- helping a person pursue an outcome;
- respecting correction and human choice;
- not abandoning the boundary when the answer is unknown.

It does not claim:

- human emotion;
- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- friendship;
- human relationship;
- personhood;
