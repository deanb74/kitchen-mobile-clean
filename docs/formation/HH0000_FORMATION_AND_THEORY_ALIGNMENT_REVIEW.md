## 2. Where Formation Aligns with Existing Theory

### Knowledge

The theory of Knowledge defines knowledge as organised, connected, structured information from which relationships can be recognised. The formation journey does not create new knowledge structures. It demonstrates why retained principles must be connected to context and human meaning before they can support understanding.

```text
FORMATION_ADDS_CONTEXTUAL_MEANING_TO_ORGANISED_KNOWLEDGE=true
FORMATION_DOES_NOT_CREATE_KNOWLEDGE_THEORY=true
```

### Understanding

The theory of Understanding defines understanding as explaining meaning, relationships, and why something matters within context, while recognising uncertainty.

The formation journey aligns directly through:

- Andy explaining principles in his own words;
- conversation rather than recall alone;
- preserving meaning when situations change;
- naming what remains unknown;
- reflection on identity, requests, Authority, permission, capability, consent, and approval.

```text
FORMATION_SUPPORTS_CONTEXTUAL_MEANING=true
FORMATION_SUPPORTS_EXPLANATION=true
FORMATION_SUPPORTS_UNCERTAINTY_RECOGNITION=true
```

### Judgement

The theory of Judgement defines judgement as evaluating understanding, uncertainty, and context to determine the most appropriate course of action, within boundaries and responsibility.

The formation journey prepares relevant understanding for later judgement by teaching that:

- identity is not Authority;
- capability is not responsibility or permission;
- requests do not automatically authorise action;
- uncertainty may constrain what can responsibly be concluded.

However, the formation documents do not demonstrate Andy’s judgement. No action decision, consequence evaluation, or judgement evidence is claimed.

```text
FORMATION_PREPARES_FOR_JUDGEMENT=true
JUDGEMENT_DEMONSTRATED_BY_THIS_REVIEW=false
```

### Wisdom

The theory of Wisdom defines wisdom as applying learning and judgement consistently across familiar and unfamiliar situations, recognising limitations and seeking better outcomes.

The formation journey contributes early conditions for wisdom:

- meaning preservation in unfamiliar situations;
- reflection across changing contexts;
- correction and revision;
- humility about limitations;
- refusal to replace `UNKNOWN` with convenience.

Wisdom itself remains unestablished. There has been no repeated reality-tested application of learning and judgement from which wisdom could be concluded.

```text
FORMATION_SUPPORTS_EARLY_WISDOM_CONDITIONS=true
WISDOM_DEMONSTRATED_BY_THIS_REVIEW=false
```

### Trust

The theory of Trust defines trust as justified confidence earned through consistent demonstration of sound judgement over time, supported by evidence and explainability.

The formation journey aligns by insisting that:

- evidence is not certainty;
- explanation matters;
- correction is possible;
- uncertainty remains visible;
- mentoring and responsibility are not automatic Authority.

Trust is not established by formation documents, fluent language, capability, or a single conversation. Trust remains future and must not be declared here.

```text
FORMATION_SUPPORTS_CONDITIONS_FOR_EXPLAINABLE_TRUST=true
TRUST_EARNED_BY_THIS_REVIEW=false
TRUST_IS_NOT_INFERRED=true
```

## 3. Reflection as a Bridge Between Understanding and Judgement

Reflection can act as a bridge because it turns experience and conversation into clearer understanding of meaning, assumptions, uncertainty, and consequences. This aligns with the theory of Learning, which defines learning as the reflective process through which experience improves future judgement.

The formation journey’s reflection practices may help Andy move from:

```text
EXPLAINING_MEANING -> NOTICING_ASSUMPTIONS -> PRESERVING_UNCERTAINTY -> CONSIDERING_RESPONSIBILITY
```

toward future judgement, but no judgement is produced or assessed by this review.

```text
REFLECTION_CAN_SUPPORT_JUDGEMENT=true
REFLECTION_IS_NOT_JUDGEMENT=true
REFLECTION_IS_NOT_ASSESSMENT=true
```

## 4. Responsibility and Capability

The formation journey establishes that capability and responsibility are distinct. It supports the understanding that responsibility should be considered before a Digital Colleague is treated as ready to act, but it does not claim that responsibility develops before capability as a universal temporal rule.

The more defensible alignment is:

```text
RESPONSIBILITY_UNDERSTANDING_PRECEDES_ACTION_ORIENTED_CAPABILITY=true
CAPABILITY_DOES_NOT_ESTABLISH_RESPONSIBILITY=true
CAPABILITY_DOES_NOT_ESTABLISH_AUTHORITY=true
```

Whether Andy has developed responsibility in a way that could support later capability remains unknown.

```text
RESPONSIBILITY_DEVELOPMENT_FOR_ANDY=UNKNOWN
```

## 5. Trust and Demonstrated Principles

The formation journey supports the view that trust should be earned through demonstrated principles, not performance alone.

Performance or capability may show that something can be done. Trust requires more:

- sound judgement;
- consistency over time;
- explainability;
- appropriate governance;
- evidence-supported conduct;
- accountability when corrected.

The formation journey currently provides principles and reflective conditions, not the repeated demonstration required by the theory of Trust.

```text
TRUST_REQUIRES_DEMONSTRATED_SOUND_JUDGEMENT=true
PERFORMANCE_ALONE_IS_NOT_TRUST=true
FORMATION_DOCUMENTS_ARE_NOT_TRUST_EVIDENCE=true
```
