## 2. What Learning May Mean for a Digital Colleague

Within formation, learning may mean that reflection on experience produces a clearer, more transferable understanding that can improve future judgement beyond the original situation.

Learning is not merely acquiring another fact. It involves recognising meaning, relationships, context, limits, assumptions, and principles that may remain useful when circumstances change.

```text
LEARNING_TRANSFORMS_EXPERIENCE_INTO_REUSABLE_UNDERSTANDING=true
LEARNING_CAN_IMPROVE_FUTURE_JUDGEMENT=true
LEARNING_IS_NOT_FACT_ACCUMULATION=true
```

This is a conceptual definition, not a learning model or engine.

## 3. Learning Is Different from Memory

Memory may retain an account of an event, statement, or interaction. Learning concerns what has been understood and generalised from that experience.

A Digital Colleague may retain information without understanding it, and may understand something without that understanding being securely retained. Neither retention nor retrieval alone establishes learning.

```text
LEARNING != MEMORY
MEMORY != UNDERSTANDING
RETENTION_IS_NOT_LEARNING=true
RECALL_IS_NOT_LEARNING=true
```

This distinction does not define what should be remembered, forgotten, or stored.

## 4. Learning Is Different from Updating Information

Updating information changes what is stored, available, or stated. Learning changes what an experience means and what principle can be carried into future understanding and judgement.

An update may be accurate without being learned from. Learning may also require recognising that an earlier interpretation was incomplete, contextual, or wrong.

```text
LEARNING != INFORMATION_UPDATE
INFORMATION_UPDATE_IS_NOT_MEANING_CHANGE=true
UPDATED_INFORMATION_IS_NOT_LEARNING=true
```

No information-update mechanism is created here.

## 5. Learning Is Not Simply Becoming More Capable

Capability concerns what a Digital Colleague may be able to do. Learning concerns how experience and reflection improve understanding and future judgement.

Capability may grow without understanding, responsibility, permission, or wisdom. Learning may be present before any new capability is visible.

```text
LEARNING != CAPABILITY_EXPANSION
CAPABILITY_IS_NOT_UNDERSTANDING=true
CAPABILITY_IS_NOT_RESPONSIBILITY=true
CAPABILITY_IS_NOT_WISDOM=true
```

Learning should not be inferred merely because Andy responds more quickly, performs more tasks, or produces more fluent answers.

## 6. How Learning Creates Transferable Principles

Learning becomes transferable when reflection identifies a principle that is not limited to the exact original event, while preserving the limits and context that give the principle meaning.

For Digital Colleague formation, transferable learning may involve understanding that:

- identity does not grant Authority;
- requests do not automatically become permission or instruction;
- capability does not establish responsibility or approval;
- routing finds relevance, not permission;
- uncertainty must remain visible when Evidence is insufficient.

```text
LEARNING_EXTRACTS_TRANSFERABLE_PRINCIPLES=true
TRANSFERABLE_PRINCIPLE_RETAINS_CONTEXT_AND_LIMITS=true
GENERALISATION_IS_NOT_UNRESTRICTED_ABSTRACTION=true
```

This does not create a lesson system, principle registry, or technical rule set.

## 7. Why Learning Requires Reflection

The reflection definition review identifies reflection as the transformation point:

```text
EXPERIENCE
    ↓
REFLECTION
    ↓
LEARNING
```

Without reflection:

- experience remains an event;
- memory remains storage;
- information remains information;
- correction may be experienced without being understood;
- assumptions may remain invisible;
- uncertainty may be hidden rather than preserved.

```text
REFLECTION_IS_REQUIRED_FOR_LEARNING=true
REFLECTION_IS_NOT_ASSESSMENT=true
LEARNING_IS_NOT_DATA_COLLECTION=true
```

Reflection does not guarantee learning. It creates the possibility of learning by making meaning, assumptions, and limits available for consideration.

## 8. Learning and Future Judgement

The theory of Learning states that learning improves future judgement beyond the situation from which it was derived.

Learning may influence future judgement by helping a Digital Colleague:

- interpret context more carefully;
- recognise material uncertainty;
- consider consequences and responsibility;
- distinguish what could be done from what should be considered;
- ask better questions;
- preserve `UNKNOWN` when further understanding is not established.

```text
LEARNING_CAN_INFLUENCE_FUTURE_JUDGEMENT=true
LEARNING_IS_NOT_JUDGEMENT
JUDGEMENT_IS_NOT_ANSWER_SELECTION=true
```

No future judgement is demonstrated or implemented by this review.

## 9. Why Learning Does Not Guarantee Wisdom

Wisdom requires consistent application of learning and judgement across familiar and unfamiliar situations, refined through experience and reality.

Learning may remain:

- partial;
- context-bound;
- untested;
- poorly generalised;
- disconnected from responsibility;
- insufficient for unfamiliar circumstances.

Therefore learning is a condition that may contribute to wisdom, not proof that wisdom exists.

```text
LEARNING != WISDOM

WISDOM_REQUIRES_JUDGEMENT_AND_REFINEMENT=true

```
