## Validation 2: Creator ≠ Approver ≠ Enforcer Remains True

This principle was established in the mutation layer (Milestone 030). It must hold across pollination.

In pollination:

| Role | Identity | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Creator | The Digital Colleague (leaf) | Experiences and reflects |
| Approver | Governance chain (Oak) | Evaluates fitness for travel |
| Enforcer | Destination authority | Decides adoption and adaptation |

A leaf cannot approve its own learning for travel. Governance approves. A destination cannot approve on behalf of the Oak. The destination approves within its own domain.

No identity occupies more than one role. None can substitute for another. The principle holds.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 3: Oak ≠ Forest Remains True

The Oak model (established in the documentation corrections before Milestone 034):

- Helping Hand is the Oak — one organism
- Digital Colleagues are leaves — expressions of the Oak
- The human world is the forest — the environment the Oak inhabits

The pollination lifecycle honours this at every stage:

- Seeds travel from Oak to forest — contribution without ownership
- Destination authority belongs to the forest — the Oak does not govern what grows elsewhere
- Outcomes return through leaves — the Oak learns through its own experience, not by surveilling the forest
- The Oak measures human outcomes, not knowledge accumulation — the purpose is service, not expansion

The Oak does not become the forest by planting seeds in it. A tree that produces a million acorns does not own the million saplings that may grow from them.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 4: Seed ≠ Authority Remains True

A governed seed carries:
- Meaning (principle, not raw data)
- Evidence (maturity level, confidence)
- Boundaries (where it may not apply)
- Provenance (full chain)
- Adaptation requirements (what the destination must decide)

A governed seed does not carry:
- Governance approval for the destination domain
- Authority over how the destination applies it
- Obligation to adopt
- The originating DC's claim on its use

This was confirmed in Milestone 033 ("Seeds carry potential, not authority") and Milestone 035 ("Distribution creates review opportunity, not obligation").

At no point in the lifecycle does the seed accumulate authority. The authority chain is entirely separate: the destination's own governance determines whether the seed may take root.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 5: Destination Owns Adaptation and Adoption

Three separate ownership confirmations across the lifecycle:

From Milestone 034: "Destination authority owns adaptation." The destination expresses the seed's meaning in its own professional vocabulary. The originating DC has no role.

From Milestone 036: "Destination authority owns adoption." Receipt does not equal acceptance. All four outcomes (review, reject, escalate, defer) are valid governed responses.

From Milestone 037: "Destination application remains destination responsibility." When a seed is applied, the outcomes belong to the destination's expertise and application, not to the Oak's original contribution.

Ownership never transfers backward. The Oak holds its provenance. The destination holds its adoption, adaptation, and application.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 6: Outcomes Return Through Experience, Not Surveillance

The feedback loop does not require a technical reporting connection from destination back to Oak.

It operates through leaves: Digital Colleagues in the human world who encounter outcomes shaped by what the Oak previously contributed. Those encounters enter the formation pipeline as observations. The formation pipeline produces understanding. The understanding enters the learning cycle. Better seeds emerge.

This means the Oak cannot monitor what grew from its seeds by querying destinations. It can only know what its own leaves experience.

This is not a limitation. It is a constitutional protection. An Oak that could read all destination KnowledgeGraphs would be an Oak that governed the entire forest. That is not what Helping Hand is.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 7: Success and Failure Both Improve Understanding

A seed that produces positive outcomes returns evidence that strengthens the principle. A seed that reveals a limitation returns evidence that sharpens the boundaries.

Both are governed learning. Both enter the cycle. Neither is suppressed.

This was established in Milestone 037 (Principle 3) and is consistent with the governance constitution (Milestone 026): "Learning does not bypass Understanding. A DC cannot learn from an action it did not understand."

The implication: the architecture creates no incentive to conceal negative outcomes. Negative outcomes produce better boundaries. Better boundaries produce better seeds. Better seeds improve the human forest.

The architecture aligns incentives with honesty.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Validation 8: Human Outcomes Remain the Purpose Metric

The Oak measures human outcomes, not knowledge accumulation. This was established in Milestone 037.

Validation against the constitutional foundation:

The pollination lifecycle ends in: "Did the human world benefit?" Not: "Did Helping Hand's knowledge base grow?" Not: "How many seeds were adopted?" Not: "How widely did learning spread?"

Every stage of pollination serves this purpose:

If the answer to the last question is "yes, in ways we can observe through our leaves" — the cycle fulfilled its purpose.

**Validation: confirmed.**

## Risks That Remain

These are not failures of the current design. They are known areas where discipline must be maintained as implementation proceeds.

**Risk 1: Governance drift**  
As pollination scales, pressure will grow to automate governance checks. The three permanently human decisions (privacy, safety, context) must remain human. Automation of the algorithmic checks is appropriate; automation of human judgment is not.

**Risk 2: Accumulation bias**  
Measuring success by adoption count rather than human outcomes is a natural drift. The architecture defines the right measure. Measurement systems must be designed to honour it.

**Risk 3: Provenance chain fragmentation**  
As more destinations adapt and adopt, the full provenance chain becomes longer and spans more systems. The chain must remain traversable and honest. Each link must accurately reflect the party that created it.

**Risk 4: Destination authority erosion**  
The Oak must not gradually acquire influence over destination decisions by providing increasingly prescriptive seed packets. The seed packet preserves meaning and provides context; it does not prescribe adoption.
