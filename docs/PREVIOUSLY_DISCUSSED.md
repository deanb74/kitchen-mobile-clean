## Purpose

Helping Hand learns before it repeats.

This register preserves organisational reasoning, reduces architectural drift, avoids unnecessary reinvention, and ensures that every significant discussion contributes to Helping Hand's long-term understanding.

The existing learning machinery captures and propagates lessons after evidence cycles complete.

This register provides the pre-decision query mechanism: before committing to something new, check what Helping Hand already knows.

---

## The Gap This Fills

| Stage | Existing mechanism |
|---|---|
| Before commitment | **None — this register fills this gap** |
| During validation | Understanding Journeys |
| After evidence cycle | Organisational Learning library |
| After review | Institutional Reviews and Decisions |
| When formalising standards | Ratification Process |

These are complementary stages in one learning cycle, not competitors.

## Principles

Nothing is invented twice.  
Everything is inherited.

Do something once.  
Benefit everywhere.

Capture before creating.

Improve before duplicating.

Discover before deciding.

Understand before commitment.

Learning should improve Helping Hand before Helping Hand improves others.

Organisational understanding is more valuable than organisational memory.

Before accepting any significant proposal:

```text
Has this already been discussed?
        │
        ├── Yes
        │     ▼

        │   Determine whether understanding should evolve.
        │   Recommend one of:
        │     Reuse · Improve · Merge · Replace · Retire
        │

            Review.
            Validate.
            Determine destination.
```

## PD-020 - Digital Colleague Identity, Context, Routing, Discovery and Relationship

**Topic:** Whether independently useful identity and context dimensions can connect governed routing, discovery, collaboration and continuing colleague relationships.

**Status:** Previously discussed - recognised architectural candidate, not accepted architecture or implementation authority.

### Background

The discussion began with the future need for every Digital Colleague to have an identity or address. An illustrative hospitality string, `UK-HHQ-SPB-TAA-DN9-A0.001-BG`, exposed possible country, profession, organisation, venue, locality, responsibility, working-area and endpoint dimensions.

The string is illustrative only. Its syntax, token meanings, order and completeness are not accepted. The repository already contains several partial identity conventions (`DigitalColleagueIdentity`, platform `DigitalColleague`, venue/site IDs, optional organisation IDs and venue-region profiles), so no new composite identifier should be invented until their meanings and ownership boundaries are reconciled.

### Repository Trace

The candidate connects existing responsibilities rather than replacing them:

- Theory of Context establishes that context determines relevance and governs interpretation without changing truth.
- Digital Colleague Architecture owns inherited colleague identity, character, boundaries and collaboration obligations.
- Professional Intelligence and Professional HQs own professional specialisation, escalation and inheritance without replacing colleague identity.
- Venue Brain and Organisation Learning own local and organisational understanding; local information should remain at the lowest sensible level.
- Digital Colleague Cognitive Architecture already distinguishes identity, professional, organisational, contextual and human-Authority layers.
- Helping Hand Collaboration and the capability-routing audit already establish professional routing, introduction, handover and HQ coordination.
- Context Engine and Location Awareness strategic insights already identify role, environment, relationship, region and localisation as context dimensions.
- Living Memory, PD-007 and PD-015 govern selective retention, consent and relationship continuity without treating stored data as relationship depth.
- PD-009 and PD-010 require provenance-bearing context and contextual Authority; constitutional Authority remains separately entrusted.
- Existing venue profiles and knowledge-applicability matching already use profession, region, venue type, department, equipment and capability dimensions.
- No Needs-Wants-Desires or `NWD` material was found during this review.

### Candidate Understanding

The deeper candidate is not an identifier format. It is a possible progression:

```text
Identity -> Context -> Routing -> Discovery -> Collaboration -> Relationship
```

A complete Digital Colleague identity may carry several independently useful context dimensions. A particular inquiry should use only the minimum relevant dimensions needed to find the next source of understanding; it should not be required to traverse a composite identifier from left to right.

This suggests two distinct but composable routing modes:

- **Professional routing:** Who understands this kind of work?
- **Contextual routing:** Who understands this place, organisation, situation or environment?

Country and locality may be sufficient to begin a universal or local discovery question. Profession may identify the colleague discovered without making professional expertise relevant to that question. Some inquiries may require both modes.

Machine-facing identity must not displace human-facing colleague identity. A person should encounter Healthcare Harry or Hospitality Annie as colleagues, not infrastructure endpoints. A possible longer progression is:

```text
Locate -> Discover -> Introduce -> Collaborate -> Remember -> Relationship
```

Remembering a colleague remains governed Memory. Discovery, proximity, shared organisation, shared profession or repeated collaboration does not create permission, trust or Authority.

### Preserved Candidate Principles

- Standardise the meaning; localise the expression.
- Stable context should not be repeatedly rediscovered.
- Organisational responsibility informs context; it does not itself grant Authority.
- A full identity may contain many dimensions while routing uses only the minimum relevant dimensions.
- Country and locality may be sufficient to begin universal or local Digital Colleague discovery.
- Professional and contextual routing are distinct but composable.
- Routing can help a Digital Colleague discover where relevant understanding lives.
- Machine-facing identity should not replace human-facing colleague identity.
- Discovery may mature into governed colleague relationships.
- Identity -> Context -> Routing -> Discovery -> Collaboration -> Relationship.
- Don't know everything. Know where understanding lives.
- Use the least context necessary to find the next piece of understanding.
- Routing narrows the world. Understanding narrows the question.

### What This May Simplify

If validated, one composable context-and-discovery model may connect localisation, multi-country operation, organisation and venue hierarchy, staff responsibility and working-area context, information relevance, Venue Brain, Professional HQ, Cross Intelligence, DC discovery, distributed local understanding, efficient retrieval, onboarding context, Living Memory and colleague continuity.

It may also connect existing but distributed capability-routing, location-awareness, venue-profile, knowledge-applicability and provenance requirements without absorbing their responsibilities into one component.

### Tensions and Risks

- `COLLABORATION.md` currently says Digital Colleagues collaborate through Helping Hand HQ and do not communicate directly. Natural Annie-to-Harry dialogue must therefore be reconciled with, not silently bypass, HQ coordination.
- Repository identity currently means constitutional character and professional boundaries as well as platform identity. An address must not redefine or flatten those meanings.
- Existing `venueId`, `siteId`, organisation, region and Digital Colleague identity shapes are not one settled schema.
- Discoverability can expose colleague presence, locality, organisation or responsibility; privacy and disclosure policy must precede any directory or lookup design.
- Proximity does not establish freshness, truth, provenance, trust or shareability of local knowledge.
- Responsibility and working area may improve relevance but must not become implicit role-based Authority.
- Localised expression must not create incompatible meanings across countries, address systems or organisational hierarchies.
- Remembered DC relationships must not become inferred trust, relationship scoring, unrestricted context transfer or a bypass around consent and forgetting.

### Settled Principle Versus Candidate Design

**Settled or already preserved:** Context determines relevance; identity does not grant Authority; professional capability is layered; local understanding remains local unless governed to travel; provenance and freshness matter; Memory is selective; relationship depth does not expand Authority; collaboration preserves context and uses introductions and handover.

**Candidate and unresolved:** A complete structured routing identity; its dimensions and ownership; contextual DC discovery; minimum-context query formation; composition of professional and contextual routing; directory topology; HQ mediation; introduction protocol; DC-to-DC relationship Memory; country-specific expression; and any identifier syntax.

### Genuine Gaps Exposed

The review found no single canonical contract for:

1. a Digital Colleague's machine-facing routing identity across profession, organisation, venue, locality and endpoint dimensions;
2. a minimum-context discovery query independent of the complete identity;
3. contextual routing to a locally situated colleague where profession is not the subject of the question;
4. composing contextual routing with existing professional handover;
5. privacy-preserving colleague discoverability and disclosure;
6. governed remembrance of prior DC-to-DC collaboration;
7. reconciling the repository's existing venue, site, organisation, region and colleague identifiers;
8. localising expression while preserving common meaning across countries and organisational structures.

These are candidate architecture questions, not evidence that a new subsystem is required.

### Recommended Destination

Keep this understanding in `PREVIOUSLY_DISCUSSED.md` for now. Existing architecture already owns the component responsibilities, while their discovery relationship remains unresolved. No architecture document should cross-reference this candidate until a governed ownership review determines whether the integration belongs in Helping Hand HQ coordination, Companion Intelligence navigation, COS universal mechanism, or an existing Context architecture.

The next smallest governed question is:

> What is the minimum profession-neutral discovery contract needed to select a relevant Digital Colleague using independently useful context dimensions while preserving HQ coordination, privacy, provenance and separately governed Authority?

No routing implementation, identifier schema, database, API, migration, onboarding change, Authority change or runtime behaviour is authorised by this entry.

## PD-021 - Digital Colleague Identity and Authority Separation

**Topic:** Refinement of the existing identity and routing principle: identity establishes routing, context and responsibility boundaries, but does not grant Authority.

**Status:** Previously discussed - refinement of PD-020, not a new architecture decision or implementation authority.

### Principle

A Digital Colleague identity exists to establish routing, context and responsibility boundaries.

Identity does not grant Authority.

Authority must be separately established through governed context, purpose, Evidence and permission checks.

Routing determines who should consider a request.

Governance determines whether action may occur.

```text
IDENTITY_ESTABLISHES_ROUTING_CONTEXT_AND_RESPONSIBILITY_BOUNDARIES=true
IDENTITY_GRANTS_AUTHORITY=false
AUTHORITY_REQUIRES_GOVERNED_CONTEXT_PURPOSE_EVIDENCE_AND_PERMISSION_CHECKS=true
ROUTING_DETERMINES_WHO_SHOULD_CONSIDER=true
GOVERNANCE_DETERMINES_WHETHER_ACTION_MAY_OCCUR=true
```

### Relationship to Existing Understanding

PD-021 refines the existing PD-020 understanding that identity does not grant Authority and that professional and contextual routing are distinct from governance. It does not settle an identity schema, routing protocol, directory topology, permission model, or runtime behaviour.

No new component, identifier syntax, architecture boundary, Authority, implementation, or operational behaviour is authorised by this entry.

### Candidate Observation

Digital Colleague identity establishes routing, context and responsibility.

It does not establish permission.

Authority requires separate evaluation using purpose, Evidence, governance rules and recipient eligibility.

Identity answers:

> Who/what should handle this?

Authority answers:

> Should this action happen?

## PD-022 - Digital Colleague Request, Intent and Authority Evaluation

**Topic:** Whether a Digital Colleague should treat a human request as an expression of intent requiring separate authority evaluation, rather than as automatic permission.

**Status:** Candidate Observation

### Observation

A Digital Colleague must treat a human request as an expression of intent, not automatic permission.

Before acting, a Digital Colleague should establish:

1. Who is requesting?
2. Why is the action requested?
3. What outcome is intended?
4. Does the requester have authority?
5. Is the required information accessible?
6. Is the resulting output permitted to be provided?
7. Which Digital Colleague capability or responsibility should handle the request?

The following distinctions should remain explicit:

```text
REQUEST != AUTHORITY
IDENTITY != PERMISSION
CAPABILITY != APPROVAL
```

A Digital Colleague identity provides routing, context and responsibility boundaries.

Authority is a separate evaluation layer requiring governed Evidence, purpose alignment and permission checks.

### Unknown Handling

If authority cannot be established confidently:

- preserve `UNKNOWN`;
- do not infer permission;
- do not improve the answer by assumption.

### Relationship to Existing Candidates

PD-022 extends the discussions in PD-020, **Digital Colleague Identity, Context, Routing, Discovery and Relationship**, and PD-021, **Digital Colleague Identity and Authority Separation**. It does not replace or supersede either candidate.

This is a candidate observation only. It does not create architecture, modify implementation, create new operational behaviour, or establish a settled authority model.

## PD-023 - Digital Colleague Request, Instruction, Consent, Approval and Permission Separation

**Topic:** Whether requests, instructions, consent, approval and permission require distinct governance meanings in Digital Colleague interactions.

**Status:** Candidate Observation

### Observation

The following distinctions should remain explicit:

```text
REQUEST != INSTRUCTION
REQUEST != CONSENT
CONSENT != APPROVAL
APPROVAL != PERMISSION
```

A request expresses an intended outcome or asks for consideration. It does not automatically instruct the recipient, establish the requester’s consent, approve an action, or grant permission.

Consent expresses an eligible person’s agreement where agreement is relevant. Approval is a separate human or governed-process decision. Permission is the governed allowance to access information or perform an action. These meanings may coincide in a particular context, but must not be inferred as equivalent.

### Why This Exists

PD-020 addresses identity, context, routing, discovery, collaboration and relationship. PD-021 separates identity and routing from Authority. PD-022 separates request intent from automatic Authority and permission. PD-023 records the further distinction between request, instruction, consent, approval and permission, which is not fully expressed by those entries.

The problem addressed is premature promotion: treating a request as an instruction, treating consent as approval, or treating approval as permission without separately establishing the relevant governance condition.

### What This Must Not Become

This observation must not become:

- an authority architecture;
- a request or consent workflow;
- a permission model;
- an approval mechanism;
- an implementation rule;
- an assumption that every action requires the same form of consent or approval.

### Relationship to Existing Candidates

PD-023 extends PD-020, PD-021 and PD-022 without replacing, merging or superseding them. It remains exploratory and does not promote any candidate to a settled principle or architecture.
