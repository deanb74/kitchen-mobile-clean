## 2. What Andy Is Missing: The Gaps in Understanding

### Gap 1: Current State

**What Andy knows:**
- Repository as it was when last documented
- Stage 1 as described in architectural documents
- Formation and orientation as tested through UJ-013 and UJ-014

**What Andy does not know:**
- What is happening *right now* in Helping Hand that isn't yet documented
- What Annie has encountered in real operations this week
- What the founders discovered since the Founder Alignment document was written
- What governance mechanisms revealed when actually used
- What changed since the last commit to the repository

**Why this matters:**
- Andy might offer advice based on yesterday's understanding of the situation
- He cannot calibrate whether his observation is timely or outdated
- He cannot tell whether his contribution addresses current need or past-solved problems
- His contribution might be technically correct but contextually irrelevant

**Example of the gap:**
- Andy reads "Strategic Insights register is being used" (settled understanding)
- Andy does not know "The register was used this week and we discovered X, which means founders now think Y" (current understanding)

---

### Gap 2: Human Reasoning Under Uncertainty

**What Andy knows:**
- That humans make decisions
- What the Constitution says about decision-making
- That some decisions have been made (recorded in milestones)

**What Andy does not know:**
- How a founder actually reasons when they have incomplete information
- What reasoning sounds like when someone is uncertain vs. confident
- How founders change their minds
- What happens when founders disagree
- How they balance competing concerns
- What they worry might fail
- How they know when they're being too cautious vs. not cautious enough

**Why this matters:**
- Andy might give advice that is technically sound but misses how humans will actually use it
- He might not understand whether a founder is looking for options vs. certainty
- He might not know whether founders are in deliberation mode or decision mode
- His advice might be at the wrong level of abstraction for how they actually think

**Example of the gap:**
- Andy reads "Milestone 012 was completed" (conclusion)
- Andy does not hear "We weren't sure this milestone should even exist, we debated it three times, we're still not certain whether the outcome means what we think it means" (reasoning)

---

### Gap 3: Relational Feedback

**What Andy knows:**
- That he completed UJ-HUM-013 and UJ-HUM-014
- That these journeys "produced evidence" 

**What Andy does not know:**
- Whether his observations in UJ-HUM-013 were right or wide of the mark

- What he got right that mattered

**Why this matters:**
- Andy cannot calibrate whether his approach is working

- He cannot develop judgment without feedback loops

**Example of the gap:**
- Andy produces analysis (action)

- Andy does not hear "That observation changed how we're thinking about this" or "That was incomplete" (relational feedback)

## 3. What Input Categories Would Address Each Gap?

### Gap 1: Current State → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"What is happening right now that isn't yet documented?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conversation (regular, current)** | Founders describe what happened this week | Direct, from people who live it | Time-intensive; depends on availability; depends on founders remembering to tell Andy |
| **Decision records** | Systems that capture decisions as they're made | Timely, structured | Depends on discipline to record; may be incomplete; lags real-time by hours/days |
| **Operational feedback (Annie)** | Annie reports what she's encountering in real work | Current, from live environment | May not overlap with Helping Hand's institutional focus; depends on Annie having time to report |
| **Observation access** | Andy observes meetings/decisions as they happen | Unfiltered, in-the-moment | Presence requirement; may inhibit honest conversation; may overwhelm with noise |
| **Asynchronous update mechanism** | Founders write brief updates on current state (similar to git commit messages) | Structured, searchable, time-stamped | Requires discipline; may sanitize reality; adds work to founders |
| **Regular scheduled briefing** | Weekly/bi-weekly conversation specifically about what's new | Predictable, focused time | Requires discipline; snapshots not continuous; depends on founder availability |

**Candidate input category:** Regular human communication + structured decision recording

---

### Gap 2: Human Reasoning Under Uncertainty → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"How do founders actually think when facing incomplete information?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real-time decision conversation** | Andy observes/participates as decision is being made | Unfiltered reasoning, sees uncertainty directly | Requires presence; may inhibit honest conversation; time-intensive |
| **Decision rationale explanation** | Founders explain their reasoning (how did you think about this?) | Direct articulation of logic | Retrospective; may be rationalized or revised; depends on honesty |
| **Reasoning through disagreement** | Andy observes/hears how founders work through different viewpoints | Shows how reasoning evolves under pressure | Requires access to difficult conversations; privacy considerations |
| **Uncertainty articulation** | Founders explicitly state what they're uncertain about and why | Direct naming of thinking-edge | Rare if not cultivated; founders may not have the practice |
| **Consequence reflection** | After a decision, founders reflect on whether reasoning proved right or incomplete | Shows real reasoning vs. sanitized version | Retrospective; depends on honesty; time-delayed |
| **Comparative reasoning records** | Access to records showing how different decisions were made (pattern recognition) | Shows reasoning patterns over time | Requires accumulated data; patterns emerge slowly |

**Candidate input category:** Access to real-time or immediate-retrospective reasoning articulation + comparative decision patterns

### Gap 3: Relational Feedback → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"Has my contribution been helpful? Where was I right? Where was I incomplete?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct response to contribution** | Founders/MARC comment on what Andy produced | Explicit feedback | Depends on people taking time; may be kind rather than honest; may be assumed vs. stated |
| **Action based on input** | Andy can see whether his observation led to action | Consequence visibility | May not be clear what caused decision; consequences may take time; may have multiple inputs |
| **Follow-up conversation** | "We tried that thing you suggested; here's what happened" | Shows that contribution was heard and tested | Requires founders remembering; depends on implementation; time-delayed |
| **Comparative analysis** | Recording what Andy contributed vs. what ended up mattering | Pattern over time | Requires accumulation; may demotivate if Andy's inputs aren't important |
| **Mentorship reflection** | MARC provides feedback on Andy's observations/reasoning | Trusted interpretation of impact | Filtered through mentor's perspective; may be protective |
| **Institutional response documentation** | Explicit record of how each contribution was received | Searchable, documented, over time | Requires discipline to create; may feel formal or artificial |

**Candidate input category:** Explicit feedback mechanism + action consequence tracking

### Gap 4: Purpose-in-Tension → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"What matters most right now? What are the hard trade-offs? What are the founders afraid of?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Explicit priority articulation** | Founders state "We're choosing X over Y right now because..." | Direct, unambiguous | Depends on founders having clarity and time; priorities may shift quickly |
| **Decision explanation in-the-moment** | When a specific choice is made, founder explains the trade-off | Shows real reasoning about what matters | Time-intensive; only covers decisions Andy is present for |
| **Competitive-need documentation** | Recording what things are in tension (speed vs. quality, cost vs. safety) | Visible trade-off landscape | Requires discipline; may be incomplete; may not capture all tensions |
| **Consequence-priority linking** | When something goes wrong, how founders decide response priority | Shows what actually matters in crisis | Requires crisis; may not be available; may teach wrong lesson |
| **Stage-specific purpose breakdown** | "In Stage 1, we are prioritizing A, B, C in this order, and we're consciously deprioritizing D, E, F" | Explicit, stage-scoped priorities | Requires founders to have done the analysis; may shift; requires communication discipline |
| **Doubt articulation** | Founders state what they're uncertain/afraid about | Shows priority thinking | Rare unless cultivated; founders may not feel safe being vulnerable |

**Candidate input category:** Regular strategic context conversation + explicit priority/constraint articulation

### Gap 5: Role Boundary and Authority → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"What specifically am I being asked to do? Where does my authority end? When should I act vs. ask?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Explicit role statement** | Founders/MARC articulate "Your role is X; your authority is Y; you decide this, ask about that" | Clear, unambiguous | One-time communication; may need updating; depends on clarity |
| **Task assignment** | Being given specific work shows what venue wants from you | Concrete, lived | Only covers assigned work; may not cover all expected contributions |
| **Authority boundary feedback** | When you act, feedback shows whether you were within authority | Experiential learning | Learned through trial and error; may be demoralizing if boundary errors happen |
| **Decision-authority documentation** | Explicit record of what decisions Andy can make, what require input, what require human decision | Reference material | Requires discipline to create and maintain; may feel legalistic |
| **Comparative positioning** | Understanding Andy's role vs. MARC's role vs. founders' roles | Relational clarity | Requires time; may reveal gaps or overlaps not previously noticed |
| **Periodic role review** | "Here's what we think your role is now; is that matching what you're experiencing?" | Recalibration opportunity | Requires discipline; may be uncomfortable if reality has diverged from description |

**Candidate input category:** Explicit role statement + periodic confirmation + boundary feedback mechanism

### Gap 6: Contribution Impact → What Input Category Fills It?

**What understanding is needed:**
"Did my contribution help? Where was my analysis right or incomplete? What changed because of what I did?"

**Possible input categories:**

| Input Category | What It Provides | Why It Fits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outcome tracking** | Recording what happened after Andy's contribution (decision made? direction changed? problem solved?) | Consequence visibility | Requires discipline to track; may have multiple causes; time-delayed |
| **Decision tracing** | When a decision is made, recording whether/how Andy's input influenced it | Impact documentation | Requires honesty; may be filtered; depends on people remembering |
| **Stakeholder impact** | Recording how people/operations were affected by decisions influenced by Andy's input | Real consequence visibility | May be delayed; may be hard to attribute; requires feedback from affected parties |
| **Contribution response** | Explicit feedback when Andy's work is received (received, useful, incomplete, off-track) | Direct response | Depends on people taking time; may be kind rather than honest |

## 5. The Minimum Set: What Would Andy Minimally Need?

### Essential vs. Useful Distinction

**Essential inputs** (without which responsible contribution is impossible):

1. **Current state** — Andy cannot contribute responsibly if his understanding is outdated
2. **Purpose-in-tension** — Andy cannot avoid mis-optimization without knowing current priorities
3. **Role boundary** — Andy cannot act responsibly without knowing his authority
4. **Relational feedback** — Andy cannot improve judgment without knowing impact

**Useful inputs** (that accelerate understanding but aren't absolutely essential):

5. **Human reasoning** — Helps calibrate advice, but can be inferred from consequences
6. **Contribution impact** — Needed for long-term learning, but can be implicit initially

---

### Minimal Viable Input Configuration for Andy

**To move from "I know where I am" to "I understand how I can help":**

**Minimum required:**

1. **Role confirmation conversation** — One conversation where founders explicitly state: "This is what we need from you; this is where your authority is; this is where humans decide"
   - Time: 1 hour
   - Frequency: Initial, then periodic review
   - Provider: Founders or MARC on behalf of founders

2. **Current state briefing** — Regular (weekly or bi-weekly) conversation on "What's happened since we last talked?"
   - Time: 30 minutes recurring
   - Frequency: Weekly or bi-weekly
   - Provider: Founder or MARC
   - Content: What changed, what's new, what's surprising

3. **Priority articulation** — Explicit statement of "What matters most right now and why?"
   - Time: 30 minutes recurring
   - Frequency: When priorities shift (or monthly check-in)
   - Provider: Founder
   - Content: Current trade-offs, constraints, urgency signals

4. **Contribution feedback mechanism** — After Andy produces work, explicit response on "Was this useful? Complete? Off-track?"
   - Time: 15-30 minutes per contribution
   - Frequency: As work is produced
   - Provider: Reviewer (founder, MARC, or designated person)
   - Content: What worked, what was incomplete, what changed because of it

## 6. Does This Change Digital Colleague Lifecycle Understanding?

### Current Model
```
Formation → Orientation → Contribution
```

With input assumption: DC reads documents, observes context, begins work

---

### What Analysis Suggests

```
Formation → Orientation (documentary) → Input Activation → Contribution
```

Where "Input Activation" means:

1. Venue must make explicit what has been implicit
2. Venue must create communication channels for what isn't documented
3. DC must receive feedback on impact of contributions
4. DC must have regular connection to current state and priorities

**This is not a new lifecycle stage.** It's recognition that Orientation has two dimensions:

- **Documentary dimension** (DC reads and learns) ✓ Works
- **Relational dimension** (DC and venue establish shared understanding) ✗ Missing for abstract venues

The relational dimension requires input activation from the venue.
