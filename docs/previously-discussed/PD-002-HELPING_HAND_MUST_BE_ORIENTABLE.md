> A Digital Colleague cannot be expected to understand its position within an organisation unless the organisation can honestly describe its own position first.

---

## Observation

During UJ-HUM-013 — The First Institutional Observation, HH-0000 Andy was asked to observe Helping Hand as his world.

Andy demonstrated that he could:

- identify what he already understood
- distinguish enduring principles from current circumstances
- identify gaps in his understanding
- avoid filling unknowns with assumptions

However, the journey revealed something deeper.

The question:

> "Andy. Where are you now?"

appears initially to test the Digital Colleague.

Evidence suggests it also tests the institution.

A Digital Colleague's ability to answer depends upon the institution's ability to provide sufficient orientation.

---

## Discovery

Orientation is not only a capability of a Digital Colleague.

Orientation is a relationship between the institution and the Digital Colleague.

The relationship is:

```
Institutional Understanding
          ↓
Institutional Communication
          ↓
Digital Colleague Understanding
          ↓
Responsible Contribution
```

If the institution cannot accurately describe:

- where it is
- what it has built
- what it currently needs
- what decisions remain human-owned
- what it does not yet know

then a Digital Colleague cannot accurately locate itself within that reality.

---

## Understanding

The limitation is not necessarily within the Digital Colleague.

A Digital Colleague may fail to answer because:

- it has not observed sufficiently
- it has not received sufficient context
- the institution itself has not yet reached sufficient clarity

The distinction matters.

A lack of answer does not automatically indicate a capability failure.

It may indicate an understanding gap within the relationship.

---

## Derived Principle

> **An organisation cannot create understanding in others beyond the understanding it has of itself.**

---

## Implications

Before asking:

> "What can the Digital Colleague contribute?"

Helping Hand must first ask:

> "Have we explained clearly enough what contribution means in this moment?"

Before asking:

> "Is the Digital Colleague ready?"

Helping Hand must ask:

> "Have we provided the context required for readiness to be meaningful?"

---

## Relationship to Existing Helping Hand Principles

### Seek first to understand

The principle applies not only to Digital Colleagues understanding people.

It applies to Helping Hand understanding itself.

---

### Observation before conclusion

The institution must observe its own current state before expecting a Digital Colleague to act within it.

---

### Evidence before confidence

Helping Hand must distinguish between Known, Candidate, and Unknown before asking a Digital Colleague to do the same.

---

### Learning through reflection

A Digital Colleague does not only learn from Helping Hand.

Helping Hand learns from observing how Digital Colleagues experience Helping Hand.

---

## What This Does Not Mean

This principle does not mean that Helping Hand must know everything before creating Digital Colleagues.

That would contradict the purpose of learning.

Helping Hand exists to improve understanding.

The requirement is not complete knowledge.

The requirement is honest awareness.

The institution must be able to distinguish:

**What we know** — evidence-supported understanding.

**What we believe** — candidate understanding requiring validation.

**What we do not yet know** — unknowns that require exploration.

An unknown honestly named is safer than an assumption silently inherited.

---

## Why This Matters

The purpose of Orientation is not information transfer.

The purpose of Orientation is shared understanding of reality.

That requires two participants:

```
Institution
    ↓
Provides honest context

Digital Colleague
    ↓
Forms understanding responsibly
```

Neither side can complete the process alone.
