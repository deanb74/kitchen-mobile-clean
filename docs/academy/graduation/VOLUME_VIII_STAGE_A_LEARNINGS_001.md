## 1. What Stage A Demonstrated

Stage A demonstrated that a graduation-readiness question can be held within an evidence boundary before any progression decision is considered.

The process separated:

- source documentation from verification evidence;
- bounded execution evidence from complete coverage claims;
- evidence assessment from governance decision;
- confirmed foundations from unresolved readiness requirements;
- completion of a Stage A evidence boundary from authorisation to continue into Stage B.

Stage A also demonstrated that a `HOLD` state can be a complete and evidence-supported boundary outcome. It records what is known, what is not established, and what may not yet follow.

## 2. Governance Principles Emerged

### Evidence Boundaries Precede Conclusions

A conclusion must follow the evidence boundary defined for the question. Stage A assessed constitutional scope, formation completion, and validation-library operation separately before reconciling the results.

### Documentation, Verification, and Governance Are Separate Layers

Documentation records sources, designs, criteria, and paths. Verification assesses bounded evidence against a stated scope. Governance determines whether verified evidence is sufficient for an authorised conclusion. One layer must not be promoted into another by implication.

### Partial Evidence Must Remain Partial

Stage A retained `PARTIAL` for requirements where direct evidence existed but did not establish the full required scope. Bounded formation execution did not become full formation completion, and Validation Set 001 did not become complete validation coverage.

### Unknowns Are Preserved Rather Than Inferred

Missing execution, incomplete coverage, unresolved scenario conditions, and absent scope connections remained visible. The evidence chain did not fill these gaps with documentation, optimism, or structural inference.

### Integrity Checking Follows Boundary Completion

After the handover and boundary records were complete, the integrity check confirmed that referenced documents existed, links resolved, no contradiction was found, and the repository state matched the recorded boundary. Integrity checking preserved the boundary rather than reopening readiness assessment.

### Digital Colleagues Require the Ability to Stop as Well as Act

The Stage A record preserved `HOLD`, withheld Stage B authorisation, and made no graduation decision where evidence did not support them. This demonstrates that governed Digital Colleague and Academy practice includes recognising when action, progression, or conclusion must stop.

## 3. Reusable Helping Hand Practice

The following process practices are reusable where Helping Hand must distinguish evidence from conclusion:

- define the evidence boundary before assessing readiness or progression;
- separate source documentation, evidence assessment, reconciliation, governance review, handover, and integrity check;
- state what each evidence record demonstrates and what it does not demonstrate;
- preserve partial findings as partial rather than accumulating them into a stronger claim without direct support;
- retain unknowns with their source boundary and dependency;
- use handover records to preserve the current truth for future continuation;
- perform integrity checks after the boundary record is complete to confirm references, links, consistency, and boundary preservation;
- preserve a stop state when evidence does not justify continuation.

These are process lessons. They do not create new architecture, implementation requirements, capability definitions, or automatic governance outcomes.
