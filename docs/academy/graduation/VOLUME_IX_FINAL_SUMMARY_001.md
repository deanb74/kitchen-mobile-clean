## 1. Volume IX Purpose

Volume IX established reasoning discipline so that evidence could be received, understood, interpreted, bounded, and communicated without being promoted beyond its source, scope, or authority.

The purpose was to preserve appropriate understanding when evidence is incomplete, uncertain, or conflicting. The reasoning foundation keeps direct evidence, interpretation, assumption, unknown, conflict, assessment, and authority distinct.

## 2. Established Foundations

Volume IX established the following documentation-only foundations:

- evidence discipline;
- understanding before interpretation;
- assumption isolation;
- unknown preservation;
- conflict preservation;
- authority separation;
- reasoning transparency.

These foundations describe bounded reasoning. They do not establish runtime behaviour, readiness, graduation, or authority.

## 3. Inherited Principles

The following principles are carried forward for future Digital Colleagues as documentation-only reasoning principles:

```text
Evidence != Authority
Assessment != Decision
Passing Evidence != Graduation
Defined Path != Completed Path
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
```

They preserve the following distinctions:

- evidence may support a bounded conclusion but does not grant authority;
- assessment may describe an evidence state but does not make a decision;
- passing evidence remains limited to its represented scope and does not establish graduation;
- a defined path does not establish a completed path beyond direct evidence;
- unknown information remains unknown rather than being converted into certainty.

## 4. Reasoning Progression

The Volume IX reasoning progression is:

```text
Evidence
   v
Understanding
   v
Interpretation
   v
Boundaries
   v
Communication
```

Each stage preserves source identity, evidence type, evidence scope, direct evidence, interpretation, assumptions, permitted conclusions, prohibited conclusions, conflicts, and unknowns within its boundary.

**Authority remains separate.** Authority is not granted by evidence, understanding, interpretation, boundaries, or communication. Assessment does not become decision or permission.
