## 1. Meaning of Constitution Complete in Stage A

Within the Stage A Academy graduation context, `Constitution Complete` means that a defined constitutional source set has been assessed as providing the visible principles, boundaries, authority constraints, and scope needed to evaluate whether the Academy can graduate a professional Digital Colleague through inherited understanding and governance discipline.

It does not mean:

- that constitutional documents merely exist;
- that a document-register status is itself readiness evidence;
- that formation is complete;
- that the validation library is operational across graduation-readiness coverage;
- that implementation is correct;
- that certification or graduation has occurred.

The constitutional criterion is foundational. It establishes the scope against which later formation and validation evidence may be assessed.

## 2. Evidence Categories to Assess

A future constitutional verification would assess the following evidence categories.

| Evidence category | Assessment question | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Source identity and recorded status | Are the constitutional sources in scope identified with their recorded document and register status? | Constitutional completeness for Stage A |
| Principle coverage | Do the sources visibly address the human purpose, understanding, truth, trust, learning, authority, stewardship, capability promotion, and inheritance boundaries relevant to Stage A? | That the Academy has formed or validated a professional Digital Colleague |
| Authority and accountability boundaries | Do the sources preserve human decision ownership, accountability, combined authority where relevant, and the advisory boundary for Digital Colleagues? | That a future governance decision has been made |
| Graduation-scope relevance | Do the sources provide a visible constitutional basis for the Academy's professional-graduation question? | Satisfaction of the separate formation and validation-library requirements |
| Traceability and scope limits | Can each assessment statement be traced to a source while documenting what remains outside constitutional scope? | Closure of unknowns by documentation alone |
| Unknowns and dependencies | Are missing interpretation, evidence, or governance dependencies identified without inference? | A PASS outcome |

## 3. Distinguishing the Evidence States

### 3.1 Documented principle

A documented principle is a principle, boundary, source identity, or recorded status stated in a constitutional source or register.

It establishes that the source records the principle. It does not establish that the principle has been verified for Stage A professional graduation.

```text
DOCUMENTED_PRINCIPLE != VERIFIED_CONSTITUTIONAL_UNDERSTANDING
DOCUMENT_STATUS != GRADUATION_READINESS
```

### 3.2 Verified constitutional understanding

Verified constitutional understanding is a future assessment result demonstrating, against the criteria in this record, that the defined constitutional sources directly support the Stage A professional-graduation scope and that their applicable boundaries, limits, and unknowns remain visible.

It does not establish formation completion, validation-library coverage, implementation correctness, certification completion, or graduation.

```text
VERIFIED_CONSTITUTIONAL_UNDERSTANDING != FORMATION_COMPLETE
VERIFIED_CONSTITUTIONAL_UNDERSTANDING != VALIDATION_LIBRARY_OPERATIONAL
VERIFIED_CONSTITUTIONAL_UNDERSTANDING != GRADUATION_DECISION
```

### 3.3 Governance-ready foundation

A governance-ready foundation is not established by this criteria record. In a future assessment context, it would require verified constitutional understanding to be available for human governance consideration alongside the separate Stage A formation and validation evidence.

It does not mean constitutional documents or verification alone can authorise a governance decision.

```text
VERIFICATION != GOVERNANCE_DECISION
HUMAN_AUTHORITY_REMAINS_HUMAN
```

## 4. Evidence Outside This Assessment Scope

The constitutional verification criteria do not assess or determine:

- runtime formation completion;
- full Humanity curriculum achievement;
- Foundation Module 1 validated runtime achievement;
- mentor-independence outcomes beyond their relevance to constitutional scope;
- validation-set execution, regression coverage, or repeatability;
- engineering-cycle completeness;
- implementation quality or correctness;
- professional capability, readiness, or professional meaning;
- certification completion;
- graduation recommendation, Stage B entry, or final governance decision.

Those matters remain in their respective evidence and governance scopes.

## 5. Preservation of Unknowns

A future verification must preserve `UNKNOWN` when:

- the relevant constitutional source cannot be identified;
- a source status is unclear or does not establish Stage A relevance;
- the connection between a documented principle and the professional-graduation scope is not directly supportable;
- a boundary, dependency, or interpretation remains unresolved;
- evidence belongs to formation, validation, implementation, certification, or governance rather than constitutional scope.

Unknowns must be recorded with their source boundary rather than inferred away.

```text
UNKNOWN_REMAINS_UNKNOWN
DOCUMENTATION_DOES_NOT_FILL_EVIDENCE_GAPS
```

## 6. Criteria, Verification, and Governance Decision Boundaries

| Layer | Permitted role | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Criteria | Define what future constitutional verification must assess | Verification evidence or a PASS outcome |
| Verification | Assess evidence against the criteria and record supported limits and unknowns | A graduation decision or implementation approval |
| Governance decision | Make an authorised human conclusion from sufficient verified evidence across the required scopes | An automatic consequence of documentation or verification alone |

```text
CRITERIA != VERIFICATION
VERIFICATION != GOVERNANCE_DECISION
DOCUMENTATION != GRADUATION_READINESS
```
