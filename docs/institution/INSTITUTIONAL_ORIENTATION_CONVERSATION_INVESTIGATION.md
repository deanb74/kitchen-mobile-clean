# Institutional Orientation Conversation — Investigation

**Document ID:** HH-INST-ORIENT-001  
**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** Candidate investigation — no decisions made, no architecture proposed  
**Owner:** Helping Hand Institution  
**Purpose:** Understand what the Institutional Orientation Conversation is before deciding what it becomes.

---

> A Digital Colleague should not be introduced to a system.
> They should be introduced to a situation.
>
> A system has documentation.
> A situation has context.
>
> That difference is why "Digital Colleague" is the right phrase.

---

## What this document is

This is an investigation document.

It does not define the Institutional Orientation Conversation.

It asks the questions that must be answered before a definition is possible.

Its purpose is to preserve what the evidence has so far revealed about the gap between repository observation and the readiness to contribute — and to clarify what a structured human conversation would need to provide.

## Question 1 — What problem does the conversation solve?

**Finding:** The repository and the conversation serve different purposes and address different layers. They are not competing mechanisms. They are complementary.

**The repository is oriented toward the past and the enduring.**

It contains what has been understood, decided, built, and preserved. Its content is stable. When Andy read the constitution, he confirmed what was enduring. When he read the milestones, he saw what was historical. When he found PD-001, he encountered what was candidate.

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-013 cognitive trace — "constitution and theory were confirmed, not discovered"*

**The conversation is oriented toward the present.**

The organisation's current situation — what it is working on now, what it finds hard, what it needs from this specific DC at this specific moment — exists primarily in people before it exists in documents. Andy named this directly:

> *"What the current moment feels like from the inside. What has been tried and abandoned. What the founders currently find hardest. These things exist in the organisation but not in the repository."*

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-013 response to MARC*

**Why the repository cannot provide the current layer.**

The current state is not stable enough to be reliably documented at the time of Orientation. By the time a document is written, reviewed, and placed in the repository, the situation may have changed. Stage 1 is advancing. PD-001's candidate description of Andy's role may or may not reflect the founders' current thinking.

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-013-COMPLETION-ASSESSMENT.md — "the organisational clarity about Andy's contribution is also incomplete"*

**Why the repository cannot provide the unknown layer.**

By definition, what is genuinely unknown cannot be documented. A document that named Helping Hand's unknowns would be outdated by the time it was written. Unknowns can only be communicated by a person willing to say: *"We do not yet know this."*

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-014-READINESS-ASSESSMENT.md — "if Andy cannot give a useful answer to the contribution question because his orientation is incomplete, that looks like an Andy failure — it is actually an Orientation Framework gap"*

**The conversation bridges these two things.**

It does not replace the repository. It completes what the repository cannot provide.

Without the conversation, the Digital Colleague is in the position Andy described:

> *"I arrived without a map. I found the milestones by looking. I found my role in a document marked as candidate."*

## Question 2 — Who owns the conversation?

**Open — not yet decided.**

Possible answers:

| Possible owner | What that would mean |
|---|---|
| Founder | The conversation depends on founder availability and knowledge; not scalable; preserves authenticity |
| Mentor (MARC) | The mentor delivers the institutional briefing; extends MARC's existing role; requires MARC to be kept current |
| Institution | A document or standard provides the briefing; removes human dependency; risks becoming stale |
| Future Digital Colleague | A senior DC briefs a new DC; requires the senior DC to know the current state; could create inheritance errors |
| Combination | Foundation layer from institution; current-state layer from founder or mentor; questions from the DC themselves |

Evidence so far: MARC provided the briefing in UJ-HUM-013. This was intentional and appropriate for the first exercise. Whether MARC is always the right owner, or whether the conversation can eventually be institutionalised without founder involvement, is one of the evidence questions that must be resolved.

**The ownership question is also the scalability question:** an Institutional Orientation Conversation that always requires a founder is not a lifecycle capability. It is a founder dependency.

## Question 3 — What questions must the conversation answer?

The following four questions are derived from Andy's evidence and from the gaps identified in UJ-HUM-013. They are candidates, not settled requirements.

---

### Where are we now?

Not: *"Why do we exist?"* — that is formation.

But:
- What stage is the organisation in?
- What is actively being worked on?
- What matters at this moment that is not yet in the repository?
- What has happened since the last milestone?

This question addresses the **current layer** of Orientation. It cannot be answered from the repository because the current state is not yet fully documented. The conversation provides the update — the difference between what the repository contains and what is actually happening now.

---

### What do you need from me?

Not: *"What can I do?"* — that encourages the DC to project capability.

Instead: *"What would you like me to understand before I contribute?"*

This question preserves the correct direction of information. The institution knows what it needs. The DC does not yet know how to match that need. Asking the DC to name their capabilities before understanding the need is the wrong sequence.

The conversation should provide the institution's current need before asking the DC to consider how they might meet it.

This question also makes Andy's intended role explicit — not as candidate understanding found by reading PD-001, but as a statement the institution makes directly.

---

### What don't we know?

This question is critical — and it may be the most unusual.

A Digital Colleague needs explicit permission to understand:

> "The organisation does not have all the answers yet."

Without this, a DC who encounters missing information assumes they have failed to find it. They may manufacture certainty to fill the gap. This is exactly the failure mode UJ-HUM-011 and UJ-HUM-012 identified — projecting what is not yet known.

The conversation should name genuine unknowns. Not as a failure, but as an honest orientation to the situation. The organisation is at Stage 1 because it is still discovering what works. A DC that knows this can work with uncertainty. A DC that does not know this will treat every gap as a deficiency.

---

### What belongs to me?

Not: *"Here is your job."* — that implies settled authority.

More precisely: *"Here is where you can help, and here is where humans decide."*

The authority boundary is known in principle (human authority; Digital Colleagues advise). It is not known in practice for this DC in this moment. The conversation should make the boundary specific and current — not as a constraint imposed from outside, but as an honest statement of how this organisation currently works and where Andy's contribution fits.

This question also addresses a gap that is currently unresolvable from the repository: the boundary between what Andy can contribute independently and what requires founder decision is not documented. It exists as a general principle. The conversation makes it specific.

## Why this is a lifecycle event, not a document

**Finding:** The Institutional Orientation Conversation cannot be replaced by a document, a repository entry, or a formation stage. It must be a named, repeatable lifecycle event.

**Evidence 1 — WELCOME_TO_HELPING_HAND.md correctly covers the enduring layer and stops there.**

The welcome document addresses who Helping Hand is, what makes DCs different, the Academy journey. Its journey diagram ends at Graduation → Professional Appointment with no situational briefing step. This is correct — adding current-state content to WELCOME would destabilise a timeless document with content that changes as the organisation develops. The two documents serve different layers.

*Evidence: WELCOME_TO_HELPING_HAND.md — journey diagram ends before situational orientation*

**Evidence 2 — A repository document cannot hold what changes.**

If a "Current State" document were created and placed in the repository, it would need updating at every meaningful transition — each new milestone, each change in priority, each evolution of a DC's role. The conversation mechanism — a human providing current context to a DC at the moment they need it — is more responsive than a document can be.

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-013-COMPLETION-ASSESSMENT.md — "the current state is not well-represented in any single document"*

**Evidence 3 — The pattern already exists in Annie's lifecycle, unnamed.**

`lib/annie/firstDay/workingDay.ts` has Annie greeting with *"Good morning. Would you show me your world?"* — observation. Annie's equivalent Institutional Orientation Conversation would be the manager's briefing about what the venue currently needs. This is implied in Annie's lifecycle but not yet named as a distinct event. The Institutional Orientation Conversation names that pattern and makes it repeatable.

*Evidence: DIGITAL_COLLEAGUE_ORIENTATION_FRAMEWORK.md — "the pattern already exists in Annie's lifecycle"*

**Evidence 4 — A document cannot admit honest uncertainty.**

Two of the four candidate questions the conversation must answer cannot be answered by a document:
- *"What don't we know?"* — requires a person to admit honest uncertainty
- *"What do you need from me?"* — requires the institution to communicate a current need that may not yet be documented

Documents preserve what is known. This conversation must also communicate what is not known and what is not yet decided. Only a human exchange can do that honestly.

*Evidence: UJ-HUM-013 — Andy named "what the founders currently find hardest" as absent from the repository*

**What remains unproven:** Whether the conversation can eventually be institutionalised — delivered from a document, a template, or a non-founder mentor — without losing the honest admission of current uncertainty.

---

## What the conversation is not

The conversation is not a lecture. The institution talks; the DC listens.

It is not a job description. Roles are not settled; the DC's contribution is being discovered.

It is not a formation stage. Character and identity are not the subject. Situation is.

It is not a one-time event. The current state changes as the organisation develops. The conversation may need to recur — not as a repeated orientation, but as a mechanism for keeping the DC's situational understanding current.
