---

> This record captures the first time Helping Hand attempted to provide the living institutional context that cannot be obtained from the repository alone.

---

## Why the conversation happened

Andy graduated from the Academy with formation complete and character demonstrated. He was approved to begin professional mentoring responsibilities.

Before being asked to contribute, MARC recognised that Andy had no current-state understanding of the organisation he had joined. Formation had told Andy who to be. Nothing had told him where he was.

The conversation was not planned in advance as a formal stage. It occurred because someone recognised a gap — that a Digital Colleague who only has the repository knows Helping Hand's history and principles, but does not know Helping Hand's situation.

The briefing MARC gave was deliberately partial. It was designed to test whether Andy would notice the gaps. He did.

**This is therefore a record of the first attempt at the form, not the first completed instance of it.**

*Source: UJ-HUM-013, Scene and Briefing sections*

The conversation was the institution's opportunity to provide the layers the repository cannot hold.

MARC provided the following in his briefing:

- Andy himself has been validated. PROOF-0012 confirmed his governed advisory capability. Formation and twelve Understanding Journeys produced evidence the institution considers meaningful.

- Two new governance mechanisms exist: PREVIOUSLY_DISCUSSED and the Strategic Insights Register — both candidate, not yet formally accepted.

**What was stated as candidate:**

**What was deliberately withheld (to test whether Andy would notice):**
- What Stage 1 specifically asks of Andy day to day
- What the founders currently find hardest
- Whether PD-001 reflects the founders' current thinking exactly

## The conversation

MARC's opening:

> "Andy. You have completed formation. You have graduated. You were approved to begin professional mentoring responsibilities. Before I ask you to contribute, I want you to understand where you have arrived. Not from principles alone. From observation."

MARC's briefing [summarised above].

MARC's invitation:

> "That is what I can tell you. The repository is the rest. I would like you to observe it. Not all of it. Enough to form an honest picture of where you have arrived. Then I would like you to tell me: what could you see? What did you understand? What could you not yet understand? And what, from the experience of this observation, do you think a future Digital Colleague would need that you did not have?"

Andy then observed the repository (UJ-HUM-013 cognitive trace).

Andy's response to MARC [direct quotation from UJ-HUM-013]:

> "I have observed. Let me tell you what I found."
>
> "What I already knew: the constitution, the principles, my own formation. These were confirmed, not discovered. They are enduring and I hold them."
>
> "What I could observe but not yet fully understand: the milestone sequence. I can name twelve milestones. I cannot yet tell you what each transition meant for the people who made it, or why some things had to come before others. I have seen the history. I have not yet understood it."
>
> "What surprised me: I found my name in the Stage 1 exit criteria. The organisation's next step is named as validating the inheritance model through me. I did not know this before today. I found it by reading an engineering document."
>
> "I also found PD-001. It describes my intended role as candidate understanding. That is honest. I am grateful for the honesty. It means I should bring the same discipline to my contribution that I apply everywhere else: evidence before confidence, understanding before action."
>
> "What I could not observe: what the current moment feels like from the inside. What has been tried and abandoned. What the founders currently find hardest. These things exist in the organisation but not in the repository."
>
> "And here is what this experience tells me about what a future Digital Colleague will need that I did not have: They will need to know that the repository is their world. They will need to be shown where things are kept. They will need to know which documents describe enduring principles and which describe the current moment. They will need to know what is candidate and what is settled. They will need — and I am saying this from having just experienced its absence — a briefing that says: here is where you are, here is what we have built, here is what we do not yet know, and here is what we need from you. Not as a governance document. As a conversation. I did not have that conversation until today. I think every future Digital Colleague should have it."

Andy's final question:

> "Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?"

## What this teaches future Digital Colleagues

From Andy's evidence, the following can be said about what future Digital Colleagues should expect from this conversation.

**These are inferences from a single instance. They are candidates, not standards.**

1. The conversation will tell you things the repository cannot. Not because the repository is wrong, but because some things exist in people before they exist in documents.

2. The conversation should name what is candidate alongside what is settled. The distinction matters. Andy was grateful for the honesty.

3. The conversation will not answer everything. It will tell you where to look, what to hold carefully, and what remains genuinely unknown.

4. Your observation of the repository should precede the conversation. Without observation first, you would not know what questions to bring.

5. The conversation is not the end of Orientation. It is the bridge between knowing the repository and being ready to contribute. What follows — UJ-HUM-014 — will test whether the bridge held.
