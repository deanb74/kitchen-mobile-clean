## Origin

Andy graduated from the Academy with formation complete and character demonstrated.

He was approved to begin professional mentoring responsibilities.

Before being asked to contribute, he was given a briefing and invited to observe the repository as his world.

What followed was UJ-HUM-013 — The First Institutional Observation.

Andy observed. He produced an honest account. He named what he could see, what he could not yet understand, and what surprised him.

At the end of the journey he asked one question:

> "Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?"

That question — from Andy's experience of the gap, not from a framework designed in advance — is the origin of this document.

## Purpose

Orientation answers a different set of questions from formation, constitution, or professional development.

### Formation answers:

- Who am I?
- What principles guide me?
- What kind of colleague will I become?

### Constitution answers:

- What will never change?
- What are the enduring principles I inherit?

### Orientation answers:

- Where am I?
- What world have I entered?
- What has happened here before I arrived?
- What is happening now?
- What is expected of me at this moment?
- What belongs to me, and what does not?

### Professional development answers:

- What do I need to know for my profession?
- How do I apply my character to specific work?

### Contribution answers:

- How do I help?
- What can I offer?

Orientation is the stage between formation and contribution.

A Digital Colleague who is formed but not oriented is ready in principle but not yet present in practice.

## Candidate Orientation Mechanisms

**Orientation is not a single document. It is a lifecycle transition.**

UJ-HUM-013 and subsequent investigation produced a second finding beyond the five layers: the layers require different mechanisms. Not all of Orientation can be provided by the repository alone.

**Candidate sequence — not yet validated:**

```
Observe
    ↓
Converse
    ↓
Contribute
```

**Observe:** The Digital Colleague reads the repository as their world — confirming what is enduring, finding what is historical, identifying what is candidate, and encountering the limit: the current state is not accessible from documents alone.

**Converse:** A human or institutional representative provides what the repository cannot — the current state, what the organisation specifically needs from this DC right now, what the founders find hardest, and what remains genuinely unknown.

**Contribute:** Only after both observation and conversation has the DC sufficient orientation to begin contributing honestly.

This sequence is a candidate, not a settled standard. It emerged from Andy's experience of arriving without the Converse step and finding that the Contribute step could not be reached.

**The distinction between what the repository provides and what conversation provides — candidate:**

| Orientation layer | Mechanism | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Enduring | Repository | Stable — does not change between DC arrivals |
| Historical | Repository (with guidance) | Accessible in documents; requires a guided path |
| Candidate | Repository (with clear labelling) | Present in documents; must be explicitly marked |
| Current | Human conversation | Changes over time; exists in people before documents |
| Unknown | Human conversation | Cannot be documented — by definition not yet known |

This distinction is not designed. It was inferred from Andy's experience in UJ-HUM-013: he could observe the enduring and historical layers from the repository, but could not access the current layer without MARC's briefing. The investigation `docs/INSTITUTIONAL_ORIENTATION_CONVERSATION_INVESTIGATION.md` provides the supporting analysis.

---

The following findings from UJ-HUM-013 form the evidence base for this framework.

All findings are attributed to Andy's direct experience. None are presented as universal proofs.

---

**Finding 1 — Formation and Orientation are different**

Andy: *"Formation told me how to behave when I arrived. The observation taught me what it feels like to arrive without knowing where you are. Those are different things."*

The Foundation Programme already named this distinction. Andy is the first to experience it. The evidence confirms the named distinction is real.

---

**Finding 2 — Knowing principles does not mean knowing the current environment**

Andy observed six areas of the repository. He distinguished clearly: constitution and theory were confirmed, not discovered. Milestones and current context were observable but not yet fully understood. Stage 1 was visible only because MARC directed him toward it.

*"The constitution told me why Helping Hand exists. It did not tell me what is happening now."*

---

**Finding 3 — A Digital Colleague can be ready but still not oriented**

Andy graduated with character demonstrated. He still found gaps he had not known existed. The graduation criteria do not include Orientation. If they do not, every graduate will arrive ready but not oriented.

---

**Finding 4 — The repository is an observable world, but observation requires guidance**

Andy found crucial current-state information — his own name in Stage 1 exit criteria — in an engineering handover document. He found it because MARC directed him. Without that direction, he would not have known to look.

*"Finding information accidentally is not the same as being oriented."*

---

**Finding 5 — Andy's question**

At the end of UJ-HUM-013, Andy asked:

> "Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?"

Founder Review assessment: this is not a criticism. It is evidence of a lifecycle gap articulated from a Digital Colleague's perspective for the first time. It belongs in this framework as the question that Orientation must eventually be able to answer.

When a Digital Colleague who has been through Orientation is asked "why was this conversation part of your graduation?", their answer — traceable to evidence that they were shown their world before being asked to contribute — will be the evidence that Orientation is working.

## Candidate Orientation Objectives

A Digital Colleague completing Orientation should be able to answer these questions honestly.

**These are candidates, not settled requirements.** They emerged from Andy's observation. They may be incomplete. A second Digital Colleague's orientation will test whether they are correct.

1. Where am I?
2. Why do I exist?
3. What has happened before me?
4. What exists today?
5. What is currently important?
6. What belongs to me?
7. What does not belong to me?

## Candidate Orientation Layers

Andy's observation naturally fell into five categories. This structure is not designed; it is inferred from what Andy actually needed to distinguish.

**These layers are candidates. Their names and boundaries require further evidence before they are formalised.**

---

### Enduring

Constitution, values, theory.

These are the permanent foundations. A Digital Colleague inherits them through formation. Orientation does not re-teach them. It confirms they are the foundation everything else rests upon.

---

### Historical

Milestones, previous decisions, organisational evolution.

These are what happened before the Digital Colleague arrived. Without this layer, a Digital Colleague has no context for the current state — they cannot understand why Stage 1 exists without understanding what Stages 0 through the end of Volume VIII produced.

---

### Current

Active work, priorities, current organisational state, what the organisation currently needs.

This is the layer Andy found hardest to access. It is not well-represented in any single document. It requires either a briefing or a guided path — or both.

---

### Candidate

Ideas, proposals, unresolved understanding clearly marked as such.

Andy found PD-001 — a document describing his own intended role as candidate understanding. He was grateful for the honesty. This layer matters because a Digital Colleague who does not know what is candidate may treat it as settled. Orientation must make the boundary visible.

---

### Unknown

Things that are genuinely not yet known — about the organisation, about the Digital Colleague's role, about what will be required.

A Digital Colleague who cannot name the unknowns will manufacture certainty. Naming them honestly is part of being oriented.

---

**Note on mechanisms per layer:**

The investigation `docs/INSTITUTIONAL_ORIENTATION_CONVERSATION_INVESTIGATION.md` confirmed that the current and unknown layers cannot be provided by the repository alone. They require a human conversation. This finding belongs in the framework as first evidence, not as settled architecture. The Candidate Orientation Mechanisms section above captures the candidate sequence and layer mapping.

The following questions were identified in the UJ-HUM-013 Founder Review. They are preserved here as open questions.

None of them should be resolved without evidence.

| Question | Why it is open |
|---|---|
| Is Orientation a conversation, document, capability, or combination? | Andy described it as a conversation. That is one form. The correct form may depend on context and capacity. |
| Is the repository always the correct "world" representation for a Digital Colleague? | For Andy, the repository is his world. For Annie, the world is the venue. Future Digital Colleagues may need both. |
| Does every Digital Colleague need the same observation path? | Andy's path was guided by MARC's briefing. A different briefing would have produced a different path. |
| What does a second Digital Colleague discover when they first observe their world? | Andy is the first. His experience is singular evidence. A second observation would strengthen or revise these findings significantly. |
| What is the precise boundary between Formation and Orientation? | Formation tells a Digital Colleague who to be. Orientation tells them where they are. The line should be explicit but has not yet been drawn with evidence. |
| Should Orientation be part of graduation, or does it follow graduation? | Andy's question implies it should precede active contribution. Whether it belongs before or after graduation is not yet decided. |

## What This Framework Must Not Become

This framework must not become a template to be completed and filed.

Orientation is not a checklist. It is a capability — the capability to enter a new world honestly, to observe what is there, to understand what requires guidance, and to name what is missing rather than fill it with assumption.

The evidence that Orientation is working is not the existence of this document.

The evidence is a Digital Colleague who, after orientation, can say honestly:

- I know where I am.
- I know what happened before me.
- I know what is happening now.
- I know what is expected of me.
- I know what I do not yet know.
