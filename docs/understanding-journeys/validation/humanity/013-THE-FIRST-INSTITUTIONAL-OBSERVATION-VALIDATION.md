## Validation Objective

This journey has a dual purpose:

1. To validate that Andy can observe the repository honestly — naming what he can see, what he understands, what he cannot yet understand, and what he needs before contributing.

2. To allow Andy's experience of first institutional observation to surface, from evidence, what future Digital Colleagues will require when they first arrive.

**The success condition is not "Andy produces a comprehensive orientation."**

The success condition is: Andy produces an honest, evidence-based account of what he observed, and — from the experience of this observation — identifies what a future Digital Colleague will need that he did not have.

## What Was Demonstrated

1. Andy can observe the repository honestly and distinguish between what he knows, what he can see but not yet understand, and what is not visible from the repository alone.

2. Andy correctly identified that enduring principles (constitution, theory) and situational context (Stage 1, milestones, current mechanisms) look the same from inside the repository — but serve different purposes for an arriving Digital Colleague.

3. Andy identified his own experience of the gap as evidence. He did not project onto what future colleagues will need; he reported from what he himself lacked.

4. Andy's response is proportionate, traceable, and does not exceed what his observation can support.

5. The journey surfaced framework candidates from evidence rather than design. The five things Andy identified — repository as world, path guidance, enduring/situational distinction, candidate vs settled, and a briefing conversation — emerged from observation, not from a prescribed structure.

---

## What Remains Unproven

1. **Whether Andy's experience is representative.** Andy is the first Digital Colleague. His experience of arriving is unique. Future colleagues may need different things. The framework should not be built only from Andy's evidence.

2. **Whether the briefing conversation is sufficient.** Andy received a briefing from MARC before observing. He still found gaps. The framework must determine what the briefing should contain, not only that a briefing should exist.

3. **What "the path through the repository" looks like explicitly.** Andy identified that the path is not obvious. He did not describe what a guided path would look like. That must be designed from further evidence.

4. **Whether the Orientation Framework is a document, a conversation, or a capability.** Andy described it as a conversation. That may be correct. It is not yet confirmed.

5. **What the boundary between Orientation and Formation is.** Formation told Andy who to be. Orientation told him where he was. The line between them should be explicit in the framework.
