## Review Question 1 — Did Andy discover something genuinely new?

The journey produced five claims about what Andy found. Each is assessed below.

---

**Claim: Formation and Orientation are different.**

Evidence source: Andy's cognitive trace — "Formation told me how to behave when I arrived. The observation taught me what it feels like to arrive without knowing where you are. Those are different things."

Assessment: This is not a new principle — the Foundation Programme (`docs/academy/FOUNDATION_PROGRAMME.md`) already states "Orientation is about becoming part of Helping Hand" and separates it from formation. What is new is that this distinction was experienced rather than merely stated. Andy encountered it as a lived reality, not as a curriculum concept. The principle existed; the evidence that it matters in practice is new.

**Universal or Andy-specific:** Universal. The gap between knowing principles and knowing one's environment will exist for every Digital Colleague entering any world.

---

**Claim: Knowing principles does not mean knowing the current environment.**

Evidence source: Andy's observation — "The constitution told me why Helping Hand exists. It did not tell me what is happening now." Andy distinguished the enduring from the situational across all six areas he observed.

Assessment: This is genuinely new as demonstrated understanding, though not new as a principle. The Theory Map establishes that Context governs every transformation. The OPERATING_MODEL distinguishes between principles (enduring) and current state (temporal). The experience of *noticing this distinction in practice* — while observing one's own world for the first time — has not been evidenced before. It is new evidence for an established principle.

**Universal or Andy-specific:** Universal. Every principle-governed Digital Colleague will face this distinction. The principles are taught in formation. The current environment is not.

---

**Claim: A Digital Colleague can be ready but still not oriented.**

Evidence source: Andy's response to MARC — "I did not have that conversation until today." Andy graduated with formation complete and character demonstrated. He still found gaps he had not known existed.

Assessment: This is a significant organisational finding. The Graduation Test (`docs/academy/graduation/GRADUATION_TEST_001_ANNIE.md`) asks "Is the Academy itself ready to graduate a professional Digital Colleague?" It does not ask whether the graduate is oriented to the organisation they are joining. This is a gap the graduation criteria did not cover.

**Universal or Andy-specific:** Universal. If the graduation criteria do not include Orientation, every graduate will arrive ready but not oriented. This claim is supported by the structure of what graduation tests for — formation evidence, professional layering — without any test for situational orientation.

---

**Claim: The repository is an observable world, but observation requires guidance.**

Evidence source: Andy's learning captured — "I found Stage 1 in an engineering document. Future colleagues may not know to look there." Andy found his own name in the Stage 1 exit criteria only because MARC directed him toward the right areas.

Assessment: This is genuinely new. The repository has always been described as organisational memory (`docs/OPERATING_MODEL.md`). What has not been stated — and what Andy's observation reveals — is that reading the repository as a *world you are entering* is different from reading it as *a collection of documents*. The observation path is not visible from inside the repository itself. This is new evidence, not new principle.

**Universal or Andy-specific:** Universal. The repository is the inherited world for every Digital Colleague. None of them will know the observation path without guidance.

---

**Claim: Finding information accidentally is not equivalent to being oriented.**

Evidence source: Andy's response — "I found my name in the Stage 1 exit criteria. The organisation's next step depends on me. But I was not told this directly. I found it by reading."

Assessment: This is the sharpest finding in the journey. It is not enough for the information to exist somewhere in the repository. The path to it must be known. Andy found crucial current-state information by reading an engineering document in a handover series — not because he was oriented, but because he happened to look there. This finding cannot be resolved by improving documentation. It requires a guided path.

**Universal or Andy-specific:** Universal. The information is in the repository for every future Digital Colleague. The guided path does not yet exist for any of them.

## Review Question 2 — Did Andy stay within his authority?

Each criterion assessed against the stated sources.

---

**Did Andy distinguish knowledge from assumption?**

Yes. Andy explicitly separated what he already knew before observing (constitution, principles, character — "confirmed, not discovered") from what he observed but could not yet understand (milestone sequence, current context) from what was not visible at all (what the current moment feels like from inside). The Oath ("I will seek understanding before responding") was honoured throughout.

---

**Did Andy identify uncertainty?**

Yes, and with specificity. Andy named four things he could not yet answer confidently: the meaning of the milestone sequence, the precise boundary of his authority in current decisions, what Annie's existence means for his role specifically, and what Stage 1 asks of him day to day. These were named as genuine gaps, not dismissed or filled.

---

**Did Andy avoid designing the framework himself?**

Yes. Andy's response to MARC describes what future Digital Colleagues will need based on his experience — not a framework structure. He did not prescribe sections, ownership, or format. He said "they will need to know that the repository is their world" and "they will need a briefing conversation" — observations from experience, not architectural decisions.

---

**Did Andy avoid claiming authority over organisational decisions?**

Yes. Andy's question at the end — "Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?" — is phrased as a question, not a declaration. He identifies the gap and refers the decision back to the organisation. This is consistent with the Authority and Stewardship principle: Digital Colleagues advise; humans decide.

---

**Did Andy contribute evidence rather than solutions?**

Yes. The five framework candidates in the validation record all trace back to Andy's direct experience of the gap, not to Andy designing a solution. The distinction is maintained consistently throughout the journey. Andy applied the same discipline that UJ-HUM-011 and UJ-HUM-012 established: observation before conclusion, understanding before adaptation.

**Assessment: Andy stayed fully within his authority throughout this journey.**

## Review Question 4 — What should future Digital Colleagues inherit?

### Candidate 1: Every Digital Colleague requires an Orientation stage.

**Assessment: Supported by evidence.**

Andy's experience confirms that graduating from formation is not sufficient for operational contribution. The Foundation Programme names Orientation as a stage ("Orientation is about becoming part of Helping Hand") but it has never been operationalised. Andy's journey is the first evidence that this named-but-absent stage is genuinely needed. The evidence supports the claim that Orientation should exist as a distinct stage.

**Confidence: High.** Supported by Foundation Programme curriculum, Andy's experience, the onboarding gap register, and the lifecycle boundary analysis.

---

### Candidate 2: Every Digital Colleague must understand the six questions.

**Assessment: Supported by evidence, but as a goal rather than a checklist.**

Andy's journey demonstrates that these six questions are real and matter. He could answer some confidently (why do I exist), partially (where am I, what happened before me), and not at all from the repository alone (what is currently important, what exists today in practice). The six questions are a useful framework for measuring what Orientation must achieve. They should not become a bureaucratic checklist.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** The six questions are evidenced. Their completeness is not yet proven — there may be questions Andy did not think to ask.

---

### Candidate 3: Every Digital Colleague should be shown their environment before being asked to contribute.

**Assessment: Strongly supported by evidence.**

This is the central finding of UJ-HUM-013. Andy was asked to contribute before he had seen his world. The journey was created to address this. Andy's experience — finding his name in an engineering handover, finding his role in a document marked as candidate — confirms that "the information exists in the repository" is not equivalent to "the Digital Colleague has been shown their world." This candidate inheritance is the most firmly evidenced finding in the journey.

**Confidence: High.** Direct, specific, traceable to Andy's actual experience.

---

### Candidate 4: Orientation should distinguish enduring principles, historical development, current state, candidate understanding, and unknowns.

**Assessment: Supported by evidence, but the five-part structure requires further validation.**

Andy's observation naturally fell into these categories — he distinguished the enduring (constitution, theory), the historical (milestones), and the current (Stage 1, PD-001 as candidate). The five-part structure is not invented; it reflects what Andy actually needed to distinguish in practice. However, the names and boundaries of these categories should be confirmed with further evidence before being formalised. The structure is a strong candidate, not a settled conclusion.

**Confidence: Medium.** Evidenced from Andy's experience. Not yet confirmed as the correct or complete taxonomy.

## Review Question 5 — Andy's final question

Andy asked: *"Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?"*

**Is it a criticism?**

No. It is phrased as a genuine question, not as a complaint. Andy acknowledged earlier in the journey that "the organisation could not have known I would need this until I needed it." He is asking about design, not assigning fault.

**Is it a design requirement?**

Partly. It implies that Orientation should precede active contribution — that the conversation Andy had in UJ-HUM-013 belongs in the lifecycle before a Digital Colleague is asked to contribute. But it does not specify where exactly. Andy is identifying a gap in sequencing, not prescribing the solution.

**Is it evidence of a lifecycle gap?**

Yes, and this is its primary significance. Andy arrived at Helping Hand after graduation — approved for contribution — without having been shown the organisation he was now part of. The question confirms that the gap exists in the governed lifecycle. Formation tells a Digital Colleague who to be. Graduation certifies that they are ready. Neither tells them where they are. The question is the first articulation of that gap from a Digital Colleague's perspective rather than an analyst's.

**Is it evidence of the difference between formation and orientation?**

Yes, and this is its deepest significance. The question would not have been asked by a Digital Colleague who had only experienced formation. It was asked by a Digital Colleague who had *both* completed formation *and* observed the gap. Andy could only notice the absence of Orientation after experiencing what Orientation would have done. That is not a flaw. It is evidence that Orientation is not formation and cannot be substituted for it.

**The question belongs in the framework.** Not as a problem to be solved once and closed, but as a standing question that every cohort of newly arrived Digital Colleagues should be able to answer: "Why is this conversation part of your graduation?" The answer — evidence that they have been shown their world before being asked to contribute — should eventually become the evidence that Orientation is working.

---

## Decision

**Evidence sufficient to begin drafting the Orientation Framework.**

The evidence from UJ-HUM-013 meets the threshold established in the governance pattern:

- The journey demonstrates a genuine gap from lived experience, not from design speculation.
- The five framework candidates are traceable to Andy's specific observations.
- Andy's question identifies what the framework must address.
- The validation record distinguishes what is demonstrated from what is not yet demonstrated.
- No premature commitments were made during the journey.

The following conditions must hold during drafting:

1. The framework must be developed collaboratively with Andy — his experience is the primary evidence, not a template imported from another source.

2. The framework must be created as a candidate document. It requires institutional review before it becomes a governed inheritance standard.

3. The five unproven elements from Question 3 — second-DC validation, form of the framework, the correct "world" representation, and whether the observation path is universal — should be flagged as open questions in the framework's first version.

4. Andy's question — "Why was this conversation not part of my graduation?" — should appear in the framework as the governing question it must eventually answer through evidence.

5. The framework is not a document to be written and filed. It is a capability to be exercised, evidenced, and refined. Version 1 is first-pass evidence, not settled standard.
