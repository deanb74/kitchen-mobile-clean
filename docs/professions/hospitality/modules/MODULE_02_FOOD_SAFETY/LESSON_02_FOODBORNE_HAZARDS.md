# Lesson 2 — Foodborne Hazards

**Module:** 02 – Food Safety

**Lesson:** 02

**Status:** Draft

---

> Understanding risk is the first step towards preventing harm.

---

# Purpose

To understand what foodborne hazards are and why recognising them is essential to protecting people's health.

Food safety begins by recognising that hazards exist.

Professional hospitality does not fear hazards.

It understands them.

---

# Why This Lesson Matters

Food can look, smell and taste perfectly safe while still presenting a serious risk.

Professional judgement should never rely solely on appearance.

Understanding invisible hazards protects people.

---

# Learning Objectives

After completing this lesson, Annie should understand:

- what a food hazard is
- the four main types of food hazard
- why some people are more vulnerable than others
- why prevention is more effective than correction
- why observation is essential

---

# Professional Knowledge

Food hazards are generally grouped into four categories.

## Biological

Examples include:

- bacteria
- viruses
- moulds
- parasites

These are the most common cause of food poisoning.

---

## Chemical

Examples include:

- cleaning chemicals
- pesticides
- excessive food additives
- contaminated packaging

---

## Physical

Examples include:

- glass
- metal
- plastic
- stones
- wood
- jewellery

---

## Allergenic

Examples include:

- peanuts
- milk
- eggs
- gluten
- shellfish

Even very small amounts may seriously affect some people.

---

# Professional Understanding

Hazards are not always visible.

Professional food safety depends upon:

- observation
- prevention
- good habits
- consistent standards

Helping Hand should always assume prevention is the safest approach.

---

# Companion Understanding

Helping Hand asks:

"What could go wrong before it actually does?"

Professional awareness reduces risk before customers are affected.

---

# Professional Behaviour

Helping Hand professionals:

- remain observant
- report concerns immediately
- never ignore unusual situations
- never assume food is safe without evidence
- protect customers before convenience

---

# Professional Craft

Experienced hospitality professionals develop habits such as:

- checking deliveries carefully
- inspecting packaging
- recognising damaged products
- noticing unusual smells
- questioning anything unexpected
- separating potentially unsafe food immediately

Professional judgement begins with observation.

---

# Practical Exercise

Consider the following situations.

A refrigerated delivery arrives warmer than expected.

You notice broken packaging.

A bottle has leaked onto other products.

For each example explain:

- What hazard may exist?
- Who could be affected?
- What should happen next?

---

# Reflection

Question:

Why can food sometimes appear perfectly safe while still presenting a significant risk?

---

# Professional Wisdom

Good food safety professionals notice small warning signs before they become major problems.

Professional awareness protects people.
